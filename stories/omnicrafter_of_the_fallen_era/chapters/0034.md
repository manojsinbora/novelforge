# Chapter 34: The Battle of the Coking Battery

The acoustic concussion of .50-caliber machine gun fire tore through the classification yard like rolling thunder.

*Day 5 // 1:44 PM.*

Along the northern levee overlooking the coal tipple, Company B’s armored Humvee opened up with blistering, synchronized violence. 

Sergeant Marcus Cole, locked into the armored roof turret behind the heavy spade grips of the M2 Browning, fired in disciplined five-round bursts:

*THUD-THUD-THUD-THUD-THUD!*

Half-inch armor-piercing incendiary rounds slammed into the sandbag machine-gun nest of the *Cinder Vanguard* at four hundred yards. The heavy tungsten penetrators shredded the timber revetments into flying matchsticks, detonating two crates of stored black-powder pipe bombs in a boiling orange mushroom of fire and black coal dust.

Instantly, the flagship of the raiders—Warlord Raymond Kane’s thirty-ton converted steam-dredger, *The Iron Behemoth*—reacted.

Its massive, steam-driven revolving turret groaned on its bronze ring gear, swinging the dual forty-millimeter Bofors autocannons ninety degrees to the north.

*KER-THUMP! KER-THUMP! KER-THUMP!*

High-explosive forty-millimeter shells tore across the yard at two thousand eight hundred feet per second, exploding against the granite riprap of the northern levee and throwing geysers of frozen mud fifty feet into the air.

"Diversion established," Arthur Vance muttered calmly, kneeling forty feet away in the darkness of the subterranean concrete cable trench beneath Track Two.

Beside him, Eleanor pulled her woolen hood tight against the dripping condensation of the tunnel ceiling. Her breath came in quiet, steady rhythms, but her green eyes were wide, glowing with deep, tranquil celestial gold.

"The tower is above us, Arthur," Eleanor whispered. "The signal wires are dead, but the copper is intact."

Arthur reached up with his right hand, the **Chimera Frame Mark III** responding with a muted hydraulic click. His gloved fingers gripped the rusted iron rungs of the maintenance ladder leading up into the foundation of **Interlocking Tower B**.

***

Arthur pushed open the cast-iron floor scuttle and vaulted silently onto the hardwood floor of the second-story switch room.

The room was a relic of twentieth-century rail automation: forty feet wide, surrounded by wrap-around industrial sash windows overlooking all twenty classification tracks. Dominating the center of the floor was a colossal fifty-foot **Union Switch & Signal Mechanical Interlocking Machine**—a row of forty polished brass and painted steel levers, each linked to electro-pneumatic valves in the basement.

On the floor beside the console lay the fallen tower operator, covered respectfully by an olive-drab canvas tarp. Above the central relay cabinet, a burst of marauder rifle fire had shattered the 24-volt direct-current terminal block, severing the control solenoids that released the mechanical interlocks.

Arthur stepped to the terminal cabinet, drew his wire strippers from his canvas apron, and exposed the twin copper bus wires.

"Eleanor," Arthur said, his voice flat, steady, and focused. "Twenty-four volts direct current. Establish bridge across Terminal Blocks Seven through Twelve."

Eleanor knelt before the open cabinet. She placed her bare index and middle fingers across the severed copper leads.

*HUMMMMMMM.*

A delicate, sapphire-tinted starlight current blossomed from Eleanor’s fingertips into the copper bus. 

Instantly, the brass indicator lamps on the interlocking machine flickered to life. Across the forty-foot console, thirty mechanical locking latches dropped with a synchronized, authoritative metallic click:

*CLACK-CLACK-CLACK-CLACK.*

"Interlocks released," Arthur announced. "Air pressure in primary reservoir: eighty-eight PSI."

Arthur stepped behind the lever frame. He seized Levers 14, 16, and 18—the three electro-pneumatic switch controls for Tracks Four, Five, and Six.

With the mechanical authority of an experienced locomotive engineer, Arthur hauled all three levers hard back into their reverse quadrants.

Outside in the rail yard, five hundred yards away, the electro-pneumatic switch machines fired in rapid succession.

*PSSSHT-THUNK! PSSSHT-THUNK! PSSSHT-THUNK!*

Under eighty-eight PSI of compressed air, the heavy manganese steel switch points slammed across the tie plates, severing the mainline through-routes and routing Tracks Four and Six into two dead-end, thirty-ton derail spurs backed by reinforced concrete bumper blocks.

The two flanking steam-dredgers of the *Cinder Vanguard*—moving at eight miles per hour to support the coal tipple—crossed the altered switches without realizing the trap.

Their sixty-inch cast-steel flanged wheels hit the diverging rails. Before the marauder drivers could comprehend the shift, both thirty-ton iron tortoises slammed headfirst into the dead-end bumper blocks with an earth-shattering crash of buckling iron and shearing rivets.

*BOOOOOOM!*

The impact derailed both machines instantly, their front trucks sinking two feet deep into the loose ballast, hopelessly wedged and immobilized.

Inside *The Iron Behemoth* on Track Five, Warlord Raymond Kane finally realized his flanks had been severed.

Through the observation windows of Tower B, Arthur saw the roof hatch of the center dredger fly open. A broad, scarred man wearing a grease-blackened leather duster and steel welding goggles stepped onto the catwalk, screaming curses into a megaphone.

*Raymond Kane.*

"TRAITORS IN THE SWITCH TOWER!" Kane’s voice bellowed across the classification yard. "DRIVER! FULL STEAM TO THE PISTONS! RAM THE CAST HOUSE DOORS! FLATTEN THE WHOLE DAMN FOUNDRY!"

The thirty-ton flagship dredger unleashed an ear-splitting shriek from its steam whistle.

Its vertical fire-tube boiler roared as coal was shoveled into the firebox. High-pressure steam flooded its dual thirty-inch drive cylinders. Thick black coal smoke poured from its stack as the iron behemoth surged down Track Five directly toward the barricaded entrance of Blast Furnace Number Four at twenty miles per hour.

Its front was armed with a five-ton solid steel ramming prow designed to smash through reinforced concrete.

If that thirty-ton mass struck the cast house barricades, the structural bosh columns supporting Blast Furnace 4 would shear like dry pine kindling, dumping four hundred tons of molten iron across seventy human beings.

Arthur Vance did not shout. He did not panic.

He reached into his waistcoat, clicked open his pocket watch, and verified the seconds.

*1:56 PM.*

"Elevated kinetic mass: thirty-two metric tons," Arthur calculated aloud. "Velocity: twenty-two feet per second. Momentum: seven hundred and four thousand pound-feet per second. A purely mechanical problem."

He turned to Eleanor. "Maintain the switch interlocks. I will resolve the locomotive intercept."

***

Arthur sprinted down the tower stairs, vaulted out into the ballast, and ran along Track Seven.

Emerging from the western repair shed at fifteen miles per hour came **Depot Shunter Number 7**, its twin Caterpillar diesels roaring under Davis’s steady hand on the throttle. On the front pilot footboard stood Arthur Vance, holding onto the handrail as the iron switcher matched speed along the parallel track.

Between Track Five and Track Seven lay the crossover switch Arthur had thrown from the tower.

"Davis! Notch Eight!" Arthur ordered into his throat mic. "Cut the throttle the moment I step across!"

The 44-ton shunter surged forward, pulling level with Kane’s roaring steam dredger.

High above on the dredger's catwalk, Kane spotted Arthur. 

"DIE, YOU BASTARD!" Kane roared, swinging the dual forty-millimeter Bofors autocannon down to point-blank elevation.

*KER-THUMP!*

A forty-millimeter armor-piercing shell blasted from the right barrel at three hundred feet.

Arthur Vance didn't flinch. 

He planted his boots on the shunter's steel running board, stepped into the direct line of fire, and snapped his left arm forward.

*The Sovereign Aegis locked.*

The two-foot curved buckler of slate-gray Star-Chitin, backed by its woven silver-copper mesh and three concentric rings of star-iron, met the forty-millimeter shell at a thirty-degree deflection angle.

*CLANGGGGGGGGG-CRACK!*

The concussive kinetic blast was deafening. The explosive shockwave shattered every window in the shunter cab behind Arthur. 

Yet the laws of geometry and star-chitin metallurgy held supreme.

The angled chitin plate deflected the two-pound steel penetrator upward, sending the ricochet screaming into the steel truss of an overhead signal bridge sixty feet in the air. 

Simultaneously, the three concentric star-iron induction rings absorbed the explosive shockwave, redirecting the massive kinetic energy down through Arthur’s legs and into the forty-four tons of rolling locomotive steel. 

Arthur’s feet did not move an eighth of an inch.

Before Kane could cycle the second barrel, Arthur leaped across the eight-foot gap between the rolling tracks, landing with a heavy, metallic crash directly on the running board of Kane’s roaring steam dredger.

Kane stumbled back, his eyes bulging behind his welding goggles as he drew a heavy .44 Magnum revolver.

"What the hell are you?!" Kane screamed, firing three shots at point-blank range.

*BANG! BANG! BANG!*

The lead bullets flattened harmlessly against the Sovereign Aegis buckler like soft lead coins, dropping to the iron grating.

"I am Arthur Vance," Arthur replied, his voice terrifyingly calm, polite, and steady beneath his rubber safety goggles. "Assistant Archivist of the Grand Municipal Library. Your operating license for heavy steam equipment is permanently revoked."

Arthur stepped past the stunned warlord. 

His sharp, analytical gray eyes locked onto the dredger’s mechanical vulnerability:

Mounted on the left side of the vertical boiler was the **High-Pressure Saturated Steam Manifold**—a brittle, uninsulated cast-iron bypass pipe eight inches in diameter, carrying **Two Hundred and Fifty PSI of steam at 406 degrees Fahrenheit** from the accumulator to the twin drive cylinders.

Arthur raised his right arm.

The **Chimera Frame Mark III** pressurized with a terrifying pneumatic shriek. The twin nitrogen cylinders charged to **Three Thousand PSI**.

Arthur drove his right fist forward into the cast-iron steam manifold flange.

He tripped the dual pneumatic solenoid.

*PSSSHT-KER-BLAM!*

The four-inch solid tungsten-carbide chisel shot forward with **two thousand four hundred pounds** of linear kinetic force.

*CRACK-SHATTER!*

The brittle cast-iron flange shattered like ice beneath an industrial sledgehammer.

Simultaneously, Arthur depressed the thumb switch on his left palm, dumping thirty-five thousand volts of starlight electrical current from his capacitors directly into the steam valve control solenoid.

*BZZZZZZT-BOOOOOOOOOOOOOM!*

A catastrophic, deafening eruption of superheated saturated steam roared from the fractured manifold.

Four hundred and six degrees of high-pressure vapor blasted across the dredger’s machinery bay, blowing the boiler’s emergency copper fusible plugs and quenching the coal firebox in a torrent of boiling white foam.

Deprived of steam pressure, the massive thirty-inch drive pistons locked instantly in their cylinders.

The thirty-ton iron behemoth skidded along the steel rails of Track Five, sparks showering forty feet into the air as its locked steel wheels ground against the track, coming to a dead, shuddering halt thirty paces before the cast house doors of Blast Furnace Number Four.

Warlord Raymond Kane, choked by steam and blinded by soot, tumbled off the running board into the ballast, coughing violently as he raised his hands in absolute, pathetic surrender.

From the surrounding classification tracks, Sergeant Cole, Captain Cross, and ten combat engineers surrounded the remaining thirty disoriented marauders, their M4 carbines and M2 Browning zeroed in with cold military finality.

The battle for Vulcan Junction was over.

Arthur Vance stepped down from the hissing, immobilized steam dredger into the crushed gravel ballast. 

He reached into his waistcoat, retrieved his brass pocket watch, clicked it open, and verified the mechanical movement.

*2:02 PM.*

"Elapsed combat duration: eighteen minutes, twelve seconds," Arthur announced calmly. "Structural damage to railway permanent way: zero point zero percent. Enemy mechanized assets: neutralized."

Behind him, the heavy steel doors of Blast Furnace Number Four swung open.

Master Foundryman Joseph Kovacs, flanked by seventy soot-covered, weeping steelworkers holding tapping rods, strode out into the daylight, staring at the lone archivist in the gray coat who had just dismantled an iron warlord with a pair of spectacles and a pneumatic chisel.

Arthur adjusted his wire-rimmed glasses, looked at Kovacs, and gave a crisp, professional nod.

"Master Foundryman Kovacs," Arthur Vance said. "We have twenty tons of wheat flour on Track Seven. And your blast furnace has sixty-two minutes before the slag begins to freeze."

Arthur gestured toward the roaring hearth.

"Let us go make steel."
