# Chapter 93: The Rust Hegemony of Gary

The southern shore of Lake Michigan was an apocalyptic monument to iron, fire, and three centuries of industrial entropy.

*Day 32 // 8:20 AM.*

Rolling off the Indiana Harbor Belt mainline, the armored train *Prometheus* slowed to five miles per hour as it entered the colossal industrial reservation of Gary, Indiana. The ambient temperature was two degrees Fahrenheit, with gale-force northern winds whipping sheets of frozen lake-spray across four thousand acres of rusted steelworks, crumbling coke batteries, and towering mountains of iron ore pellets and limestone flux.

Rising against the gray winter sky like the cathedrals of a forgotten metallurgical titan were twelve colossal blast furnaces. Most stood dead, their towering hundred-and-fifty-foot steel mantles wrapped in rust and choked with dead scrub trees. But at the eastern end of the complex, near the frozen turning basin of the Grand Calumet River, a single chimney stack still breathed:

**Gary Blast Furnace Number Eight.**

A thick, undulating plume of fiery orange sulfur gas and incandescent dust billowed from the furnace top, accompanied by the deep, agonizing shudder of struggling steam turboblowers.

Arthur Vance stood on the open gangway of the locomotive cab, his binoculars focused on the casthouse apron two hundred yards ahead.

Beside him stood Donald Vance Miller, his gloved hands gripping the brass cab rail, his face filled with visceral, agonizing recognition.

"Furnace Eight," Miller rasped, pointing toward the roaring base of the mantle. "The last living blast furnace in the Midwest. My father helped pour the iron out of that hearth when I was a boy. If Furnace Eight dies, the entire steel craft between the Mississippi and the Atlantic goes cold forever."

"And currently, it is on the verge of catastrophic solidification," Arthur observed, pulling his leather ledger from his greatcoat and clicking his mechanical pencil. "Observe the top gas flame: its color is shifting from clear reducing violet to a dull, smoky yellow. The blast pressure has spiked to thirty-eight PSI while the wind volume has collapsed by forty percent. The furnace is suffering from severe **Hearth Chill and Slag Viscosity Inversion**."

Master Joseph Kovacs leaned out of the cab, his face pale beneath his soot. "Hearth chill?! God in heaven, Arthur! If the hearth temperature drops below two thousand two hundred degrees Fahrenheit, sixty tons of molten pig iron will freeze into a solid block of dead skull iron! It'll take twenty sticks of dynamite just to clear the hearth, and the furnace will never blow again!"

"Which is why we have precisely forty-five minutes to intervene," Arthur said calmly.

The *Prometheus* pulled onto the raw material high-line trestle, stopping directly above the stockhouse bins.

Arrayed across the casthouse railway apron, barricaded behind overturned slag ladles and heavy steel billet shields, were eighty heavily armed smelters of the **Gary Metallurgical Syndicate**. They wore scorched split-cowhide foundry coats, asbestos aprons, and tinted cobalt-glass face shields, aiming heavy pneumatic rivet guns, converted carbon-arc torches, and scoped bolt-action hunting rifles directly at the locomotive.

At their head stood a formidable woman in a flame-blackened leather furnace master's jacket, holding an ignited magnesium flare in one hand and a six-foot star-iron tapping bar in the other.

"Stand down that engine!" the woman bellowed across the howling wind. "I am **Chief Smelter Karen Novak**! If you’re Malakor’s raiders coming for the iron tithe, you’re too late! Furnace Eight is dying! We will blow the bustle pipe before we let a warlord take our hearth!"

Donald Miller stepped down from the pilot car, raising both hands into the clear, icy light.

"Karen! Put down the flare!" Miller shouted with desperate urgency. "It's Donald! Donald Miller!"

Karen Novak pushed up her tinted face shield, revealing sharp, soot-blackened cheekbones, graying braided hair, and blazing, skeptical hazel eyes.

"Donald?!" Karen gasped, her jaw dropping as she saw his clean, unarmored duster and the sterile white dressing covering his eye. "Where is your chrome armor? Where is your Colossus?"

"The Colossus is scrap, Karen! It's generating heat for the refugees in Council Bluffs!" Miller called out, stepping toward the barricade. "I am no longer Baron! The Iron Barony is dead! The man behind me is Arthur Vance, Chief Archivist of the Western Confederation! He has the blueprints of the ancients, pure star-iron rail, and the power to save Furnace Eight!"

Karen Novak stepped onto the top of the slag ladle, glaring at Arthur’s clean charcoal waistcoat, his pocket watch chain, and the gleaming titanium-niobium struts of the **Chimera Frame Mark VI**.

"An archivist?" Karen spat bitterly. "You brought an academic in a three-piece suit to save a froze-up blast furnace?! The hearth is dropping twenty degrees a minute! The tuyeres are burning back! The slag has turned to sticky black glue! In thirty minutes, this furnace will be a sixty-ton tombstone of solid cast iron!"

Arthur Vance stepped down from the train, his boots crunching calmly on the black coal dust.

Beside him walked Eleanor Vance, her gentle Mid-Tier Sovereign starlight casting a warm golden perimeter that parted the bitter freezing wind, keeping the toxic sulfur fumes from entering their lungs.

Arthur stopped exactly ten paces from Karen Novak’s tapping bar.

"Chief Smelter Novak," Arthur began, his voice dry, analytical, and devoid of the slightest tremor. "Your diagnostic assessment of the hearth is accurate, but your thermodynamic conclusion is deficient."

Karen blinked, taken aback by his icy clinical tone. "My conclusion?!"

"You are currently burning low-grade, high-ash Illinois bituminous coke containing twelve percent volatile matter and two point four percent sulfur," Arthur stated, consulting his ledger. "The excessive ash content has elevated your slag basicity ratio $\text{CaO} / \text{SiO}_2$ above one point four five, dramatically increasing the slag melting point from two thousand one hundred degrees to two thousand three hundred and eighty degrees Fahrenheit. When your un-preheated blast air dropped below one thousand six hundred degrees due to moisture condensation in the stove checkers, the slag began to freeze around the blowpipes, choking the combustion raceway."

Karen Novak froze, her mouth slightly open. The smelters behind her lowered their weapons, staring at the archivist in absolute shock.

"How... how could you possibly know our basicity ratio without taking a slag sample?" Karen whispered.

"Basic chemical stoichiometry," Arthur replied deadpan. "The color of your stack discharge indicates incomplete carbon monoxide combustion resulting from insufficient blast enthalpy. The thermodynamic equation governing your hearth heat balance is elementary:"

$$\Delta H_{\text{hearth}} = Q_{\text{blast}} + Q_{\text{combustion}} - \left(Q_{\text{iron}} + Q_{\text{slag}} + Q_{\text{losses}}\right)$$

"Your net heat balance is currently negative forty-two thousand BTU per minute per tuyere," Arthur explained. "To reverse the hearth freeze, we do not require dynamite or prayers. We require three simultaneous thermodynamic interventions:"

"First: an immediate injection of pure liquid oxygen into the cold blast main, elevating blast oxygen content from twenty-one percent to thirty-two percent, doubling the raceway flame temperature to three thousand four hundred degrees Fahrenheit."

"Second: the introduction of two thousand pounds of calcined fluorspar and star-iron flux pellets from our flatcar, which will immediately drop your slag viscosity from twenty-five poise to three point two poise."

"And third: the mechanical clearance of the eight frozen tuyere blowpipes using the high-pressure pneumatic chisel of the Chimera Frame Mark VI."

Karen Novak looked from Arthur to Donald Miller, then up at Eleanor’s tranquil, starlight-bathed eyes.

"You have liquid oxygen?" Karen demanded, her voice cracking with desperate hope. "On that train?"

"We carry forty thousand gallons of cryogenic liquid oxygen in our vacuum-insulated tender cars, transported directly from the petrochemical liquefaction towers of the Wasatch Refining Depot," Arthur confirmed.

Karen Novak hurled her magnesium flare into the snow, leaping down from the slag ladle.

"Open the high-line gates!" Karen screamed at her smelters. "Get the oxygen lines coupled to the bustle pipe! Bring the star-iron flux! If this man can save Furnace Eight, he owns the Gary works!"

Within twelve minutes, the railhead was transformed into a coordinated metallurgical rescue operation.

Confederation roughnecks and Gary smelters dragged heavy braided-stainless-steel cryogenic hoses from the *Prometheus* tender, clamping them directly into the blast furnace’s primary cold-blast main. 

Arthur Vance stepped up to the casthouse apron, standing directly before the glowing, shuddering base of Furnace Eight.

The heat radiating from the furnace bosh was an oppressive wave of two thousand degrees Fahrenheit, but the vacuum-jacketed silica aerogel blankets lining the **Chimera Frame Mark VI** halted the radiant heat with zero thermal transfer to Arthur's waistcoat.

Arthur pulled his gold pocket watch, clicked the hunter case open, and checked the second hand.

*8:48:15 AM.*

"Master Kovacs, crack the liquid oxygen valve," Arthur instructed through his throat microphone. "Introduce twelve hundred cubic feet per minute of ninety-nine-point-five-percent pure oxygen into the bustle pipe. Mark."

*FSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSH!*

Cryogenic liquid oxygen flashed into superheated gas as it entered the blast main, surging through the twelve copper tuyeres directly into the furnace hearth.

The chemical reaction inside the white-hot raceway was instantaneous and breathtaking.

The blast furnace roared like a waking dragon! 

The combustion temperature inside the hearth skyrocketed by eight hundred degrees in twenty seconds. The dull, smoky yellow top gas flame flared into a brilliant, dazzling jet of pure reducing violet and incandescent white!

Arthur Vance stepped directly onto the mud-gun platform before Tuyere Number Four—where a thick, frozen plug of black slag had completely choked the blowpipe.

He leveled the Mark VI’s vanadium-alloyed diamond chisel directly against the frozen slag collar.

He set the tandem hydraulic intensifier to **Seven Thousand Five Hundred PSI**.

"Clearing Tuyere Four," Arthur said.

*KABOOOOOM!*

Thirty thousand pounds of focused kinetic thrust punched through the vitrified slag crust with the sound of a naval artillery cannon.

*CRACK-SHATTER!*

The frozen slag plug exploded into thousands of brittle glass fragments. 

*ROOOOAAAR!*

A blinding torrent of three-thousand-degree white-hot blast air surged through the cleared blowpipe, illuminating the casthouse in breathtaking, incandescent glory!

Arthur moved methodically around the circular bustle pipe, clearing Tuyere Five, Tuyere Six, and Tuyere Seven in rapid, synchronized four-minute intervals. 

With every cleared tuyere, the furnace pressure stabilized. 

On the master blast gauge, the needle dropped from thirty-eight PSI back to its optimal operating zone of twenty-four PSI. Inside the hearth, sixty tons of frozen skull iron liquefied into a churning, free-flowing river of pure molten pig iron.

Karen Novak and fifty veteran ironworkers stood on the casthouse deck, their eyes wide with tear-filled, reverent awe.

"It's liquid..." Karen wept, slapping her leather gloves against the railing. "The hearth is liquid! The furnace is saved!"

Karen turned to Arthur Vance, taking off her furnace master's helmet and bowing her head with profound, unyielding respect.

"Chief Archivist Vance," Karen Novak declared, her voice trembling with emotion. "You didn't just save a machine. You saved the soul of Gary. From this day forward, the Gary Metallurgical Syndicate answers to the Western Confederation."

Arthur Vance released the hydraulic chisel, wiping a fleck of gray dolomite dust from his wool lapel. 

He pulled his gold pocket watch, clicked the lid open, and checked the time:

*9:24:10 AM.*

"Total hearth rescue duration: thirty-five minutes and fifty-five seconds," Arthur recorded in his ledger with his mechanical pencil. "Furnace Eight thermal equilibrium restored. Slag viscosity: three point two poise. Operational capacity: fifteen hundred tons of molten iron per day."

Arthur snapped the pocket watch shut.

He looked through the casthouse arch toward the frozen expanse of Lake Michigan.

"Now that Furnace Eight is breathing," Arthur said calmly, "we prepare to tap the iron and begin the restoration of the Inland Steel oxygen converters."
