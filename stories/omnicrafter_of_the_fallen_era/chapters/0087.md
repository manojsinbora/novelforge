# Chapter 87: The Fall of Baron Malakor

The winter sun rose over the frost-glazed roofs of Council Bluffs, casting brilliant silver light across an ocean of surrendered steel.

*Day 28 // 8:15 AM.*

In the expansive freight marshalling yard east of the passenger terminal, the defeated army of the Iron Barony of the Rust Belt stood in orderly, subdued ranks under the watchful overwatch of Captain Rachel Cross and Company B. 

Arranged across the frozen gravel were mounds of surrendered weaponry: four hundred and twenty bolt-action and semi-automatic rifles, sixty heavy machine guns, eighteen armored steam technicals with their distributor linkages safely removed, and two hundred thousand pounds of salvaged scrap iron, ammunition casings, and high-tensile wire rope. 

At the center of the yard, the tilted, sixty-foot bulk of the **Colossus of Gary** stood silent and dead, its boilers cold, its severed steam lines capped with lead plugs, and its massive walking shoes sinking harmlessly into the frozen Iowa clay.

Inside the heated brick waiting room of the Council Bluffs passenger station, a formal civil tribunal had been convened.

Arthur Vance sat behind a long oak desk, wearing his freshly brushed charcoal archivist three-piece suit. On the desk rested his open brass-bound leather ledger, his mechanical pencil, an antique brass balance scale, and his gold pocket watch. Over his shoulder, the **Chimera Frame Mark VI** stood docked in its mobile maintenance rig, its accumulator gauges resting at an idle four thousand PSI.

Beside Arthur stood Eleanor Vance, radiating a gentle, tranquil 528 Hz starlight that warmed the entire room to sixty-eight degrees Fahrenheit, banishing the freezing draft from the broken frosted-glass transoms.

Seated in a heavy oak chair before the desk, stripped of his hammered chrome armor plates and wolf-pelt mantle, was **Baron Malakor**.

In the clean light of day, without his war harness and terrifying bullhorn, Malakor was revealed to be an exhausted, prematurely aged man in his late fifties. His face was weathered like dried saddle leather, marked by deep lines of chronic strain and pain. Around his left eye, the crude brass-gear cybernetic ocular implant was severely corroded with green verdigris and battery acid scale, leaking toxic zinc-copper fluids into the inflamed tissue of his orbital socket.

Arthur Vance looked up from his ledger, his gray eyes calm, calculating, and devoid of rancor.

"Baron Malakor," Arthur began, his voice dry and steady. "Real name: Donald Vance Miller, born in the year two hundred and fifty-four post-Collapse at the Gary industrial settlement on Lake Michigan. Formerly apprentice machinist at the Inland Steel rolling mills before seizing control of regional salvage gangs twenty-two years ago."

Malakor flinched, his single biological eye widening in shock. "How do you know my name? That name died thirty years ago."

"Archival records recovered from the telecommunications central office in Omaha," Arthur replied calmly, clicking his mechanical pencil. "The First Dynasty preserved comprehensive municipal census and personnel registries across optical storage media. An archivist does not forget."

Arthur tapped the ledger page with the eraser of his pencil.

"I have completed the actuarial audit of your twenty-two years as ruler of the Iron Barony of the Rust Belt," Arthur continued. "The ledger reveals a catastrophic socio-economic failure. Through the coercive enforcement of the so-called *Iron Tithe*, your warbands extorted an average of two hundred and forty thousand bushels of grain and eighty tons of scrap iron annually from forty-two farming communes along the Missouri and Des Moines river basins."

Arthur turned the page, pointing to a neatly drawn balance column:

$$\text{Extortion Caloric Deficit: } \Delta E = -180,000 \text{ kcal/person/year}$$

$$\text{Infant Mortality Rate in Tithed Communes: } 14.2\% \quad (\text{vs. } 2.1\% \text{ Confederation Baseline})$$

"By confiscating fifty percent of their agricultural surplus while destroying regional rail and bridge infrastructure," Arthur said, "you induced chronic malnutrition across twelve thousand human beings. Furthermore, seventy-two percent of the metal you extorted was wasted on non-productive siege machines—such as your two-hundred-and-ten-ton Bucyrus-Erie monstrosity—which contributed zero calories, zero medical aid, and zero civil security to the continent. Your rule was not an empire of strength, Mr. Miller. It was a parasitic thermodynamic drain."

Malakor hung his head, his hands trembling against his worn denim trousers.

"You don't know what it was like out there in the Rust Belt," Malakor rasped, his voice breaking with decades of accumulated bitterness. "When the mills failed and the power died, people were eating rats in the streets of Chicago and Gary. Warlords were skinning families for copper wire! I built an army to keep the raiders away! I gave them order! I gave them iron!"

"You gave them feudal tyranny and mechanical incompetence," Arthur corrected without raising his voice. "Order without standardized measurement, without civil law, and without sustainable agricultural reproduction is merely organized predation."

Arthur stood up from the desk, walking around the table with measured steps. He reached into his medical kit and withdrew a sterile stainless-steel scalpel, a pair of platinum-tipped forceps, and a bottle of bio-compatible silver-nitrate antiseptic:

"Furthermore, Mr. Miller, your judgment has been severely impaired by advanced galvanic neuro-toxicity," Arthur observed, pointing his forceps at the corroded brass ocular implant. "Your cybernetic ocular assembly was fabricated using dissimilar metals: brass casing, copper wire, and lead-tin solder. Bathed in ocular fluid and sweat, the dissimilar metals formed a miniature galvanic cell generating zero point eight volts of continuous electrical leakage, poisoning your optic nerve with lead ions and inducing chronic cerebral inflammation."

Malakor’s hand reflexively touched his swollen, blackened left eyebrow. "The headaches... they never stopped for ten years. Every time the weather changed, it felt like white-hot needles in my brain."

"A predictable manifestation of heavy-metal neuropathy," Arthur said. "Hold your head still."

Arthur stepped forward. Beside him, Eleanor placed a gentle, radiant hand on Malakor’s right shoulder.

A warm, soothing wave of 528 Hz starlight passed through Malakor’s body. The searing, throbbing migraine that had tormented the warlord for a decade vanished in a heartbeat, replaced by a profound, numb coolness that relaxed his clenched jaw.

With surgical speed and microscopic precision, Arthur Vance severed the corroded brass mounting screws with his fine diamond cutters, lifting the toxic, green-crusted ocular assembly cleanly from the socket without tearing a single millimeter of healthy skin.

*SNAP-CLICK.*

He swabbed the inflamed orbital tissue with silver-nitrate antiseptic and applied a sterile, breathable collagen dressing soaked in Eleanor’s synthesized Sovereign lipid stabilizer.

Malakor exhaled a ragged, shuddering breath, a tear rolling down his weathered cheek. He touched the clean white dressing, his expression transformed from feral defiance into bewildered, quiet peace.

"The needles... the fire in my head... it’s gone," Malakor whispered, staring at Arthur in disbelief. "Why didn't you just hang me from the gantry? That's what every warlord between here and the Great Lakes would have done."

"Execution is an irrecoverable waste of mechanical skill," Arthur replied, wiping his instruments clean and returning them to the case. "You spent thirty years in the machine shops of Inland Steel. You understand gear cutting, lathe tooling, boiler tube rolling, and steam distribution manifolds. The Western Confederation does not execute productive labor; we redirect it."

Arthur returned to his desk, picking up his fountain pen and sliding a heavy parchment document across the blotter:

"**The Midwestern Industrial Reconstruction Covenant**," Arthur read:

"Article One: The Iron Barony of the Rust Belt is permanently dissolved. All four hundred and twenty surrendered combatants are paroled into the Confederation Civil Labor Corps under the supervision of Captain Cross.
Article Two: Donald Vance Miller is stripped of the title of Baron and assigned to a five-year restorative civil labor term as **Master Maintenance Machinist of the Council Bluffs Rail Depot**, under the direct authority of Master Joseph Kovacs.
Article Three: All extorted grain, livestock, and tools held in the Barony’s forward depots are hereby restored to the farming communes of the Missouri and Des Moines river basins."

Arthur placed the pen before Malakor. "Sign the covenant, Mr. Miller."

Donald Miller’s hand trembled as he picked up the pen. With a deep breath, he signed his true name across the parchment line:

*Donald Vance Miller.*

Eleanor placed her hand upon the signature, her starlight sealing the covenant with an indelible golden watermark.

"Now, Mr. Miller," Arthur said, turning to page eighty-six of his ledger. "We address the disposition of your Colossus."

"She’s ruined, isn't she?" Miller asked quietly. "The cam sheared, the header blown."

"As a walking siege engine, it was an engineering monstrosity and has been permanently decommissioned," Arthur answered. "However, its two vertical fire-tube boilers possess a combined thermal capacity of fifteen tons of steam per hour, and its electrical dynamometer generates two point four megawatts. Under Master Kovacs’ direction, the chassis will be stripped of its walking shoes and flail arms and converted into the **Council Bluffs Central District Heating & Power Station**. It will provide continuous electricity and district steam heating for the passenger terminal, the refugee hospital, and five square miles of new civilian housing."

Arthur turned to Master Kovacs, who had just entered the office carrying a clipboard of mechanical tooling orders.

"Master Kovacs," Arthur directed. "Unbolt the eccentric walking cams and remount them onto stationary concrete piers as ten-ton inertia flywheels for the two-point-four-megawatt dynamo. Re-plumb the boiler superheater lines into our closed-loop district hydronic piping network. By nightfall, this station must be delivering heat to every residential building within two miles."

"Consider it done, Arthur!" Kovacs grinned broadly, slapping Miller on the shoulder. "Come along, Miller. Let's see if you remember how to scrape babbitt bearings on an actual dynamo!"

Meanwhile, outside in the freight shed, Eleanor oversaw thirty village elders who had arrived in horse-drawn sledges from the surrounding farming communes. With joyful tears and profound relief, the farmers received fifty thousand bushels of their stolen corn and wheat back, along with twenty sacks of elite heritage seed stock from the Omaha vault.

At 10:15 AM, Arthur ascended the communications platform of the *Prometheus*.

Sergeant Major Bradley threw the switch on the transcontinental telegraph line:

*CLICK-CLACK! CLICK-CLACK!*

Arthur transmitted the official victory dispatch to the continental network:

**[TO: HUB 01 BUNKER 07 // HUB 02 OAKHAVEN // HUB 10 YELLOWSTONE]**  
**[FROM: EXPEDITION COMMAND // COUNCIL BLUFFS IOWA]**  
**[IRON BARONY DISSOLVED. HOSTILE SIEGE ASSETS CONVERTED TO MUNICIPAL POWER GENERATION. 12,000 CITIZENS SECURED. ADVANCING TOWARD ANCHOR NODE 06.]**

Within two minutes, Oakhaven acknowledged:  
**[CONGRATULATIONS ARCHIVIST. THE MIDWEST IS OPEN.]**

Arthur checked his gold pocket watch.

*10:24 AM.*

"One final matter, Mr. Miller. Your survey maps indicated that your scout columns located the subterranean First Dynasty research complex in central Iowa."

Miller nodded eagerly, pulling a folded pre-Collapse road atlas from his coat pocket and spreading it across the desk. He tapped a red grease-pencil circle eighteen miles west of Des Moines, along the Raccoon River valley:

"Facility Nine," Miller said, pointing to the coordinates. "Buried beneath an ancient seed testing station. We tried to force the outer blast doors with five kegs of black powder six months ago, but we couldn't even scratch the metal. The whole ground around the vault hums with a strange vibration that makes your teeth ache."

Arthur examined the topographical coordinates, matching them with the optical data disk recovered from Omaha:

"Subsurface Facility Zero-Nine: **First Dynasty Anchor Node Six**," Arthur confirmed, his eyes lighting with calculated purpose. "The Bio-Resonant Horticultural Core of the Midwest."

Arthur closed his ledger with a decisive, crisp snap.

He stood upright, buttoning his charcoal archivist greatcoat and adjusting his cuffs.

"The Iron Barony is demobilized. Council Bluffs is secured. The power plant is operational."

Arthur looked at Eleanor and Captain Cross.

"Captain Cross, whistle up the *Prometheus*. We depart for Des Moines to awaken Anchor Node Six."
