# Chapter 35: The First Pour

The interior of Blast Furnace Number Four was an inferno waiting on the knife-edge of death.

*Day 5 // 2:42 PM.*

Inside the colossal cast house, the heat was a physical blow to the chest. The air smelled of sulfur dioxide, burning anthracite coal, and the dry, blinding radiation of white-hot refractory brick.

Towering eighty feet above the sand floor rose the circular belly of the blast furnace. Wrapped around its waist like a bronze belt were twenty water-cooled **copper tuyeres**—the heavy blowpipes that injected compressed air into the roaring core of the hearth.

Arthur Vance stood on the main pouring platform, his wool coat replaced by a heavy, full-length split-cowhide foundry apron. Over his spectacles, he wore green shade-five cobalt furnace goggles. His clear eyes watched the optical pyrometer gauge on the control console:

```
HEARTH TEMPERATURE: 842°C (1,547°F)
COOLING WATER PRESSURE: 8.2 PSI // CRITICAL LOW
SLAG VISCOSITY: ELEVATED // THICKENING
TIME BEFORE SOLIDIFICATION: 28 MINUTES
```

Beside Arthur stood Master Foundryman Joseph Kovacs, his leather hood pushed back, wiping sweat from his gray beard with a blackened canvas glove.

"The hearth is choking, Arthur!" Kovacs shouted over the deafening hiss of the dying blowers. "The cooling water pressure dropped when Kane's men shot our primary intake pump. If those copper tuyeres burn out from the heat, the cooling water will leak into the hearth and trigger a steam explosion that will tear this furnace off its foundation!"

"The primary intake pump is an induction-motor-driven centrifugal unit rated for four hundred and eighty volts," Arthur answered calmly, not shouting, his cadence cutting through the roar with mechanical clarity. 

"Miller! Davis! Run our heavy three-phase locomotive feeder cables from Depot Shunter Number Seven through the cast house window! Tie the shunter's auxiliary forty-kilowatt diesel generator directly into the pump motor starter!"

Miller didn't hesitate. Outside, sixty yards away, the Caterpillar diesel of Depot Shunter 7 roared to high idle. Within four minutes, heavy rubber-insulated cables were hauled through the window and clamped into the motor contactor.

*CLACK-WHIRRRRRRR!*

The heavy centrifugal water pump surged back to life.

On the console, the cooling water pressure gauge leaped instantly from eight PSI to **Sixty-Two PSI**. Streams of cold river water flooded through the copper tuyeres, carrying away excess heat with a furious, relieved hiss of clean steam.

"Tuyere cooling restored," Arthur recorded in his field notebook with a flick of his pencil. "Now, we restore hearth stoichiometry."

Working under Kovacs’s shouted commands, forty steelworkers hauled two tons of metallurgical coke and ten bags of crushed limestone flux to the base of the skip hoist. The motorized skip bucket rattled up the eighty-foot inclined track, dumping the charge directly through the double-bell hopper at the top of the furnace.

Inside the hearth, the fresh coke met the compressed air blast.

*ROOOOOOOOAR!*

A deep, volcanic rumble shook the concrete pouring floor. The pyrometer needle reversed its descent, climbing swiftly: **920°C... 1,080°C... 1,350°C.**

"Combustion stabilized!" Kovacs bellowed with a roar of pure, triumphant laughter that echoed off the structural steel girders. "The hearth is alive! The iron is molten!"

Arthur stepped to the edge of the taphole platform, where Eleanor stood quietly beside a wooden crate unbolted from the flatbed technical truck.

The crate contained two extraordinary materials:
First: Six sacks of pulverized **star-iron ore tailings** dredged from the River Pylon’s foundation flume.
Second: Three pounds of micro-braided **star-iron wing filaments** harvested from the fallen Sky-Rift Wyrm on Mount Sentinel.

Kovacs knelt beside the open crate, running his blackened fingers through the iridescent, slate-gray metallic powder. His eyes widened behind his cobalt goggles.

"Sweet mother of steel..." Kovacs whispered in reverent awe. "I’ve spent forty years working blast furnaces from Gary, Indiana to the Ruhr Valley. I have never seen an iron ore with a crystalline luster like this. What is this alloy?"

"It is meteoric star-iron," Arthur explained smoothly, pulling a slide rule from his leather apron. 

"Its tensile yield strength exceeds twelve gigapascals, with zero electrical resistance and a negative coefficient of thermal expansion when aligned with high-frequency electromagnetic resonance. If we introduce zero point zero four percent of this refined powder into the molten iron during tapping, and subject the stream to Eleanor’s starlight harmonic frequency, we will create an entirely new metallurgical grade: **Star-Infused Structural Alloy Steel**."

"Can it be rolled into heavy rail?" Kovacs asked, his breath catching.

"It will possess three times the fatigue limit of standard high-carbon rail steel," Arthur confirmed. "It will resist thermal cracking down to minus sixty degrees, and will conduct starlight resonance across hundreds of miles of continental track without signal attenuation."

Kovacs looked at Arthur, then at Eleanor, and slowly smiled a broad, toothy grin.

"Then let's make history, boy. Grab the oxygen lance!"

***

At 4:15 PM, the cast house was cleared for the pour.

Seventy steelworkers, suited in silver-foiled aluminized fire suits and asbestos face shields, stood behind the safety barriers along the concrete runners.

Arthur Vance stood at the furnace taphole, holding an eight-foot **Oxygen Thermal Lancing Pipe** connected to twin cylinders of high-pressure oxygen. Beside him, Eleanor took her position on the elevated observer platform, her hands resting on the copper safety railing.

Her Starlight Sovereign resonance flooded the cavernous room, filling the smoky air with a tranquil, shimmering golden-sapphire aura that caused the suspended coal dust to glow like floating stars.

"Open the oxygen valve," Arthur ordered.

*HSSSSSSST!*

Arthur touched the steel tip of the lance to a burning birch splinter.

The iron pipe ignited into a four-thousand-degree chemical cutting flame. Arthur drove the burning pipe straight into the refractory clay plug sealing the furnace taphole.

*FZZZZZZT-CRACK!*

The ceramic plug burned through in eight seconds flat.

Arthur withdrew the lance and leaped back onto the raised safety walkway.

*ROOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM!*

A blinding, incandescent river of four hundred tons of molten pig iron—flowing at **one thousand five hundred and fifty degrees Celsius (2,822°F)**—erupted from the furnace taphole in an awe-inspiring, roaring torrent of liquid white fire.

Showers of golden sparks shot forty feet into the air, bouncing harmlessly off the corrugated iron roof.

The molten river cascaded down the refractory sand runners, heading directly toward the heavy two-hundred-ton torpedo ladles on the lower rail siding.

Arthur threw the release lever on the hopper chute.

A continuous stream of pulverized star-iron powder dropped directly into the molten iron torrent.

Simultaneously, Eleanor raised both hands, her green eyes blazing with pure, serene celestial sovereignty.

*SHUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUU!*

Her Starlight Sovereign resonance enveloped the flowing river of molten metal.

The effect was nothing short of miraculous.

Under normal tapping conditions, molten iron boils and spits violently as dissolved gases escape. But under Eleanor's starlight frequency, the boiling ceased instantly. The molten metal transformed into a glassy, smooth, incandescent ribbon of glowing gold and turquoise light, flowing with the viscosity of liquid silk.

Every microscopic impurity—dissolved hydrogen, sulfur, and nitrogen—was instantaneously expelled into the slag crust, leaving behind an ultra-pure, defect-free crystalline molecular lattice.

The liquid steel poured through the water-cooled copper molds of the **Continuous Bloom Caster**, solidifying in seconds under high-pressure water sprays into massive, glowing slabs of red-hot alloy steel.

From the bloom caster, the red-hot steel entered the heavy rolling train.

*CLANG-THUMP! CLANG-THUMP!*

The massive four-high rolling mills compressed the glowing billets through eight successive shaping passes, reducing the coarse dendritic grain structure into a dense, fine-grained pearlitic-bainitic matrix. 

Water-descaling jets operating at two thousand PSI stripped the mill scale with sharp, acoustic cracks, shaping the alloy into magnificent, thirty-nine-foot sections of **One-Hundred-and-Thirty-Six-Pound-per-Yard Continuous Star-Infused Rail Steel**.

Arthur stepped to the runoff table with his optical pyrometer and a portable Brinell hardness tester. He pressed the tungsten indenter against the cooling rail head:

"Brinell hardness: four hundred and ten HBW," Arthur reported with deep professional satisfaction. "Yield strength: one thousand one hundred and twenty megapascals. Elongation: fourteen percent. Zero inclusions, zero hydrogen embrittlement. The molecular integration of star-iron into the carbon matrix has created a self-healing crystalline lattice."

The rails emerged onto the cooling beds glowing with a soft, permanent, deep sapphire sheen that never faded.

The entire cast house erupted into an uncontrollable, deafening roar of cheers and applause.

Seventy veteran steelworkers wept openly, hugging each other, banging their tapping rods against the structural steel columns, and tossing their leather caps into the air. Outside the pouring shed, their families—spouses and children who had spent five days shivering in the foundry cellars fearing starvation and slaughter—poured into the warm yard as Davis and Miller began unloading the twenty tons of hard red winter wheat flour from Car 42.

Dr. Ward immediately set up a mobile triage station beside the foundry dispensary, treating chemical burns and heat exhaustion with fresh zinc-silver tinctures.

Master Foundryman Joseph Kovacs walked up to Arthur Vance on the pouring platform. His aluminized coat was smoking, his eyes bright with tears.

Without a word, Kovacs reached into his heavy canvas toolbelt, pulled out a heavy brass foundry seal stamped with the crossed hammers of the **Cascade Steelworkers Guild**, and laid it squarely in Arthur’s open, leather-gloved palm.

"Arthur Vance," Kovacs said, his booming voice trembling with deep, unyielding reverence. "The Cascade Steel Works formally signs the **Confederation Metallurgy Protocol**. Our four blast furnaces, our rolling mills, our seventy craftsmen, and every ton of steel we pour from this day forth belong to the Blackwood Confederation."

Arthur looked down at the heavy brass seal. He turned it over in his hand, feeling the solid, honest weight of human labor and industrial pride.

"Your alliance is accepted, Master Kovacs," Arthur Vance said, his voice calm, polite, and steady. "The Confederation guarantees your workers nineteen tons of wheat flour, free medical treatment from St Jude’s archives, and continuous hydroelectric power from Iron Ridge Dam. In return, you will forge forty miles of continuous rail to link the Three Pylons."

Arthur reached into his waistcoat, pulled out his brass pocket watch, and clicked the release stem.

*6:14 PM.*

He looked through the open bay doors of the cast house toward the western horizon.

Across the vast river valley, the setting sun bathed the rolling hills and pine forests in brilliant rose and gold. 

Far beyond the rail yards, along the frontier roads and across the distant survivor settlements, the news was already spreading like wildfire. Survivors, wandering refugees, and terrified warlord remnants were staring eastward at the great plumes of white steam rising from the newly awakened stacks of Vulcan Junction.

The radio waves on the civilian emergency bands were crackling with awed, superstitious terror:

The feared and invincible **Ghost Artificer of Blackwood Ridge** had not merely slain monsters—he had marched into Vulcan Junction, broken an iron warlord’s mechanized army in eighteen minutes, and commanded the very fires of the earth to forge rails of glowing star-steel.

Arthur Vance pulled out his leather-bound field journal and freshly sharpened pencil. He pressed the lead to the clean cream paper, his handwriting angular, precise, and permanent:

```
Record Date: Day 5 // Post-Convergence.
Location: Cascade Steel Works // Vulcan Junction.
Status: Blast Furnace No. 4 saved from catastrophic freeze; operational at 100%.
First Heat: 100 metric tons of Star-Infused 136-lb rail steel poured successfully.
Alliance Formed: 70 master metallurgists integrated into the Blackwood Confederation.
Logistical Victory: Heavy metallurgical and structural steel independence achieved.
Next Objective: Lay continuous welded rail connecting Vulcan Junction to Oakhaven and Sentinel Base.
The work continues.
```

Arthur closed the journal with a crisp, authoritative snap, tucked his pencil into his waistcoat pocket, and turned toward the quiet hum of Depot Shunter Number 7.

"Eleanor," Arthur Vance said, adjusting his spectacles with a faint, rare smile. "Let us supervise the unloading of the flour."

"The frontier has its steel."
