# Chapter 78: The Scrappers of the Black Hills

The dark pine-covered ridges of the Black Hills rose against the northern horizon like a fortress of ancient stone.

*Day 23 // 7:45 AM.*

Following the newly stabilized rail line northeast from Fort Platte along the old Chicago and North Western right-of-way, the armored train *Prometheus* slowed to five miles per hour as it approached a massive industrial graveyard buried in snow: the **Edgemont Locomotive Roundhouse and Smelting Yard**.

The temperature stood at zero degrees Fahrenheit. A dense ground fog hung over the frozen marshlands of the Cheyenne River, swirling around rusted rail cars, toppled coal trestles, and colossal spoil heaps of black smelter slag that had accumulated over centuries of mining before the Great Collapse.

Arthur Vance stood on the open gangway of the locomotive cab, his binoculars trained on the circular brick structure of the roundhouse three hundred yards ahead.

Beside him, Master Joseph Kovacs tapped his cold pipe against the brass cab railing, squinting through the sulfur-tinted mist.

"If the old maps are accurate, Arthur," Kovacs rumbled, his thick mustache bristling with frost, "this junction was the primary trans-shipment hub for the Homestake vanadium and iron mines. If we want to harden the locomotive axles and fabricate high-strength tooling to punch through Baron Malakor’s heavy boilerplate armor, we need vanadium. Vanadium steel gives you twice the fatigue strength of plain carbon steel."

"Vanadium acts as a powerful grain-refining agent and carbide former," Arthur noted, pulling his leather ledger from his greatcoat. "Even a trace addition of zero point one five to zero point two zero percent vanadium to a chromium-steel melt increases the yield strength from six hundred megapascals to over twelve hundred megapascals, while maintaining exceptional fracture toughness at sub-zero temperatures. We require five hundred pounds of ferro-vanadium concentrate."

The train came to a smooth stop outside the roundhouse yard.

Standing atop the perimeter slag mounds, armed with heavy pneumatic rivet guns, converted mining drills, and scoped lever-action rifles, was a defensive perimeter of thirty heavily bundled fighters. They wore leather welding aprons over sheepskin coats, their faces protected by perforated iron welding masks painted with white skull motifs.

A tall woman in an armored leather welding jacket stepped forward onto the rim of the slag heap. She lowered an acetylene torch fitted with an igniter switch, resting a six-foot pry bar against her shoulder.

"Hold your fire, scrapper boys!" the woman barked in a clear, commanding voice. She looked down at the gleaming silver locomotive and Arthur’s clean charcoal waistcoat. "I am **Clara Thorne**, chief metallurgist of the Black Hills Scrapper League. Who brings a live steam engine into the Dead Lands?"

Arthur stepped down from the locomotive carriage, his boots crunching on the frozen ballast. Behind him, Eleanor Vance walked with graceful steps, her gentle Mid-Tier Sovereign starlight radiating in a soft aura that melted the frost from the cross-ties around her feet.

"I am Arthur Vance, Chief Archivist of the Western Confederation," Arthur replied calmly, stopping ten paces from the base of the mound. "We are restoring the transcontinental rail corridor from the Pacific slope to the Mississippi River. We have arrived to inspect your roundhouse facilities and negotiate the procurement of vanadium and molybdenum concentrates."

Clara Thorne pushed up her welding mask, revealing soot-streaked cheeks and piercing, skeptical hazel eyes.

"Procurement?" Clara scoffed, leaning on her pry bar. "Baron Malakor’s scavengers come through here every three weeks with ten-ton steam flatbeds, demanding 'iron tithes' at gunpoint. You look like a clerk who got lost on his way to an accounting office. What could you possibly offer the Scrapper League that we can't dig out of the ground ourselves?"

"Three things, Ms. Thorne," Arthur stated deadpan, counting off on his fingers:

"First: five hundred gallons of refined kerosene and synthetic lubricants from the Wasatch Refining Depot. Second: thirty sacks of high-protein wheat flour and medical antibiotics from Ceres Oasis. And third: the mechanical rehabilitation of your seized one-hundred-and-twenty-foot locomotive turntable, which has sat immobilized by oxidized grease and bearing misalignment since before your grandmother was born."

Clara froze. The scrapper sentries behind her exchanged astonished glances.

"You can fix the big turntable?" Clara demanded, her skepticism wavering. "The turntable has been frozen solid for eighty years! We've hooked three twenty-ton steam winches to it, and all we did was snap the cables. The center bearing is locked tight in sixty tons of hardened slag."

"A cable winch applies eccentric tangential shear against a bound pivot, exacerbating bearing cocking," Arthur replied. "The problem is not lack of horsepower, Ms. Thorne. It is lack of solvent chemistry and proper hydraulic leverage. Take me to the center pivot."

Clara waved her sentries down, gesturing for Arthur, Eleanor, and Kovacs to enter the roundhouse yard.

The central turntable was an immense one-hundred-and-twenty-foot balanced through-plate girder bridge, designed to rotate three-hundred-ton locomotives across twenty-four radial roundhouse service bays. Currently, the massive steel girder sat slightly canted in its circular concrete pit, buried under three feet of frozen mud, crushed limestone, and petrified petroleum grease.

Arthur lowered himself into the central pivot pit, pulling a pocket dental mirror, an ultrasonic thickness gauge, and a scraper from his tool vest.

He cleared away a crust of black, vitrified grease from the master center bearing assembly:

"Center bearing assessment: spherical roller thrust bearing, diameter: thirty-six inches," Arthur announced, checking his ledger. "The rollers are undamaged, but the bearing cavity is completely filled with oxidized calcium-soap grease that has polymerised into a rigid asphaltic mass. Furthermore, the north quadrant support shoe has suffered two point five millimeters of thermal creep, jamming the annular ring."

Arthur turned to Master Kovacs and Clara Thorne.

"We execute a two-phase restoration," Arthur directed:

"Phase One: Solvent liquefaction. We will inject twenty gallons of preheated kerosene from the *Prometheus* tender, combined with five percent butyl cellosolve, into the bearing housing at one hundred and sixty degrees Fahrenheit. The solvent will dissolve the polymerized asphaltic binders in twenty minutes."

"Phase Two: Hydraulic realignment. We will apply the Chimera Frame Mark V to the bound support shoe, exerting twelve thousand pounds of vertical lifting force to reseat the spherical thrust collar."

Clara’s roughnecks quickly brought the heated kerosene lines from the train. 

Arthur supervised the injection. As the hot solvent surged into the frozen bearing cavity, clouds of dark, pungent vapor hissed from the grease seals. Layers of petrified petroleum dissolved into a black, free-flowing liquid that drained into catch buckets.

Arthur stepped to the edge of the north support shoe.

He strapped the **Chimera Frame Mark V** over his shoulders, locking the lower titanium-niobium foot clamps into the concrete foundation.

He aligned the frame’s hydraulic lift piston directly beneath the turntable girder's load-bearing rib.

"Pressure gauge at six thousand PSI," Arthur murmured, placing his hand on the primary control valve. "Initiating hydraulic lift."

*CH-CH-CH-HISSSSS!*

The Mark V’s two-inch-bore piston engaged. 

Twelve thousand pounds of smooth, steady hydraulic thrust surged upward against the massive steel girder. For three seconds, the iron structure groaned under the immense strain.

And then, with an echoing metallic *BOOM* that rang through the roundhouse rafters:

*CRACK-CLACK!*

The frozen spherical bearing snapped free. The turntable settled perfectly level into its lubricated center race, its thirty-six-inch rollers seating with microscopic precision.

Arthur stepped back, releasing the hydraulic pressure.

He gave the massive, sixty-ton steel turntable girder a single, firm push with his left hand.

With smooth, silent, frictionless grace, the one-hundred-and-twenty-foot bridge began to rotate smoothly around its central pivot, gliding across its circular rail ring as if it were floating on air.

The scrapper roughnecks stared in stunned, breathless silence.

Clara Thorne’s jaw dropped. She dropped her six-foot pry bar into the dirt, staring at the rotating turntable bridge, then at Arthur’s clean, unwrinkled suit.

"By the ancestors..." Clara breathed, running her soot-stained fingers through her hair. "You turned sixty tons of dead steel with one hand. Who are you?"

"Arthur Vance, Archivist," Arthur replied, brushing a fleck of grease from his cuff. He pulled his gold pocket watch, clicked it open, and noted the time:

*10:14 AM.*

"Total repair time: one hour and twenty-nine minutes. The turntable is restored to full three-hundred-ton operational capacity. Now, Ms. Thorne, let us discuss the vanadium."

Clara broke into a wide, radiant smile. "Come into the smelting vault, Archivist. We'll give you all the vanadium you can carry."

Inside the vaulted stone cellar beneath the roundhouse machine shops, Clara led them into a secure cavern stacked high with wooden crates.

Inside the crates rested hundreds of ingots of heavy, dark-gray metal:

"Seven thousand pounds of ninety-eight-percent pure ferro-vanadium concentrate," Clara explained proudly. "And two thousand pounds of titanium-carbide pellets salvaged from the old drill-bit factory at Deadwood. We’ve had it locked up for forty years, but our old charcoal bloomery couldn't get hot enough to melt it."

Master Joseph Kovacs rubbed his hands together with ferocious blacksmith glee. "Leave the smelting to Vulcan Junction, lass! We brought our portable three-hundred-kilowatt star-iron induction crucible on the flatcar!"

Within two hours, the induction crucible was coupled to the *Prometheus*’s auxiliary turbo-generator.

Under Arthur and Kovacs’ precise metallurgical direction, five hundred pounds of high-grade star-iron scrap were melted down. Arthur introduced calcium carbide and burned lime flux to strip away dissolved sulfur impurities:

$$\text{Desulfurization: } \text{FeS} + \text{CaO} + \text{C} \longrightarrow \text{CaS} + \text{Fe} + \text{CO} \uparrow$$

Once the sulfur content dropped below zero point zero zero five percent, Arthur added precise alloying increments of ferro-vanadium, chromium, and ferrosilicon:

$$\text{Melt Chemistry: } 0.50\% \text{ C}, \ 0.85\% \text{ Mn}, \ 0.95\% \text{ Cr}, \ 0.22\% \text{ V}, \ \text{Balance: High-Purity Star-Iron}$$

The incandescent alloy was poured into permanent steel molds, producing twelve ultra-high-strength forged drive axles and thirty specialized armor-piercing kinetic chisel blanks for the Chimera Frame.

Arthur performed a standard Brinell hardness test on a cooled test coupon using a ten-millimeter tungsten carbide ball under a three-thousand-kilogram load:

"Indentation diameter: two point nine five millimeters," Arthur recorded in his ledger. "Calculated Brinell Hardness: four hundred and twenty-eight HB. Tensile yield strength: one thousand two hundred and sixty megapascals. Charpy V-notch impact energy: forty-two joules at minus forty degrees Fahrenheit. This alloy will punch through four inches of rolled homogeneous armor without micro-fissuring."

While the metal cooled, Eleanor Vance stepped into the scrapper living quarters beneath the machine shop. 

Fifteen elderly retired miners lay shivering on cot beds, afflicted with chronic silicosis and coal pneumoconiosis contracted from decades of dry underground drilling. Eleanor knelt among them, raising both hands to project a gentle, resonant 528 Hz starlight field. The soothing harmonic energy stimulated pulmonary cellular healing, breaking down fibrotic scar tissue and clearing their airways. For the first time in twenty years, the old miners drew deep, clear breaths without coughing blood.

Tears of profound gratitude streamed down Clara Thorne’s face as she watched the elders sit up and embrace their families.

Without a second thought, Clara signed the formal **Black Hills Industrial Annex** in Arthur’s ledger, committing all forty scrappers to Confederation rail service.

At 1:30 PM, Arthur and Kovacs utilized the newly functional turntable to rotate the *Prometheus* and install the first newly forged vanadium-alloyed driving axle into the locomotive’s third wheel set, raising its mechanical fatigue rating by two hundred percent.

"We have our vanadium, our axles, and thirty armor-piercing chisels," Arthur recorded in his ledger, clicking his pencil shut.

He looked eastward toward the Nebraska border, where the Great Plains stretched toward the domain of the Iron Barony.

"Next destination: The North Platte crossing. It is time to meet Baron Malakor's vanguard."
