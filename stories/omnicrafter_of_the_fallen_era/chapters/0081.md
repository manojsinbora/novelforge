# Chapter 81: The Missouri River Survey

The frozen expanse of the Missouri River stretched across the eastern horizon like a continental moat of fractured gray ice and wind-sculpted snowdrifts.

*Day 26 // 8:10 AM.*

At Milepost Four Hundred and Ninety, the advancing vanguard of the Western Confederation crested the rolling loess bluffs of eastern Nebraska. Before them lay the ancient river valley, nearly a mile wide, where the Missouri flowed past the snow-buried skyline of Omaha toward Council Bluffs on the Iowa shore. The ambient temperature was a frigid minus twelve degrees Fahrenheit, with sharp northwesterly gusts carrying the biting scent of frozen mud, river silt, and distant coal smoke.

Arthur Vance stepped down from the forward observation car of the armored train *Prometheus*, his heavy charcoal greatcoat buttoned securely against the bitter wind. In his right hand he carried an ultrasonic acoustic bathymeter and a calibrated steel ice core sampler; in his left vest pocket, his gold pocket watch ticked with steady, unwavering precision.

Beside him walked Eleanor Vance, her Mid-Tier Sovereign starlight radiating in a soft, spherical aura that insulated their footsteps from the biting draft. Captain Rachel Cross and Master Joseph Kovacs followed closely behind, accompanied by four veteran scouts of Company B carrying surveying transits and sounding rods.

"River width at this crossing: four thousand eight hundred and sixty feet," Arthur announced, opening his brass-bound ledger and unclicking his mechanical pencil. "Historically known as the Union Pacific Missouri River Bridge crossing. The pre-Collapse double-track steel truss bridge was dropped into the riverbed during the Collapse wars. Four fallen spans lie submerged beneath forty feet of ice and sediment. Our objective is to determine whether the winter ice pack can support a temporary heavy-duty tactical pontoon rail crossing, or if we must clear the collapsed bridge abutments."

Master Kovacs spat into the snow, eyeing the vast sheet of jagged river ice with a hardened blacksmith’s caution. "Arthur, the *Prometheus* weighs two hundred and ten tons with a full tender of coal and water. You put two hundred tons on river ice, and if that sheet flexes by an inch too much, the whole consist plunges straight to the bottom of the Missouri."

"Which is why we do not guess," Arthur replied deadpan. "We drill, measure, and calculate according to the classical mechanics of elastic plates on an elastic foundation."

Arthur walked down the embankment onto the frozen river shore, stopping fifty feet onto the ice sheet. 

He knelt on one knee, placing the hardened steel tip of the ice core sampler against the surface. With the Chimera Frame Mark V engaged at minimum torque, the rotary spindle bored cleanly through the ice, extracting a three-inch-diameter cylindrical core:

"Ice core evaluation," Arthur stated, inspecting the crystal structure with a pocket magnifying lens. "Clear blue congelation ice exhibiting columnar crystalline grains oriented perpendicular to the freezing interface. Frazil ice and porous snow-slush layers comprise less than eight percent of the total cross-section. Measured ice thickness: thirty-six point four inches (zero point nine two five meters)."

Arthur turned a page in his ledger and inscribed the governing structural equation:

$$\text{Gold's Formula for Ice Bearing Capacity: } P = C \cdot h^2$$

"Where $P$ is the safe allowable static wheel load, $h$ is effective ice thickness in inches, and $C$ is the empirical safety coefficient for high-quality freshwater ice, taken conservatively as fifty pounds per square inch," Arthur explained to Kovacs and Cross.

$$P = 50 \times (36.4)^2 = 50 \times 1,324.96 \approx 66,248 \text{ lbs per axle} \quad (33.12 \text{ tons})$$

"Furthermore, when augmented by our star-iron load-distributing timber mattress—laying twelve-by-twelve-inch Douglas fir timbers transverse to the track centerline spaced at twelve-inch intervals—the effective flexural rigidity increases by a factor of three point eight. The maximum distributed deflection under a moving two-hundred-ton train at four miles per hour is zero point eight five inches, well within the elastic yield limit of thirty-six-inch congelation ice."

Kovacs pulled off his heavy glove and scratched his beard, impressed. "So the river will hold the train? Provided we crawl across like a turtle?"

"The ice sheet will hold," Arthur confirmed, clicking his pencil. "However, structural ice capacity is not our primary obstacle. Observe the eastern channel."

Arthur raised his brass naval telescope, training it across the frozen river toward the Iowa shore.

Two thousand yards away, anchored in an open channel kept liquid by the thermal discharge of an old coal-fired power station, sat two colossal ironclad river gunboats of the Iron Barony of the Rust Belt: the *Iron Wyrm* and the *Leviathan of Gary*.

The vessels were monstrous, improvised river dreadnoughts over two hundred feet in length, their hulls sheathed in three-inch boilerplate armor salvaged from agricultural silos. Mounted on their elevated center-line casemates were revolving turrets housing twin seventy-five-millimeter naval guns. Puffs of black lignite smoke chugged lazily from their armored funnels.

Between the gunboats and the Nebraska shore, patrolling the frozen ice shelf, were four heavy armored steam sledges—tracked combat chassis fitted with forward steering skis and armed with heavy fifty-caliber machine guns.

Captain Cross peered through her binoculars, her jaw tightening. "They've established an interlocking fire zone across the entire river channel. If we try to lay timber mattresses and rails across the ice, those seventy-five-millimeter naval guns will blow the ice sheet to pieces from a mile away."

"Correct," Arthur said. "Baron Malakor’s river fleet relies on artillery interdiction to control the crossing. An assault across an open ice plain against fortified gunboats would result in unacceptable kinetic expenditure and significant friendly casualties."

"Then what is our operational vector, Arthur?" Cross asked.

"Sub-surface hydraulic sabotage," Arthur replied calmly.

Eleanor stepped forward, her eyes catching the pale winter sunlight. "The river power station, Arthur?"

"Precisely," Arthur nodded. "The gunboats maintain an open-water sanctuary by utilizing the cooling water discharge canal of the pre-Collapse Omaha Geothermal and Coal Generating Station. The canal pumps forty thousand gallons per minute of ninety-degree condenser effluent into the river, preventing ice formation in their docking basin. If we infiltrate the generating station's water intake vaults and divert the thermal discharge, the docking basin will flash-freeze at minus twelve degrees within forty minutes."

Arthur turned to Eleanor. "In addition, Eleanor, your Mid-Tier Sovereign starlight can induce accelerated atmospheric condensation and cryogenic freezing. By coupling your resonance with the cold river draft, we can drop the ambient temperature across the gunboats' docking basin to minus forty degrees Fahrenheit."

Eleanor smiled softly, a determined glint in her violet eyes. "The water will freeze around their hulls before they can weigh anchor."

"And once the ironclads are trapped in twelve inches of solid ice," Arthur concluded, "their gun turrets will be immobilized by hull listing, and their boilers will freeze when their intake sea-chests clog with frazil ice. We will capture the river fleet intact without discharging a single artillery shell."

Arthur checked his gold pocket watch.

*8:46 AM.*

"Transit to the Omaha intake pumphouse will require thirty-five minutes on foot through the river bluffs. Captain Cross, deploy your snipers to cover the intake portal. We initiate the hydraulic diversion immediately."

Moving along the frozen drainage culverts of the Omaha river bluffs, Arthur, Eleanor, and Captain Cross reached the subterranean intake complex of the generating station. The massive concrete structure was buried forty feet beneath the frozen riverbank, half-concealed beneath three centuries of alluvial silt and dead cottonwood logs.

Arthur knelt before the reinforced intake hatch, inspecting the corroded brass locking dogs.

He drew a small syringe of penetrant solvent from his tool harness, applying it to the threads. He engaged the **Chimera Frame Mark V**, locking the pneumatic jaws onto the master valve wheel.

*CLANK-CREAK!*

With a steady application of three hundred foot-pounds of torque, the ancient locking wheel turned. The heavy submarine hatch parted with a hollow sucking sound of escaping stagnant air, revealing a spiraling iron staircase descending into the dark subterranean pumphouse.

Inside the cavernous pump gallery, three colossal five-hundred-horsepower centrifugal pumps were roaring, their electric motors powered by deep geothermal thermopiles tapping the river fault lines. A massive six-foot-diameter cast-iron butterfly sluice valve directed forty thousand gallons of boiling thermal effluent through the eastern discharge flume, keeping Baron Malakor's gunboat basin ice-free.

Arthur stepped directly to the master sluice gate actuator.

"Sluice gate valve topology: six-foot gear-driven butterfly valve," Arthur analyzed, examining the brass gear teeth with his dental mirror. "To divert the thermal effluent away from the gunboat basin and route it into the deep southern alluvial drainage canal, the valve sector must be rotated through ninety degrees."

Arthur aligned the Chimera Frame’s vanadium-tipped drive adapter into the manual override pinion socket.

He set the accumulator throttle to three thousand PSI.

*CH-CH-CH-CH-WHIRRRRRR!*

The hydraulic drive engaged. The massive six-foot butterfly disc began to turn smoothly within its cast-iron casing, rotating against thousands of gallons of churning thermal water.

*THUD!*

The valve seated with a resounding hydraulic shock. 

The roaring torrent of ninety-degree condenser water was instantly cut off from the eastern discharge canal. Deprived of the thermal plume, the water in Baron Malakor’s gunboat basin was immediately exposed to the brutal, sub-zero river currents flowing at minus twelve degrees Fahrenheit.

Arthur turned to Eleanor.

"The hydraulic conduit is closed," Arthur said. "Eleanor, initiate the cryogenic thermal harvest."

Eleanor stepped out onto the concrete parapet overlooking the river basin. 

She closed her eyes, inhaling the freezing draft, and raised both arms toward the two anchored ironclad gunboats five hundred yards away.

*SHUUUUUU-VOOOOOOOM!*

A magnificent, cascading wave of 528 Hz starlight rolled across the open water of the docking basin.

Rather than radiating heat, Eleanor modulated the Sovereign field to extract kinetic enthalpy from the liquid surface, accelerating the rate of nucleation across the water molecules:

$$q = \dot{m} \cdot C_p \cdot \Delta T + \dot{m} \cdot L_f$$

Where $L_f = 334 \text{ kJ/kg}$ is the latent heat of fusion for water.

Under the irresistible pull of the starlight field, trillions of joules of thermal energy were sucked out of the water in seconds, radiating away as harmless shimmering auroral luminescence into the winter sky.

The surface of the docking basin flashed into solid ice with the sound of a thousand cracking whips:

*SNAP-CRACK-BOOM!*

Sheets of black river ice formed at the staggering rate of four inches per minute. 

Aboard the ironclad *Iron Wyrm*, the warning sirens began to wail in frantic, hysterical terror:

"The river's freezing! Weigh anchor! Reverse engines!" the gunboat captain screamed from the armored pilot house.

It was already too late.

The massive four-inch ice sheets gripped the ironclad’s three-inch boilerplate hulls in an immovable, crushing vice. The ship’s twin screw propellers struck solid blocks of ice, their drive shafts shearing with horrific metallic screeching:

*SCREEECH-BANG!*

Simultaneously, thick frazil ice slush was drawn into the gunboats' sea-chest cooling strainers, choking their condenser pumps and starving their steam boilers of feedwater. The boilers hissed wildly as safety valves popped, killing all engine power.

The two hundred-foot warships tilted four degrees to the port side, permanently locked in six feet of solid, impenetrable river ice, their heavy seventy-five-millimeter gun turrets listing uselessly toward the cloudy sky.

On the ice shelf, the four armored steam sledges attempted to turn back toward the Iowa shore, but Captain Cross’s snipers opened fire from the bluffs:

*PHUT! PHUT! PHUT! PHUT!*

Four precision shots punched through their un-armored fuel vaporizers. The sledges sputtered, coughed, and ground to an inert halt on the ice.

Within fifteen minutes, white parley flags fluttered from the masts of both captured ironclads.

One hundred and twenty shivering Barony sailors emerged onto the icy decks with hands raised high in unconditional surrender, staring in utter disbelief at the golden-lit girl and the calm archivist who had frozen an entire war fleet in place without firing a single cannon.

Arthur Vance walked calmly down onto the frozen river surface, his boots crunching lightly on the ice. He pulled his gold pocket watch, clicked the lid open, and checked the second hand:

*9:38:20 AM.*

"Total elapsed operation time: fifty-two minutes and twenty seconds," Arthur recorded in his ledger with his mechanical pencil. "Hostile river fleet neutralized. Zero casualties sustained. Two ironclad gunboats and four steam sledges captured in operable mechanical condition."

Arthur turned to Master Kovacs, who was already leading a work party of sixty Confederation soldiers and Shoshone roughnecks down to the riverbank with flatbed wagons loaded with twelve-by-twelve-inch timber beams and star-iron rails.

"Master Kovacs," Arthur directed. "Deploy the load-distributing timber mattress. We cross the Missouri River now."

With clockwork, rhythmic precision, Kovacs’ crew laid the timber beams across the frozen Missouri, spiking forty-foot sections of one-hundred-and-thirty-six-pound star-iron rails directly onto the wood mattress.

At 11:15 AM, the two-hundred-ton armored train *Prometheus* sounded its magnificent whistle and rolled out onto the frozen river.

The thirty-six-inch ice sheet bore the immense load without a single crack. The *Prometheus* crawled across the four thousand eight hundred feet of ice at a steady four miles per hour, its wheels singing upon the star-iron rails until the massive locomotive reached the eastern shore, rolling triumphantly into Council Bluffs on the Iowa side.

The Missouri River was conquered.

Arthur closed his ledger, snapped his pocket watch shut, and looked eastward across the snow-covered fields of Iowa.

"The river boundary is breached," Arthur said calmly. "Next objective: The pre-Collapse archives and seed vaults of Omaha."
