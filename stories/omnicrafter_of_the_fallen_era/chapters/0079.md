# Chapter 79: First Contact with the Iron Barony

The flat, snow-draped plains of western Nebraska stretched toward a low horizon dominated by the ancient sandstone monolith of Chimney Rock.

*Day 24 // 11:20 AM.*

At Milepost Two Hundred and Eighty along the North Platte riverbed, where the abandoned Union Pacific mainline cut through the snowy sagebrush, the advance of the armored train *Prometheus* came to an abrupt halt.

Straddling the railroad right-of-way five hundred yards ahead was a heavily fortified roadblock.

Three massive, smoke-belching armored war machines blocked the rails. Known across the plains as **Rust-Stalkers**, these fifteen-ton combat scout vehicles were built upon lengthened pre-Collapse mining truck chassis. They were powered by crude, roaring two-cylinder steam engines burning unrefined lignite coal, sheathed in three layers of riveted boiler-plate steel armor, and topped with revolving gun turrets armed with twin twenty-millimeter autocannons. Flanking the vehicles in the frozen ditches were forty heavily armed raiders clad in spiked scrap cuirasses and fur hoods, brandishing heavy automatic shotguns and crude pneumatic harpoons.

Arthur Vance stood on the open forward gangway of the locomotive pilot car.

He raised his brass naval telescope, methodically surveying the hostile formation from turret to track bed. Beside him, Captain Rachel Cross stood with her hand resting on the receiver of her scoped designated marksman rifle, while Eleanor watched with a look of calm, sorrowful contemplation.

"Visual and mechanical analysis of the hostile blockade," Arthur reported quietly to his pocket ledger, clicking his mechanical pencil. "Three armored steam technicals. Boiler configuration: horizontal locomotive-type, estimated operating pressure: one hundred and eighty PSI. Hull plating: three-quarter-inch mild scrap steel fastened with structural rivets showing severe shear stress and cold embrittlement."

"They're waving a parley banner," Captain Cross observed, pointing toward a jagged piece of red sheet metal hoisted on a star-iron pipe above the lead vehicle. "Someone's stepping onto the tracks."

A tall, broad-shouldered man in a scorched leather duster and an articulated iron breastplate stepped out from behind the lead Rust-Stalker. He walked with heavy, arrogant strides to the center of the cross-ties, resting an antique nickel-plated revolver against his hip. Across his chest hung a necklace made of severed copper telegraph wire and brass shell casings.

Arthur Vance stepped down from the pilot car, followed by Eleanor and Captain Cross.

Arthur walked with even, measured paces until he stood exactly twenty paces from the warlord. His charcoal-wool three-piece suit was crisp and clean; his left thumb was hooked comfortably into his brass pocket watch chain. Over his right shoulder, the **Chimera Frame Mark V** hummed with quiet, steady hydraulic readiness, its newly fitted vanadium-alloyed PDC diamond chisel gleaming in the winter sun.

The warlord spat a stream of dark tobacco juice onto the snow, sizing up Arthur with a sneer of contempt.

"I am **Commander Vane Kroll**," the man declared, his voice rough and abrasive like gravel grinding in a drum. "First Centurion of the **Iron Barony of the Rust Belt**, loyal servant of Baron Malakor. You’re trespassing on the sovereign territory of the Great River, stranger."

"The territory through which this track runs is sovereign public right-of-way established under the Continental Commons protocol of the Western Confederation," Arthur replied evenly. "I am Arthur Vance, Chief Archivist. What is the purpose of your obstruction?"

Kroll laughed—a harsh, barking sound echoed by his scavengers in the trenches.

"Chief Archivist? You brought a schoolmaster to fight a war!" Kroll sneered. "Listen to me, bookkeeper. Baron Malakor owns every scrap of iron, every drop of oil, and every bushel of grain between the Missouri River and the foothills. By decree of the **Iron Tithe**, you have two choices: surrender that magnificent silver locomotive, uncouple your fuel tank cars, and surrender your tools to the Barony, or we will blast you off the roadbed and use your skulls as boiler ornaments."

Arthur Vance listened to the entire speech without blinking.

He reached into his waistcoat, withdrew his gold pocket watch, clicked the hunter case open, and noted the time:

*11:27:15 AM.*

"Commander Kroll," Arthur began, his voice flat, analytical, and devoid of the slightest trace of emotion. "Your ultimatum is rejected on four distinct technical and thermodynamic grounds."

Kroll blinked, his sneer faltering. "What?"

"First," Arthur said, taking a step forward, "your three Rust-Stalker combat vehicles suffer from catastrophic mechanical vulnerabilities. Your horizontal fire-tube boilers are constructed from non-normalized scrap boiler-plate with a yield strength not exceeding two hundred megapascals. At an operating pressure of one hundred and eighty PSI, your hoop stress is approximately twenty-four thousand pounds per square inch, leaving an operational safety factor of barely one point one five. A single localized pressure spike or temperature excursion will trigger explosive catastrophic shell rupture."

Kroll's eyes narrowed in confusion and rising anger. "You talk a lot of nonsense, clerk—"

"Second," Arthur continued relentlessly, "your twin twenty-millimeter autocannons utilize black powder propellant charges. The burn rate of coarse black powder in a rifled twenty-millimeter barrel yields a muzzle velocity of scarcely three hundred and eighty meters per second, with severe barrel fouling after five consecutive discharges. Against our star-iron composite carriage armor, your projectiles possess insufficient kinetic energy to achieve penetration, yielding a zero-percent ballistic defeat probability."

Kroll’s face flushed dark crimson with rage. "You arrogant little bastard!"

"Third," Arthur said, raising a single finger, "the socio-economic model of your Iron Barony is fundamentally non-viable. Extorting fifty percent of regional agricultural output while destroying rural transportation networks induces an exponential decay in total caloric availability. According to our actuarial models, your Barony’s food supply will collapse completely within fourteen to eighteen months, forcing your armed units into cannibalism or internal mutiny."

Arthur snapped his pocket watch shut.

"And fourth: you are currently outmatched by several orders of magnitude. You have forty-five seconds to uncouple your blockade, clear the right-of-way, and withdraw to a distance of five miles."

Kroll trembled with explosive, unbridled fury. He drew his nickel-plated revolver and pointed it directly at Arthur’s forehead.

"Kill them all!" Kroll screamed. "Fire the guns!"

Aboard the lead Rust-Stalker, the gunner slammed the electric firing switches on the twin twenty-millimeter autocannons:

*THUMP-THUMP-THUMP-THUMP!*

A salvo of eight heavy twenty-millimeter steel slugs erupted from the gun barrels in a cloud of thick white smoke, screaming toward Arthur and Eleanor at three hundred and eighty meters per second.

Arthur Vance did not duck. He did not blink.

Beside him, Eleanor stepped smoothly into the center of the line.

She raised her right hand, palm open toward the oncoming barrage.

*SHUUUUU-VOOOOOM!*

Her Mid-Tier Sovereign starlight erupted in a shimmering, semi-transparent golden shield.

When the heavy twenty-millimeter steel slugs struck the starlight field, they did not shatter or ricochet. 

Instead, the coherent 528 Hz standing wave extracted their kinetic energy instantaneously through quantum acoustic damping:

$$E_k = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2} (0.13 \text{ kg}) (380 \text{ m/s})^2 \approx 9,386 \text{ Joules per projectile}$$

All eight rounds decelerated from twelve hundred feet per second to zero in less than four inches of flight. Deprived of kinetic momentum, the heavy steel slugs dropped straight down into the snow at Eleanor’s feet with soft, harmless *plops*.

The gunners in the turrets froze in sheer, paralyzed terror.

"Now," Arthur said calmly, "I shall demonstrate boiler depressurization."

Arthur lunged forward.

The **Chimera Frame Mark V** engaged with explosive hydraulic torque. The titanium-niobium leg struts propelled Arthur across the twenty paces of snow in less than zero point six seconds.

Kroll fired three shots from his revolver at point-blank range. 

The bullets struck the Sovereign Aegis buckler on Arthur’s left forearm with crisp metallic *tings*, deflecting uselessly into the sky.

Arthur bypassed Kroll entirely, slipping past the warlord’s shoulder and leaping eight feet into the air directly onto the hood of the lead Rust-Stalker.

His boots locked onto the rusted boilerplate casing. His right arm snapped forward, aligning the newly forged vanadium-alloyed PDC diamond chisel directly against the vehicle's primary boiler check-valve housing.

"Checking safety relief geometry," Arthur announced aloud.

He pulled the pneumatic firing trigger.

*KABOOOOM!*

The Closed-Loop Accumulator discharged at six thousand PSI.

The vanadium-hardened diamond chisel punched through the two-inch cast-iron check-valve housing with nineteen thousand pounds of kinetic force. The strike was calibrated with microscopic precision: the chisel did not breach the main steam drum, but sheared the primary valve stem, forcing the spring-loaded safety relief stack permanently wide open.

*FSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSHHHHHHHHHHHH!*

A colossal, deafening geyser of high-pressure white steam erupted fifty feet into the winter sky from the relief stack. 

The boiler pressure inside the lead Rust-Stalker collapsed from one hundred and eighty PSI to zero in less than twelve seconds. Deprived of steam pressure, the massive two-cylinder engine ground to a shuddering, impotent halt, coughing a final puff of gray smoke before dying completely.

Arthur leaped down from the dead war machine, landing lightly on the snow directly behind Commander Kroll.

Before Kroll could turn around, Arthur’s left hand shot out. 

The Chimera Frame’s pneumatic grip clamped onto Kroll’s iron collar with two hundred pounds of pressure, lifting the two-hundred-and-twenty-pound warlord six inches off the ground and slamming him against the rusted track of his own vehicle.

*THUD!*

Kroll dangled helplessly, his boots kicking at the air, his eyes bulging in pure, unadulterated terror as he stared into Arthur’s calm, unblinking gray eyes.

The forty raiders in the trenches threw their shotguns into the snow and raised their hands in unconditional surrender.

"Commander Kroll," Arthur Vance said, his voice quiet, calm, and perfectly steady. "Your machine is depressurized. Your weapons are ineffective. Your command is broken."

Arthur pulled a folded parchment envelope from his greatcoat pocket with his free right hand and tucked it into Kroll’s breastplate harness.

"Take this dispatch to Baron Malakor," Arthur instructed evenly. "It contains the formal **Ultimatum of the Western Confederation**. It outlines our terms: the immediate cessation of the Iron Tithe, the complete demobilization of your armed river gunboats, and the integration of the Great Plains into the standard-gauge railway network."

Arthur lowered Kroll back to the ground, releasing his collar with a soft hiss of pneumatic pressure.

"Tell the Baron that if his Colossus of Gary attempts to contest this line, it will suffer precisely the same fate as your check valve. He has five days to prepare his reply."

Kroll scrambled backward into the snow on his hands and knees, clutching his bruised throat and gasping for breath.

"You're... you're him..." Kroll stammered, his voice trembling in horror. "The Ghost Artificer... the demon from the mountains..."

"I am an Archivist, Commander Kroll," Arthur replied deadpan, turning his back on the warlord and walking calmly toward the *Prometheus*. "And I strongly advise you to clear this roadblock within fifteen minutes. The train does not like to wait."

Captain Cross stepped forward with her fire team, systematically disarming the remaining two Rust-Stalkers by disconnecting their distributor linkages and siphoning their auxiliary fuel tanks into Confederation barrels. 

Under the cold, watchful gaze of Company B's marksmen, the forty surrendered raiders hastily attached heavy tow cables to the two functioning steam vehicles and dragged the disabled lead technical off the tracks into the prairie ditch.

Arthur returned to the pilot car, unbuckling the Chimera Frame's telemetry collar and pulling his brass ledger from his pocket.

With calm, rhythmic strokes of his mechanical pencil, he recorded the incident on page seventy-two:

*Incident Log: Milepost 280 (Chimney Rock Sector).*  
*Hostile Encounter: Iron Barony Reconnaissance Detachment (Commander Vane Kroll).*  
*Hostile Casualties: 0 Fatalities; 1 Lead Combat Vehicle Depressurized.*  
*Friendly Casualties: 0.*  
*Ammunition Consumed: 0 Rifle Cartridges; 1 Vanadium Chisel Pneumatic Stroke.*  
*Diplomatic Status: Confederation Ultimatum Dispatched to Baron Malakor.*

Eleanor Vance stood beside him on the gangway, looking back at the retreating raiders who were stumbling through the snow toward the east.

"You broke their will without drawing a drop of blood, Arthur," Eleanor whispered softly, her violet eyes shining. "They looked at you as if you were an ancient titan who walked out of the earth."

"A common human cognitive bias known as the halo effect of technological superiority," Arthur noted, checking his pocket watch.

*11:48 AM.*

"When an adversary observes macroscopic physical laws being manipulated with microscopic precision, they attribute the result to the supernatural. In reality, it is merely the difference between scrap-iron blacksmithing and First Dynasty thermodynamic discipline."

Arthur looked ahead down the clear, unbroken tracks stretching across the Nebraska prairie.

"Captain Cross, blow the whistle. All clear on the main line. We proceed at twenty-five miles per hour toward the North Platte trestle."
