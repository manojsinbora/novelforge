# Chapter 9: The Quarry Ambush

The morning sun over Blackwood Ridge was cold, pale, and blinding.

It crested the eastern granite cliffs at exactly seven-fifty-two, casting long, sharp shadows across the limestone quarry floor eighty feet below. Down in the turnaround, the fourteen men of Ironfang Cole’s syndicate stood in a tight semicircle around their three armored technicals, their rifles aimed at the thirty-ton steel blast doors of Bunker Zero-Seven.

None of them looked up.

Human beings, as Arthur Vance had noted in multiple historical treatises on trench warfare and siege tactics, possessed an innate psychological bias: when confronted with a fortress, they assumed the threat was enclosed entirely behind the primary gate.

Arthur stepped out through the narrow, camouflaged emergency escape hatch onto a three-foot granite ledge cut directly into the vertical cliff face. 

The air up here was crisp and still. The morning breeze carried the faint, sweet scent of mountain pine needles mixed with the bitter exhaust fumes of the syndicate’s idling V8 engines below.

Arthur adjusted his wire-rimmed spectacles, pulled the leather cinches of the Chimera Frame tight against his right forearm, and verified his equipment:

*Pneumatic pressure:* eighteen hundred PSI. Gauge needle pinned against the red limit line.  
*Capacitor reserve:* four nickel-iron cells fully saturated at twenty-five thousand volts.  
*Physical condition:* resting heart rate eighty-two beats per minute; zero musculoskeletal fatigue.

Down in the quarry, Vance "Grip" Rogan raised the battery-powered bullhorn to his mouth once more.

"TWO MINUTES LEFT, ARCHIVIST!" Rogan’s amplified voice echoed against the rock walls. "YOU HEAR ME IN THERE? FIVE SECONDS AFTER THAT TIMER DIES, MY BOYS LIGHT THE DYNAMITE! OPEN THE DOORS OR SUFFOCATE IN YOUR OWN COLD IRON!"

Beside the lead truck, two enforcers in grease-stained denim jackets were already kneeling in the gravel, unrolling two fifty-foot coils of black waterproof safety fuse attached to a wooden crate marked **DANGER: 60% EXTRA DYNAMITE**.

Beside them, mounted in the bed of the lead four-wheel-drive pickup, the gunner in the spiked football helmet sat behind the twin spade grips of a heavy Soviet-pattern DShK 12.7-millimeter machine gun. The gunner was chewing on a toothpick, scanning the seams of the bunker doors with bored, lazy confidence.

"Distance to truck bed: seventy-four feet," Arthur calculated under his breath, his eyes tracing the natural fault lines in the granite shelf. "Angle of descent: sixty-two degrees. Talus scree slope on the north flank provides twenty feet of sound-dampening gravel."

He reached to his waist, uncoiled twelve feet of high-tensile aircraft cable, and snapped the forged carabiner around a weathered iron mooring ring embedded in the ledge.

He didn't make a sound. He didn't issue a warning.

Arthur dropped over the lip of the shelf.

The descent took two point four seconds. 

He slid down the steep granite face on the rubber heels of his work boots, using the cable to control his velocity, and kicked off the final rock face directly behind the lead technical.

He landed on the steel diamond-plate bed of the truck with a muffled, dull *thud*.

The machine gunner’s toothpick twitched. The man began to turn his head to the left, his hands tightening on the DShK’s spade grips—

Arthur’s left hand shot out like an iron vise, clamping over the man’s throat and pinning his vocal cords against his cervical spine.

Simultaneously, Arthur brought his right arm forward.

*PSSSHT-CLANG!*

The pneumatic cylinder of the Chimera Striker fired forward two inches with four hundred pounds of linear thrust. The tool-steel jaws, tipped with the diamond-hard Bone-Tail talons, struck the steel feed-tray cover of the heavy machine gun.

*CRUNCH.*

The half-inch hardened steel talons sheared through the cast-iron feed pawl, crushing the heavy steel linkage like dry kindling. In the same fluid motion, Arthur depressed the thumb switch on his palm.

*SNAP-ZAP.*

Twenty-five thousand volts bridged across the gunner’s neck through the silver-brazed jaw.

The gunner didn't cry out. He gave a single, rigid shudder as every voluntary motor neuron in his cerebral cortex overloaded simultaneously. His eyes rolled into his head, his hands dropped limp from the spade grips, and he slumped forward across the ruined machine gun, thoroughly and cleanly unconscious.

"Heavy ballistic threat: neutralized," Arthur whispered, already vaulting over the truck’s tailgate into the gravel. "Elapsed time: three point one seconds."

The two men by the dynamite crate heard the thump of the tailgate.

They whipped around, their eyes going wide as they saw the solitary figure in the soot-stained gray smock and wire-rimmed glasses standing three feet behind them, an alien bracer of dark gray monster-chitin glinting on his right arm.

"What the—" one enforcer choked out, reaching for a sawed-off double-barrel shotgun resting against the wooden dynamite crate.

Arthur took a single, explosive stride forward.

He didn't draw a blade. He brought the flat, twenty-millimeter chitin plate of the Chimera Frame across the shotgun’s double barrels with a sharp horizontal parry.

*CLANG.*

The heavy steel barrels were batted aside with contemptuous ease. 

Before the man could recover his balance, Arthur drove his left heel down against the enforcer’s instep, fracturing two metatarsals with a crisp *snap*, and hooked his right palm behind the man’s collarbone.

A two-tenth-second pulse of five thousand volts surged through the collarbone into the brachial plexus.

The enforcer dropped like an empty sack of flour.

The second dynamite handler shrieked in terror, dropping his lighter into the dirt, and scrambled backward on his hands and knees.

"ALARM!" the man screamed, his voice cracking into a ragged wail. "HE'S BEHIND US! THE GHOST IS BEHIND US!"

In the center of the quarry, Vance "Grip" Rogan spun around, nearly dropping his bullhorn.

The remaining eleven syndicate enforcers froze in utter disbelief. They had expected a squad of desperate National Guard soldiers or trembling civilians to emerge from the vault. 

Instead, standing amidst their shattered machine gun and unconscious gunners, was a single, slight young man in an archivist's smock, casually dusting a flake of gray chalk from his wool waistcoat.

"Shoot him, you idiots!" Rogan bellowed, his scarred face turning the color of an overripe plum. "BLOW HIM TO PIECES!"

Four rifles raised simultaneously.

Arthur didn't stand in the open. 

He grabbed the nearest wooden crate of dynamite by its hemp rope handle, lifted the forty-pound box with the pneumatic leverage of the Chimera Frame, and threw it with pinpoint accuracy directly beneath the radiator of the second technical truck.

*THUD.*

"Rifles at twenty yards," Arthur called out, his voice ringing across the quarry with terrifying, clinical calm. "The wooden crate contains sixty percent nitroglycerin dynamite sticks. Nitroglycerin decomposes into volatile liquid esters at ambient temperatures. If a copper-jacketed bullet strikes that case, the detonation velocity will reach twenty-four thousand feet per second."

The four riflemen froze. Their fingers hovered over their triggers, their eyes widening in sudden, paralyzing horror as they realized their line of fire crossed within inches of forty pounds of high explosive resting under their own fuel tank.

"Hold your fire! HOLD YOUR FIRE!" one rifleman screamed, desperately shoving his comrade's rifle barrel toward the sky. "It'll blow the whole mountain!"

"Cowards!" Rogan roared.

The massive syndicate captain ripped off his fleece-lined jacket, revealing a torso as broad as a beer keg. His forearms, bare and glistening in the morning light, were horrifying to behold: thick, overlapping plates of jagged black keratin scales covered the skin from his knuckles to his biceps—the grotesque mark of an early-stage mutant brawler who had bathed in the toxic subterranean miasma.

In his right hand, Rogan hefted his signature weapon: a modified, five-foot hydraulic rescue spreader—the heavy, engine-driven "jaws of life" used by firefighters to tear open crushed automobiles. 

Rogan had welded hardened triangular steel teeth to the scissor tips and replaced the pneumatic pump with a roaring, smoke-belching two-stroke chainsaw engine strapped to his back.

*BRRRRRRR-WHEEEEEEE!*

The engine screamed. The hydraulic scissor tips expanded with five tons of crushing force.

"You like tools, boy?" Rogan snarled, taking heavy, thudding strides toward Arthur, the veins on his scarred neck bulging with thick, black mutant blood. "I tore an armored personnel carrier in half with this rig! Let's see how that little arm brace holds up when I peel your ribcage like a melon!"

Rogan charged.

He moved with terrifying speed for a man of three hundred pounds, propelled by mutated muscle density and raw, adrenaline-fueled sadism. 

At ten feet, Rogan swung the five-foot hydraulic jaws in a sweeping horizontal arc designed to sever Arthur at the waist.

Arthur didn't leap backward. Retreating gave the attacker momentum.

Arthur stepped *forward*, ducking three inches beneath the roaring scissor arms, and pivoted his body forty-five degrees to the right.

The steel tips of the hydraulic jaws missed his chest by two inches, the wind of their passage whipping his tie across his collar.

"Hydraulic fluid flow rate: two gallons per minute," Arthur analyzed in three tenths of a second. "Operational pressure: ten thousand PSI. Structural pivot: one single cast-steel central hinge bolt."

As Rogan’s immense momentum carried the heavy tool past him, Arthur planted his feet in the gravel, raised his right arm, and fired the Chimera Striker.

*PSSSHT-CRACK!*

Four hundred pounds of pneumatic linear thrust slammed the three diamond-hard Bone-Tail talons directly into the exposed hydraulic line where the high-pressure rubber hose met the steel manifold on Rogan's back.

The talons severed the reinforced steel-braided hose in a clean, surgical slice.

*FSSSSSSSSSSSH!*

Ten thousand PSI of red hydraulic oil erupted into the air in a blinding, atomized geyser.

The pressure inside the scissor arms vanished instantly. The heavy steel jaws went limp, their crushing power evaporating in a fraction of a second.

Rogan stumbled forward, throwing his weight off balance, his scarred face drenched in spraying red oil. "My rig! You broke my—"

Arthur didn't give him time to finish.

He closed the distance, stepped inside Rogan's guard, and brought the palm of the Chimera Frame flat against the center of Rogan's chest.

"Anatomical evaluation: advanced keratin dermal thickening," Arthur said evenly. "Insulative resistance to kinetic trauma: high. Electrical resistance of subcutaneous cardiac nerve pathways: unchanged."

Arthur depressed the red palm switch and held it down for a full two seconds.

*BZZZZZZZZZZZT!*

Twenty-five thousand volts of pure celestial static poured into Rogan’s chest in a blinding, sustained discharge.

A brilliant blue flash illuminated the entire quarry floor, reflecting off the granite cliffs like a bolt of daylight lightning.

Rogan’s mouth flew open. His eyes went completely white. Every tendon in his massive three-hundred-pound frame locked into an agonizing, rigid arch. His feet left the gravel by two inches as his central nervous system collapsed under the massive voltage overload.

Arthur released the switch.

*THUD.*

Vance "Grip" Rogan crashed face-first into the gravel, his arms and legs twitching once before going completely, beautifully still.

Silence fell over the quarry.

The two-stroke chainsaw engine on Rogan's back gave a final, coughing sputter and died, leaving only the sound of hot exhaust ticking in the cold mountain air.

Arthur lowered his right arm, flicked a droplet of red hydraulic fluid from his spectacles with his left cuff, and clicked open his brass pocket watch.

*7:58 AM.*

"Total elapsed time: five minutes, forty-two seconds," Arthur stated calmly. "Caloric expenditure: sixty-eight calories."

He turned slowly to face the surviving nine syndicate enforcers.

The men were frozen. Their rifles hung limp in their trembling hands.

They looked at their twelve-gauge machine gun, crushed and smoking on the truck bed. They looked at their four-hundred-pound champion, comatose in a pool of his own hydraulic oil. And they looked at Arthur Vance—standing alone in his neat gray smock and wire-rimmed glasses, an alien gauntlet of dark chitin smoking with blue static on his arm.

To these men, he was no longer an archivist.

He was the terrifying, unkillable demon of the mountain—**The Ghost Artificer**, a master of lightning and impossible steel who broke weapons with his bare hands and struck down giants without breaking a sweat.

"D-Drop 'em..." one rifleman whimpered, throwing his scoped rifle into the dirt. "Drop the guns! RUN!"

The enforcers didn't hesitate for another second.

They hurled their rifles, shotguns, and machetes into the gravel, scrambled into the third technical truck like terrified children, and jammed the transmission into reverse. The tires shrieked against the stone, throwing plumes of gray dust into the air as the truck whipped around and fled down the mountain access road as fast as its cylinders could carry it.

Arthur didn't bother watching them go. 

He walked over to the wooden dynamite crate, carefully unclipped the safety fuses, and stacked the sixty-percent nitroglycerin sticks neatly into an empty canvas toolbag.

Behind him, with a deep, pneumatic groan of equalizing nitrogen pressure, the thirty-ton steel blast doors of Bunker Zero-Seven swung open.

Dr. Nicholas Ward, Miller, and Davis stepped out into the morning sunlight, carrying their shotguns and revolvers.

They stopped dead in their tracks, their jaws hanging open as they surveyed the quarry:

Two heavy four-wheel-drive trucks captured intact.  
One hundred gallons of clean diesel fuel in the auxiliary tanks.  
A pile of abandoned rifles, shotguns, and ammunition.  
And Vance Rogan—the most feared enforcer in the Oakhaven basin—lying face-down in the gravel, snoring softly like a tranquilized bear.

Miller walked up beside Arthur, stared down at Rogan’s comatose form, and then looked at the smoking chitin bracer on Arthur’s arm.

"Arthur..." Miller croaked, his voice cracking with awe. "What... what did you do to them?"

Arthur picked up his canvas toolbag, slung it over his shoulder, and checked his pocket watch one final time.

"Routine municipal maintenance," Arthur said, adjusting his spectacles. "We had an unscheduled obstruction in the turnaround. I cleared it."

He looked back toward the open vault doors where fifty-five survivors were watching from the safety of the entry airlock.

"Miller, grab the siphoning hoses. We have eighty gallons of clean fuel to transfer to the backup generators."

Arthur turned on his heel, his boots crunching rhythmically across the quarry stone, and led the way back into the mountain.
