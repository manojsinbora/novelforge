# Chapter 49: The Promontory Point Bastion

The Great Salt Lake stretched toward the northern horizon like an immense sheet of beaten lead beneath the bruised twilight sky.

*Day 10 // 5:15 PM.*

From the crest of a craggy limestone ridge twelve miles west of the lake's shores, Arthur Vance adjusted the brass vernier knobs of a high-precision pre-Collapse surveying theodolite mounted on a heavy ash-wood tripod.

Through the crosshairs of the thirty-power optical telescope, the industrial heart of the eastern wasteland came into razor-sharp focus: **The Promontory Point Bastion**.

It was a monstrous amalgamation of heavy pre-Collapse petrochemical infrastructure, historic transcontinental rail yards, and brutalist warlord fortifications. 

Spread across five hundred acres of the lake's southern headland stood the towering silhouette of the **Wasatch Petrochemical Complex**: six three-hundred-foot fractional distillation columns, twin spherical butane storage spheres wrapped in rusted structural steel girders, an automated catalytic reforming unit, and a vast labyrinth of pressurized piping manifolds and overhead high-pressure steam conduits. Plumes of sour flare-gas burned bright orange against the darkening sky, casting long, wavering shadows across the concrete aprons and gravel rail sidings.

Surrounding the refinery was a fortress of terrifying scale. 

The historic Union Pacific marshalling yard had been transformed into a walled citadel. Two-foot-thick reinforced concrete blast walls, topped with double-apron razor wire and electrified star-iron mesh, enclosed the perimeter. Mounted on reinforced railroad turntables at the eastern and western bastions were two colossal pre-Collapse M1918 155-millimeter coastal artillery rifles, their long, rifled barrels pointed ominously toward the western mountain approaches.

"Good God," Captain Rachel Cross breathed, peering through her rubber-armored field binoculars beside Arthur. "Look at those gun mounts. If Cole spots *The Iron Vanguard* on the open flats, a single high-explosive ninety-five-pound shell from those 155s will turn our locomotive into scrap metal before we get within three miles."

"The M1918 155-millimeter gun utilizes a manual Welin interrupted-screw breech and separate-loading bagged nitrocellulose propellant charges," Arthur Vance stated with unruffled clinical detachment, his number-two drafting pencil scribbling ballistics data onto his grid-lined plotting pad. "Maximum effective range with standard high-explosive projectile: nineteen thousand yards. Muzzle velocity: two thousand four hundred feet per second. Rate of fire: two rounds per minute per gun."

Arthur adjusted the theodolite's horizontal angle with a gentle twist of his thumb:

"However, the turntable mounts are mechanically linked to pre-Collapse steam-donkey winches powered by coal-fired fire-tube boilers. Azimuth traversal speed is approximately one point two degrees per second. If our vector of advance remains tangential to their angular tracking limit, their traverse mechanism cannot maintain continuous fire solutions on a target moving faster than twenty-five miles per hour. Furthermore, their minimum elevation angle is limited to five degrees above the horizontal, creating a permanent dead zone of eight hundred yards immediately beneath the promontory bluffs."

Beside them, Sergeant Major Bradley lowered his binoculars, spitting a stream of dark tobacco juice into the dry sagebrush.

"It's not just the big coastal guns, Arthur," Bradley grunted, pointing toward the forward gorge two miles ahead of their position. "Look at the choke point. That's Pumping Station Four."

Where the canyon walls narrowed into a sheer limestone notch barely eighty feet wide, Pumping Station Four stood like a jagged medieval tollgate.

The pumping station itself was a heavy reinforced-concrete bunker housing the primary centrifugal crude pumps that pulled petroleum from the subterranean pipeline wells. Bolted directly into the canyon's vertical limestone cliffs were two massive, four-inch-thick anchor plates of rolled carbon steel. Stretched between them, suspended three feet above the rail ties, were the two braided steel recovery cables Raider Captain Vane had described—three inches in diameter, drawn taut with heavy hydraulic turnbuckles.

Behind the cables, mounted on sandbag parapets atop the pump house roof, sat two 37-millimeter M3 anti-tank guns and a fortified squad of thirty mutant raiders armed with automatic weapons.

"The cables are anchored at seventy tons of static tension," Arthur observed, checking the cable sag through the theodolite reticle. "If *The Iron Vanguard* strikes three-inch high-carbon plow steel at forty miles per hour, the kinetic collision will not merely deflect; the cable will ride up the wedge of the biogenic plow and shear the locomotive's cab structure clean off the frame, decapitating the operating crew."

"Can we blow the anchor plates with satchel charges?" Cross asked, tapping the explosive pouches on her tactical harness.

"Negative," Arthur answered, shaking his head. "The anchor plates are embedded twelve feet into the limestone bedrock, directly adjacent to the forty-thousand-gallon crude oil bypass manifold. Detonating twenty pounds of C-4 or dynamite within fifteen feet of that manifold will generate a shockwave exceeding the rupture threshold of the pipeline's ASTM A106 Grade B carbon steel line pipe. The resulting vapor cloud explosion would unleash eighty tons of TNT equivalent, destroying the pumps and incinerating everyone within six hundred yards."

Arthur paused, turning the page of his ledger to show Captain Cross and Bradley the refinery’s process flow diagram:

"The Wasatch Petrochemical Complex is not a crude fuel dump; it is an automated thermodynamic distillation refinery," Arthur explained. "The atmospheric column separates crude into light naphtha, kerosene, diesel, and heavy gas oil. But its true technological treasure is the **Continuous Catalytic Reformer**—the platforming unit. That unit houses forty metric tons of extruded platinum-alumina catalyst pellets. Platinum is an indispensable catalyst for reforming low-octane hydrocarbons into high-octane aromatic rings and synthesizing poly-alpha-olefin synthetic lipids."

He looked toward Eleanor, who sat quietly on a nearby boulder.

"Without that platinum catalyst, we cannot synthesize the organo-platinum stabilizing chelate required to dissolve Eleanor’s intracellular starlight crystals," Arthur said, his voice lowering with solemn gravity. "If the refinery is damaged or sabotaged by Cole in a protracted artillery duel, Eleanor’s cure is destroyed."

Eleanor pressed her fingertips against the cold limestone rock. Beneath her skin, the golden crystalline veins in her forearms pulsed in slow, resonant cycles. 

"Artie," Eleanor said softly, her breath catching slightly. "The big fortress at the lake... it's crawling with mutants. Over four hundred of them. But the one at the center... Ironfang Marcus Cole... his body feels wrong. It doesn't feel like a normal bio-mutant."

"Elaborate, Eleanor," Arthur said, turning his keen gaze upon her.

"His heart... it isn't beating with blood," she whispered, her green eyes wide and luminous in the dusk. "It's humming. It's like he has an engine inside his ribcage. A machine that drinks the starlight from the deep ground. And around him... there's a second presence. A giant. Covered in bone armor. It feels like a moving mountain of calcium."

"Bone-Crusher Silas," Bradley muttered, his face darkening. "Cole’s chief enforcer. We’ve heard the horror stories from escaping slaves. He’s an eight-foot monster whose bones grow outside his skin in thick interlocking plates. Fifty-caliber rounds bounce right off his breastplate. He broke the previous resistance leader in half over his knee."

Arthur Vance closed the theodolite's objective cover with a gentle click.

"Biological bone tissue is primarily composed of carbonated hydroxyapatite: $\text{Ca}_{10}(\text{PO}_4)_6(\text{OH})_2$, embedded in a collagen protein matrix," Arthur stated evenly. "While exceptionally tough under compression, hydroxyapatite possesses a brittle fracture mode under rapid shear stress, especially when subjected to extreme cryogenic thermal shock. Every biological structure possesses an exact thermodynamic vulnerability."

He turned back to the tactical group, his voice ringing with quiet authority.

"Here is our operational plan for Pumping Station Four:

"First, we will not ram the cables with the locomotive. Instead, we will deploy a specialized **Cryogenic Embrittlement & Acoustic Resonant Shear** assault.

"We have on board *The Iron Vanguard* four double-walled vacuum-insulated Dewar flasks of liquid nitrogen, maintained at minus three hundred and twenty degrees Fahrenheit (seventy-seven Kelvin) for the mobile laboratory's cryo-distillation equipment. 

"Standard ASTM A36 carbon steel possesses a ductile-to-brittle transition temperature between minus forty and minus ten degrees Fahrenheit. When immersed in liquid nitrogen at minus three hundred and twenty degrees, the face-centered cubic crystal lattice of the steel contracts, dislocation mobility drops to zero, and the material’s Charpy V-notch impact energy plummets from one hundred and twenty foot-pounds to less than four foot-pounds. The steel transitions from ductile metal to a completely brittle state identical to window glass."

Arthur raised his right forearm, where the **Chimera Frame Mark IV** gleamed in the twilight.

"At 8:00 PM, under cover of darkness, Eleanor, Captain Cross, Bradley, and I will infiltrate the northern canyon ridge using the salvaged raider skiff. We will carry the Dewar flasks and a specialized cryogenic spray manifold. We will apply the liquid nitrogen directly to the cable anchor turnbuckles for ninety seconds. 

"Once the steel is cryogenically embrittled, a single focused kinetic strike from the Mark IV's steam-pneumatic chisel—delivering fourteen thousand pounds of impact force—will shatter the three-inch cable like an icicle."

***

*Day 10 // 6:40 PM.*

Back inside the mobile machine shop of *The Iron Vanguard*, Arthur Vance conducted the final bench trials of the cryogenic spray equipment.

On the heavy maple workbench lay a test specimen: an eighteen-inch section of three-inch braided carbon steel recovery cable, clamped into a six-ton hydraulic test vice. 

Arthur adjusted the brass needle valve on a silver-plated vacuum Dewar flask. A frosted copper delivery wand hissed, venting a pale, ghostly fog of boiling liquid nitrogen across the braided steel strands.

Within thirty seconds, thick rime frost encrusted the metal. The steel turnbuckle turned bone-white, popping and creaking as thermal contraction exerted microscopic internal shear stresses across the crystalline grains.

"Temperature of the test cable: minus three hundred and twelve degrees Fahrenheit," Arthur observed, checking the display of a chromel-alumel thermocouple pyrometer. "Lattice mobility frozen. Plastic deformation capacity reduced to zero point zero percent."

He picked up a standard two-pound ball-peen hammer from the tool rack.

Arthur struck the frozen steel cable with a single, moderate swing.

*PING-SHATTER!*

The three-inch solid braided steel cable shattered into forty jagged, crystalline fragments that scattered across the workshop floor like broken glass shards.

Captain Cross and Sergeant Major Bradley stared at the shattered metal on the floor in dead silence.

"Well I'll be damned," Bradley muttered, kneeling down and picking up a frozen chunk of cable with his leather glove. "It snapped like peppermint candy."

"That is basic materials science, Sergeant Major," Arthur said calmly, setting the hammer back in its shadow-board rack. "Dislocation movement along slip planes requires thermal energy. Remove the thermal energy, and metallic yield strength gives way to catastrophic brittle cleaving."

He turned to Eleanor, who was adjusting the leather straps of her field harness.

"Eleanor, your responsibility during the infiltration is electromagnetic suppression," Arthur instructed. "Pumping Station Four is surrounded by an invisible aerosol haze of volatile hexane, pentane, and benzene vapors escaping from valve packings. If a single static spark or electrical discharge occurs during our approach, the fuel-air mixture will reach its lower explosive limit of one point two percent by volume."

Eleanor nodded, her face serious. She held out both hands, and a soft, pale-gold sphere of Starlight Sovereign flux manifested around her palms, glowing with gentle warmth.

"I can create a harmonic starlight Faraday cage," Eleanor said. "The starlight will ground all atmospheric static charges within fifty yards, keeping the electrostatic potential at absolute zero."

"Precisely," Arthur agreed. "Zero static potential equals zero spark ignition."

Arthur Vance reached into his waistcoat, clicked open his gold pocket watch, and noted the time.

*7:15 PM.*

"Ambient desert temperature: forty-eight degrees Fahrenheit and falling. Wind velocity: seven miles per hour out of the northwest. Perfect atmospheric conditions for stealth insertion."

He looked at Captain Cross, Bradley, and Eleanor.

"Load the cryogenic flasks onto the sand skiff. Silence all metallic sling buckles with friction tape. We move out to neutralize Pumping Station Four."
