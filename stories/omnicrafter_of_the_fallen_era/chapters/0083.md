# Chapter 83: The Rust Baron's Warmachine

The snow-covered cornfields of western Iowa trembled under the seismic cadence of approaching mechanical doom.

*Day 27 // 1:45 PM.*

From the high wooden observation platform of the Council Bluffs grain elevator, Arthur Vance peered through his brass naval telescope, training its quartz optics eastward toward the frozen horizon of the Nishnabotna River valley. The temperature hovered at ten degrees Fahrenheit beneath a pall of rolling black coal smoke that blotted out the pale afternoon sun.

Ten miles away, tearing through five-foot snowdrifts and crushing frozen farmsteads beneath an unstoppable tide of iron, came the main battle column of the **Iron Barony of the Rust Belt**.

And striding at the vanguard of the horde was the nightmare of the plains:

**The Colossus of Gary.**

The machine was an industrial monstrosity of colossal scale. Salvaged from the ruins of an ancient open-pit strip mine near the southern shore of Lake Michigan, the dreadnought was built upon a converted pre-Collapse Bucyrus-Erie walking dragline excavator chassis. It stood nearly sixty feet tall and weighed an estimated two hundred and ten metric tons. Its superstructure resembled a floating iron fortress, constructed from three-inch rolled steel armor plates salvaged from freight car sides and blast furnace ladles, encrusted with jagged welding slag, barbed wire, and defensive machine-gun galleries.

Instead of wheels or continuous caterpillar tracks, the Colossus moved upon two colossal articulated walking shoes. Each shoe was thirty feet long and eight feet wide, driven by massive forty-ton eccentric walking cams that lifted the immense machine, swung it forward seven feet at a stride, and set it down with an earth-shattering boom that sent shockwaves rippling through the frozen topsoil:

*KER-THUMP... CREEEAAAK... KER-THUMP!*

Twin vertical fire-tube boilers towered forty feet above its deck, venting billowing volcanic columns of black bituminous coal smoke and screaming plumes of excess steam from four whistling safety stacks. Mounted atop the central command cupola was a revolving armored turret housing twin 152-millimeter naval siege mortars salvaged from a sunken Great Lakes ore carrier. Hanging from its lateral flanks were two articulated forty-foot hydraulic excavator arms, retrofitted with spinning barbed-chain flails and six-foot circular rotary buzzsaws designed to pulverize fortified walls and barbed-wire entanglements.

Escorting the walking juggernaut across the snowy prairie was a mechanized army of five hundred raiders: twenty armored steam technicals, forty armed motorcycle outriders, and hundreds of foot soldiers clad in spiked sheet-metal cuirasses, marching beneath banners made of scorched iron sheet metal.

Perched upon the open armored bridge wing of the Colossus, his gloved hands gripping a brass speaking trumpet, stood the ruler of the Rust Belt himself:

**Baron Malakor.**

The warlord was a towering, broad-chested man sheathed in polished chrome automotive bumpers that had been hammered into an articulated suit of plate armor. Over his left eye was a crude cybernetic ocular assembly crafted from brass watch gears and red optical glass; across his shoulders hung a mantle of wolf pelts pinned with the silver rail-spikes of fifty destroyed settlements.

Through his pneumatic megaphone, Malakor’s voice boomed across the valley like rolling thunder:

"Crawl out of your holes, mountain rats! I have seen your silver train! I have seen your fancy toys! By sunset, the *Prometheus* will be hitched to my Colossus, and your Ghost Artificer will be boiling alive inside my firebox! Surrender the seed vaults of Omaha, or I will grind Council Bluffs into powder!"

Standing on the observation platform three miles away, Arthur Vance did not alter his breathing by a single fraction.

He held his leather ledger flat against the timber railing, clicking his mechanical pencil twice with his thumb.

"Visual, kinematic, and structural evaluation of the hostile siege asset," Arthur dictated calmly to the page. "Designation: walking dragline modification, estimated operating mass: two hundred and ten metric tons. Velocity: zero point eight miles per hour, stride frequency: eight point five strides per minute."

Captain Rachel Cross stood beside him, her sniper rifle resting against a sandbag embrasure, her knuckles white on the receiver.

"Arthur, that thing is a walking battleship," Cross said, her voice taut with tactical gravity. "Those twin one-hundred-and-fifty-two-millimeter mortars have an effective indirect range of six miles. If Malakor begins lobbing eighty-pound high-explosive shells into our railhead, our ammunition flatcars and fuel tankers will detonate in a chain reaction. We have four hundred civilian refugees from the river communes sheltering in the freight sheds behind us."

"A valid tactical concern, Captain Cross," Arthur replied evenly. "However, structural mechanics reveals that the Colossus is fundamentally unstable. Observe its stride cycle."

Arthur pointed his pencil at the Colossus's left walking cam as it lifted its massive thirty-foot shoe from the frozen earth:

"Ground contact pressure calculation," Arthur explained:

$$\text{Static Foot Area (Dual Shoes): } A_{\text{total}} = 2 \times (30\text{ ft} \times 8\text{ ft}) = 480 \text{ sq ft} = 69,120 \text{ sq in}$$

$$P_{\text{static}} = \frac{W}{A_{\text{total}}} = \frac{462,000\text{ lbs}}{69,120\text{ sq in}} \approx 6.68 \text{ PSI}$$

"Under static conditions with both shoes planted, the ground contact pressure is six point six eight pounds per square inch—entirely supported by frozen prairie loam," Arthur continued. "However, during dynamic walking locomotion, the entire two-hundred-and-ten-ton mass is transferred onto a single shoe while the eccentric cam rotates:

$$P_{\text{dynamic}} = \frac{462,000\text{ lbs}}{34,560\text{ sq in}} \approx 13.37 \text{ PSI}$$

"When that dynamic load strikes uncompacted snow over alluvial river clay, the bearing capacity of the soil is exceeded by a factor of two. The machine is experiencing twelve to fourteen inches of ground subsidence per step, causing extreme cyclical fatigue across the central eccentric walking shaft."

Arthur adjusted his naval telescope, focusing on the machine's underbelly:

"Furthermore, observe the primary prime mover: Malakor’s engineers have plumbed the twin vertical boilers directly into four exposed six-inch uninsulated steam headers that feed the walking cam hydraulic pumps. Because the headers lack thermal expansion loops and are exposed to minus twelve-degree crosswinds, they are losing seventeen percent of their thermal enthalpy to the atmosphere every ten feet. The operating pressure inside the cam cylinders is hovering at a dangerous two hundred and forty PSI—well above the fatigue threshold of their cast-iron flanges."

Arthur noted the fuel consumption rate on the margin of his ledger:

$$\text{Bituminous Coal Burn Rate: } \dot{m}_{\text{coal}} \approx 5,200 \text{ lbs/hr} \quad (2.6 \text{ tons/hour})$$

"With an on-board bunker capacity of approximately twenty tons of coal, the Colossus possesses an operational endurance limit of scarcely seven point six hours of sustained combat movement before its boilers run dry," Arthur observed. "It is an engineering absurdity that cannot endure prolonged maneuver."

*BOOOOOOM! BOOOOOOM!*

Two miles across the snowfields, twin sheets of orange flame erupted from the Colossus’s top turret.

Two eighty-pound high-explosive mortar shells screamed through the winter sky, trailing plumes of black smoke on an arching ballistic trajectory toward the Council Bluffs railhead:

$$\text{Ballistic Trajectory: } R = \frac{v_0^2 \cdot \sin(2\theta)}{g} \approx 10,560 \text{ ft} \quad (2.0 \text{ miles})$$

"Incoming artillery! Salvo One in the air!" Cross shouted, reaching for the alarm cord.

Arthur did not move. He turned calmly toward Eleanor Vance, who stood at the center of the railhead platform below.

Eleanor stepped forward into the clear snow, her hands raised gracefully toward the heavens. Her violet eyes shone with blinding, pure golden brilliance.

*SHUUUUUU-VOOOOOOOM!*

Her Mid-Tier Sovereign starlight erupted upward in a towering parabolic dome of coherent 528 Hz energy, enveloping the entire Council Bluffs rail station, the *Prometheus*, and the refugee freight sheds in an impenetrable golden aurora.

The two eighty-pound mortar shells plunged out of the sky at seven hundred feet per second, aimed directly at the station’s main roof.

The instant the explosive steel casings touched the outer boundary of the starlight field, their kinetic momentum was arrested. The extreme impact energy was converted into harmless acoustic vibrations that resonated at 528 Hz, while the mechanical firing pins inside the nose fuses were chemically damped, preventing percussion detonation.

The two heavy shells lost all velocity and dropped harmlessly into a six-foot snowdrift outside the perimeter fence with two muffled *thuds*, completely inert.

A collective gasp of awe and relief swept through the hundreds of refugees watching from the freight shed windows.

Across the valley, Baron Malakor lowered his brass speaking trumpet in disbelief, his cybernetic eye spinning wildly as he stared at the glowing golden dome protecting the railhead.

"What is that light?!" Malakor roared at his gunners. "Reload the mortars! Fire a full five-round salvo! Break that bubble!"

Arthur Vance stepped down from the observation tower, his boots ringing evenly on the iron ladder rungs.

He joined Master Joseph Kovacs, Eleanor, and Captain Cross in the locomotive machine shop car.

"Eleanor’s starlight field can maintain atmospheric and kinetic shielding over Council Bluffs for approximately six hours without core fatigue," Arthur stated, consulting his pocket watch.

*2:18 PM.*

"However, passive defense is an inefficient expenditure of Sovereign reserve energy. A protracted artillery duel will exhaust our defensive envelope and risk collateral damage to the rail lines. The Colossus must be neutralized through direct, surgical kinetic intervention."

Master Kovacs looked at Arthur’s right arm, where the Chimera Frame Mark V was mounted. "Arthur, the Mark V has six thousand PSI of punch, but that walking monster has four inches of armor on its chassis and walking cams twelve feet in diameter. How are you going to reach the critical joints without getting crushed under sixty tons of moving iron?"

Arthur pulled a fresh blueprint sheet from his drafting drawer, spreading it across the workbench under the glare of an electric inspection lamp.

"By executing an immediate tactical upgrade," Arthur answered calmly. "We shall forge the **Chimera Frame Mark VI (High-Kinetic Sovereign Chassis)**."

Arthur clicked his mechanical pencil, tapping the clean schematic:

"We have seven thousand pounds of Black Hills ferro-vanadium, the high-purity star-iron billets from Vulcan Junction, and the high-output induction crucible on the flatcar. By integrating high-pressure pneumatic jump thrusters into the leg struts and replacing the primary piston with a twin-stage hydraulic-pneumatic tandem amplifier, the Mark VI will deliver thirty thousand pounds of kinetic shearing force, with an agile vertical jump capability of twenty-five feet."

Kovacs’ eyes lit up with intense craftsman zeal. "A twenty-five-foot pneumatic leap? You'll be able to jump straight onto the walking gimbals while the machine takes a stride!"

"Precisely," Arthur confirmed. "We target the primary walking cam shaft, sever the high-pressure steam distribution headers, and dismount Malakor before his secondary artillery can breach our perimeter."

Arthur turned to Captain Cross. "Deploy two squads of Company B to reinforce the perimeter sandbags with our water-cooled machine guns. Direct them to engage Malakor’s motorcycle outriders and light technicals if they attempt to bypass the flank."

"On it," Cross barked, racking her rifle bolt. "The perimeter will hold."

Arthur looked at Kovacs and Daniel Teton.

"We have three hours before Malakor closes the distance to the rail perimeter," Arthur said, clicking his pocket watch shut. "Master Kovacs, heat the induction crucible to two thousand eight hundred degrees Fahrenheit. We begin fabrication of the Mark VI immediately."
