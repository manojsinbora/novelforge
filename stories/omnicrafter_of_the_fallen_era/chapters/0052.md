# Chapter 52: Infiltration of the Cracking Towers

The sky above the Wasatch Petrochemical Complex was an inferno of orange flare-gas and choking black plumes of heavy oil smoke.

*Day 11 // 12:15 AM.*

A hundred and twenty feet above the refinery concrete aprons, Arthur Vance walked in steady, measured balance along the narrow catwalk of an overhead high-pressure steam trestle. 

The wind blowing off the Great Salt Lake was biting cold, carrying sharp crystals of alkali dust, but here amidst the pipes, the air radiated intense, oppressive heat. Beneath his vulcanized rubber boots, two twenty-four-inch insulated steel pipes vibrated with superheated steam at six hundred PSI and seven hundred and fifty degrees Fahrenheit, hissing through aged gasket flanges like restless vipers.

Arthur wore his chemical respirator strapped securely over his mouth and nose, his clear safety goggles sealed against the corrosive vapors of hydrogen sulfide, sulfur dioxide, and aromatic hydrocarbons. Over his shoulder hung a heavy tool bag containing brass spanners, pneumatic jumpers, and diagnostic gauges. On his right arm, the **Chimera Frame Mark IV** gleamed in the flickering flare light, fully charged and ready.

Five paces behind him moved Eleanor Vance. 

Her lightweight canvas field jacket was zipped tight to her neck. She did not require a chemical respirator; around her head and upper torso, a thin, shimmering corona of golden Starlight Sovereign flux filtered the ambient air, neutralizing toxic hydrocarbon molecules on contact through bio-photonic oxidation.

Arthur paused at an inspection platform to check his sister’s condition. He pulled a portable optical pyrometer from his vest and aimed the sensor at her cheek.

"Facial skin temperature: ninety-six point four degrees Fahrenheit," Arthur observed, checking his pocket watch. "Resonance frequency: five hundred and twenty-eight hertz. Are you experiencing any dizziness from the ambient hydrocarbon vapor concentrations?"

"No, Artie," Eleanor answered softly, shaking her head. "The starlight burns it away before it reaches my lungs. But the heat... the whole refinery feels like a living, feverish body. It's groaning under the pressure."

Down below in the shadows of the rail siding, Captain Rachel Cross and Sergeant Major Bradley led thirty riflemen of Company B toward the concrete intake of the stormwater drainage sluice. 

Through the miniature tactical radio earpiece, Cross’s voice crackled:

"Alpha Team has breached the storm drain, Arthur. We’re sixty yards from Sub-Level Two. Zero sentry contact so far. We will secure the civilian technicians before Cole can initiate the gas purge."

"Understood, Rachel," Arthur replied in a steady murmur into his throat microphone. "Maintain strict noise discipline. If Cole detects your breach, he will attempt to vent the hydrogen sulfide lines into the lower ventilation ducts. Keep your atropine and amyl nitrite auto-injectors at hand."

Arthur reached the end of the steam trestle, stepping onto the steel grating of Column Number Three—a colossal three-hundred-foot atmospheric fractional distillation column that rose like a rusted skyscraper into the dark.

Inside the column, thousands of barrels of crude petroleum were boiling at temperatures ranging from one hundred to seven hundred degrees Fahrenheit, separating into volatile fractions across forty internal sieve trays.

Suddenly, a series of violent, rhythmic thuds rattled the entire steel tower.

*THUD-KER-THUMP! THUD-KER-THUMP!*

The four-inch steel tower groaned beneath catastrophic internal hydraulic hammer. 

On an illuminated instrument panel bolted to the catwalk wall, the brass needle of the tower's primary differential pressure gauge was vibrating violently against its red stop: **Four Hundred and Sixty PSI**—climbing toward the column’s ultimate rupture threshold of five hundred PSI.

"Artie!" Eleanor cried out, grabbing the safety rail as the tower swayed beneath their feet. "What's happening? The whole tower feels like it's about to explode!"

Arthur stepped up to the instrument panel, his gray eyes scanning the liquid level indicators and reflux flowmeters in less than three seconds.

"The column is experiencing catastrophic **Liquid Carryover and Tray Flooding**," Arthur diagnosed, his voice completely calm, devoid of panic. "Cole’s untrained raider crew has overfired the bottom reboiler furnaces without adjusting the top reflux rate. The rising hydrocarbon vapor velocity has exceeded twelve feet per second, lifting liquid heavy gas oil into the upper trays and plugging the vapor downcomers."

He pointed to the rising pressure gauge:

$$P_{\text{internal}} = 460 \text{ PSI} \xrightarrow{\text{trend}} 510 \text{ PSI within 180 seconds}$$

"If the internal pressure exceeds five hundred PSI," Arthur explained, "the welded head seam of this three-hundred-foot column will suffer catastrophic hoop stress rupture. The sudden depressurization will trigger an instantaneous **Boiling Liquid Expanding Vapor Explosion (BLEVE)**: forty thousand barrels of superheated petroleum flashing into aerosol, followed by a six-hundred-yard fireball that will incinerate the entire refinery and everyone in it."

"Can we vent it?" Eleanor asked, her voice tight.

"The automated emergency pressure relief valve at the top of the tower has suffered mechanical vapor-lock," Arthur said, looking up at an external bypass manifold fifty feet higher on the tower. "We must manually open the sixty-degree manual reflux bypass gate to divert the liquid slug directly into the emergency flare header."

*TAT-TAT-TAT-TAT!*

A burst of automatic gunfire erupted from the opposite catwalk.

Three mutated raider sentries, alerted by the tower’s vibrations, had stepped out onto the upper platform fifty yards away. Their 7.62-millimeter assault rifles flashed in the dark, sending copper-jacketed rounds ricocheting off the distillation column’s steel shell with deafening *PANG-PANG-PANG* impacts.

One bullet sheared through the instrument glass inches from Arthur’s face.

Arthur did not flinch.

"Eleanor. Suppression and grounding," Arthur commanded calmly.

Eleanor Vance stepped in front of Arthur, her green eyes blazing with pure celestial starlight.

She extended her left arm. The golden aura of the Starlight Sovereign expanded into a crystalline hexagonal buckler of concentrated electromagnetic energy.

*SPARK-PING-CLANG!*

A volley of rifle rounds struck Eleanor’s starlight shield, flattening into lead discs and falling harmlessly to the steel grating. Simultaneously, Eleanor projected a blinding beam of concentrated photon radiation across the catwalk, burning directly into the sentries' night-adapted retinas.

The three raiders shrieked, clutching their scorched eyes and dropping their rifles in agony.

Arthur Vance moved.

With the agility of a trained mountaineer, he surged up the vertical steel ladder toward the reflux manifold fifty feet above. The heavy Chimera Frame Mark IV on his right arm balanced his center of mass with mechanical precision.

He reached the bypass platform.

Mounted to the forty-inch reflux pipe was a massive cast-iron gate valve with a three-foot iron handwheel, encrusted with dried petroleum sludge and rust.

Arthur jammed his heavy twenty-four-inch forged-steel pipe spanner into the handwheel spokes, bracing his boots against the safety railing.

He looked down at the pressure gauge: **Four Hundred and Eighty-Five PSI**.

The tower shell was creaking; rivets along the perimeter were popping like gunshots under the immense internal stress.

Arthur leaned his entire body weight into the spanner.

"Reflux ratio calculation," Arthur muttered through his respirator, muscles straining against the frozen valve threads:

$$R = \frac{L}{D} \approx 3.5 \rightarrow \text{Bypass volume required: 450 gallons per minute}$$

*SCREEEEEECH-CLANK!*

The rusted valve stem yielded with a shuddering groan.

Arthur spun the handwheel three full revolutions counter-clockwise, opening the twelve-inch gate valve wide into the flare header.

*ROOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOAR!*

With a deafening acoustic scream, eighty tons of trapped high-pressure hydrocarbon vapor and liquid gas oil tore through the open bypass line, shooting three hundred feet into the air through the towering flare stack on the northern headland.

A colossal jet of white-hot fire erupted from the flare tip, lighting the entire Great Salt Lake in daylight brilliance.

Inside Column Number Three, the hydraulic hammer ceased.

On the instrument panel, the pressure needle shuddered, reversed course, and plummeted back down to safe operating parameters:

**Four Hundred... Three Hundred and Twenty... Two Hundred and Ten PSI.**

The structural vibrations died away. The three-hundred-foot tower settled back into its solid concrete footings.

Arthur Vance wiped sweat and black soot from his spectacles with his sleeve, taking a deep breath of filtered air through his respirator.

He climbed back down the ladder to where Eleanor stood.

***

*Day 11 // 12:55 AM.*

Before descending to the ground, Arthur led Eleanor across the lower pipe gallery to the **Central Instrument Air Compressor House**.

The small brick building housed the twin reciprocating compressors that supplied one-hundred-PSI control air to all automated safety valves throughout the refinery.

Outside the door, four raider saboteurs were preparing to open the manual bypass lines to vent toxic hydrogen sulfide into the ventilation ducts.

Arthur did not draw a firearm.

He activated the Mark IV's steam-pneumatic accumulator, crossed the threshold in two blinding strides, and drove the blunt biogenic elbow strut of the frame into the lead saboteur's sternum, knocking him unconscious before he could turn around. The remaining three saboteurs dropped their wrenches and surrendered on the spot.

Arthur stepped to the main instrument air distribution manifold:

"This is the pneumatic nerve center of the complex," Arthur said to Eleanor. "Every emergency dump valve on the platinum reactors is held shut by pneumatic control pressure. If Cole attempts to trigger the scuttling mechanism from his control console, he relies on this compressed air line."

Arthur unslung his heavy pipe cutters. 

With two swift, calculated turns of the hardened alloy cutting wheel, he severed the two-inch copper main header.

*PSHHHHHHHHHT!*

The compressed air vented into the room, dropping instrument header pressure to zero PSI.

Throughout the continuous catalytic reforming complex, hundreds of heavy spring-return fail-safe actuators snapped shut with deep, resonant metallic clanks.

"Fail-safe mode verified," Arthur announced, inspecting the pressure gauges. "All reactor dump valves are now mechanically locked in their isolated, non-scuttle configurations. Cole’s remote self-destruct mechanism has been rendered completely inoperable."

Through the tactical radio, Captain Cross’s voice burst through with triumph:

"Arthur! We’ve breached Sub-Level Two! All six hundred civilian technicians and families secured! We’ve eliminated the gas dispersal team and isolated the ventilation valves! The workers are safe!"

"Superb execution, Captain," Arthur replied, his voice level and steady. "Now initiate defensive perimeter around the lube-oil warehouse. Secure the civilians and prepare the medical bay."

Arthur turned to his sister, who was looking toward the massive, blast-shielded concrete bunker at the center of the refinery: **The Platforming Catalyst Vault**.

Beneath the double-walled steel blast doors, a heavy, dull vibration shuddered through the earth.

"Artie," Eleanor whispered, stepping to his side. "The workers are safe. But inside that vault... Silas knows we're here. He just locked the main blast door from the inside."

Arthur Vance stepped to the edge of the catwalk, looking down at the vault doors sixty feet below.

He raised his right arm, checking the pressure dial of the Chimera Frame Mark IV: **Four Thousand Five Hundred PSI**.

He retrieved his pocket watch, clicking it open under the glare of the flare stack.

*1:08 AM.*

"Bone-Crusher Silas guards forty tons of platinum reforming catalyst," Arthur said, his eyes hard and focused behind his safety goggles. "And my sister's cure lies inside those reactors."

Arthur Vance adjusted his leather apron and drew his diamond-honed chisel into striking position.

"Let us go introduce Mr. Silas to the laws of fracture mechanics."
