# Chapter 46: The Return to Vulcan Junction

The exhaust manifolds of the Baldwin-Westinghouse DR-12-8-1500 rumbled with a deep, rhythmic bass that resonated through the granite foundations of the Cascade Mountains.

*Day 9 // 8:15 AM.*

*The Iron Vanguard* rolled slowly into the marshalling yard of Vulcan Junction, its steel wheels grinding smoothly against the newly laid 136-pound star-iron track. Behind the 160-ton locomotive, six 50-ton steel hopper cars groaned beneath the weight of their cargo: eighteen point five metric tons of premium Ceres Oasis winter wheat, five tons of dried legumes, and three point two metric tons of biogenic nickel-chromium-iron tusk-blades harvested from the Salt-Crust Behemoth.

A dense cloud of fragrant woodsmoke, hot lubricating grease, and coal gas hung over the valley. 

Through the morning mist, the four massive blast furnaces of the Cascade Steel Works loomed like soot-blackened bastions, their sixty-foot brick stacks belching incandescent orange embers into the pale mountain sky.

Standing on the raised wooden unloader platform, Master Foundryman Joseph Kovacs leaned over the safety railing, a wide, soot-streaked grin splitting his gray beard.

"Haul that coupling pin, Miller!" Kovacs bellowed at the yard crew. "Clear Track Two for the ore cars! Get the pneumatic crane over here before that biogenic iron cools down to ambient temperature!"

Arthur Vance stepped down from the locomotive’s rear vestibule onto the timber platform. 

His boots made a crisp, solid sound against the creosote-treated wood. He wore his heavy work apron over his dark wool waistcoat, his spectacles polished clean, his number-two drafting pencil tucked behind his right ear. In his left hand, he carried a black oilcloth satchel containing the mineral assays and the ratified parchment of the Great Basin Accord.

Beside him, Eleanor leaped down with effortless, cat-like grace. Her green eyes sparkled in the morning light, her blonde hair caught in a leather cord. Over her shoulder hung a canvas sack packed with fresh, sweet Ceres oranges—the first fresh fruit seen in the mountain passes in nearly seven months.

"Catch, Joseph!" Eleanor called out, lobbing a plump, sun-ripened orange through the air.

Kovacs caught it in one massive, leather-gloved hand. He sniffed it, his eyes widening in pure delight.

"By the hammer of Vulcan," Kovacs breathed. "Real citrus. The boys in the rolling mill will weep like schoolgirls. They’ve been drinking pine-needle tea for vitamin C since November."

"Thirty-two crates of fresh citrus are allocated to Vulcan Junction and Iron Ridge Hydro-Dam," Arthur Vance stated evenly, stepping up to the crane console. "It is an essential nutritional countermeasure against scurvy and hypovitaminosis. Furthermore, eighteen tons of Ceres wheat are being transferred to the silo cars for distribution to Oakhaven and High Rock."

Arthur gestured toward the flatcar where the massive, curved tusk-blades of the Salt-Crust Behemoth lay lashed with three-inch steel cables.

"What interests us from an industrial standpoint, however, is this," Arthur said, tapping the glistening, quartz-encrusted metal with the brass ferrule of his pocket rule.

Kovacs approached the flatcar, his craftsman’s eye gleaming with intense scrutiny. He pulled a portable hardness tester from his toolbelt and pressed the diamond indenter into the cleaned bevel of the tusk.

"Rockwell C sixty-two," Kovacs whispered, reading the dial. "And that's *as-cast*, without quench or temper. Arthur, how did that beast grow metal this hard in its face?"

"Subterranean biochemical chemosynthesis," Arthur answered, opening his ledger to a page filled with molecular diagrams. "The creature inhabited a hyper-saline hydrothermal vent system containing elevated concentrations of dissolved nickel, chromium, and molybdenum. Its metabolic secretions deposited iron and transition metals in alternating crystalline lamellae, mimicking the micro-structure of ancient Damascus crucible steel. When melted down in your electric arc furnace under an inert argon-slag blanket, this three-point-two tons of biogenic alloy will yield sufficient high-tensile material to cast sixty heavy railroad switch points and thirty sets of wear-resistant locomotive brake shoes."

Kovacs let out a low whistle. 

"Standard carbon-steel switch points on the mountain incline wear out every four hundred transit miles because of the sixty-degree wheel-flange friction. If we cast them from this biogenic matrix..."

"Their service life will increase by a factor of eight," Arthur calculated. "Mean time between replacement: three thousand two hundred operating miles."

"Praise be to engineering," Kovacs chuckled, waving to the overhead gantry operator. "Rig the slings, boys! Melt down the monster’s teeth! Let’s make some track!"

***

*Day 9 // 11:45 AM.*

While the overhead gantry crane hoisted the first ton of biogenic iron toward the furnace skip-hoist, Arthur Vance walked into the Vulcan Junction telegraph and operations office.

The office was a solid brick structure nestled beneath the shadow of Blast Furnace Number Two. Inside, the rhythmic clatter of the Edison polar duplex sounder was already singing against its brass stop.

Corporal Davis looked up from the operator’s desk, tearing a yellow carbon-paper telegram from the receiver platen.

"Chief Vance! Urgent wire from Fort Sterling," Davis announced, his voice tight. "Sergeant Major Bradley sent it priority-alpha three minutes ago."

Arthur took the yellow sheet. His sharp gray eyes scanned the typed Morse transcription:

```
FROM: LOGISTICS DEPOT FORT STERLING // SGT MAJ HANK BRADLEY
TO: CHIEF ARTIFICER ARTHUR VANCE // VULCAN JUNCTION / BUNKER 07
DATE: DAY 9 // 11:42 AM
PRIORITY: ALPHA-IMMEDIATE

RECONNAISSANCE PATROLS REPORT SEVERE UNREST EAST OF GREAT BASIN.
IRONFANG MARCUS COLE HAS MOBILIZED THE RED SANDS SYNDICATE.
OVER FORTY ARMORED SAND SKIFFS AND TWO ARMORED REPAIR TRAINS
SPOTTED MOVING WEST FROM PROMONTORY POINT TOWARD WASATCH GAP.

WARLORD FORCES HAVE SEIZED THE INTERMEDIATE OIL PUMPING STATIONS.
PETROLEUM PIPELINE TO FORT STERLING HAS SUFFERED PRESSURE DROP OF 85%.
OUR CRUDE DIESEL STORAGE STANDS AT 72,400 GALLONS—CURRENT CONSUMPTION
RATES WILL EXHAUST RESERVES IN 28 DAYS WITHOUT WASATCH REFINERY CRUDE.

WARNING: INTERCEPTED RAIDER TRANSMISSIONS CONFIRM THEY ARE HUNTING
THE 'GHOST ARTIFICER' RESPONSIBLE FOR THE CERES OASIS DEFEAT.
THEY BELIEVE WE POSSESS A PRE-COLLAPSE MILITARY WEAPONS CACHE.
RECOMMEND IMMEDIATE STRATEGIC CONVERGENCE.
```

Arthur Vance read the telegram twice, his expression remaining completely placid.

He clicked open his pocket watch.

*11:46 AM.*

"The math is straightforward," Arthur observed calmly, setting the telegram on the drafting table. "Fort Sterling holds seventy-two thousand four hundred gallons of refined diesel. Operating *The Iron Vanguard*, the Vulcan blast furnace blowers, the Iron Ridge hydro-turbines' auxiliary pumps, and the High Rock agricultural tractors requires two thousand five hundred gallons per week. Without crude oil input from the Wasatch Petrochemical Complex, the Confederation will face an energy deficit within twenty-eight days. At that point, our rolling stock halts, our foundries chill, and our mutual defense perimeter collapses."

Eleanor stepped to his side, looking at the eastern map pinned to the pine wall.

"Ironfang Marcus Cole," Eleanor said quietly. Her voice hardened, the gentle warmth of her starlight aura dimming into a cold, protective focus. "The rumors we heard in the Blackwood refugee clinics... they called him the 'Flayer of Promontory'. He was an early Stage Four mutant warlord who seized the pre-Collapse refinery by force. He feeds dissenters into the catalytic cracking furnaces."

"Cruelty is merely a symptom of inefficient political hierarchy and technological scarcity," Arthur remarked, pulling a fresh sheet of drafting paper onto his drawing board. "However, a Stage Four bio-mutant possessing control over a regional petrochemical refinery and sixty-four miles of eastern rail corridor represents an unacceptable entropy vector."

Captain Rachel Cross stepped into the office, her muddy field coat draped over her uniform, her M4 carbine slung securely. She had just arrived on the morning shuttle car from Iron Ridge Dam.

"What's the play, Arthur?" Cross asked, leaning her hands on the table. "Company B is at full combat readiness. We have sixty-two riflemen, three M2 fifty-caliber heavy machine guns, and ninety thousand rounds of five-five-six ammunition. If Cole brings his sand skiffs across the salt flats, we can fortify the Ceres Oasis perimeter and dig in."

"A static defense across an open salt desert is strategically flawed," Arthur replied, shaking his head. "The perimeter of Ceres Oasis is four miles in circumference. Enclosing it requires excessive manpower and ammunition expenditure. Furthermore, defending Ceres does not resolve the pipeline pressure drop. The fuel refinery at Wasatch Gap must be captured intact."

Arthur tapped the map at the eastern bottleneck: **The Wasatch Gap**.

"The Wasatch Gap is a natural geological choke point," Arthur explained. "On either side rise sheer limestone cliffs eight hundred feet high. Through the center runs the single-track rail line of the old Central Pacific and the six-inch petroleum pipeline. If we control the gap, we control the flow of fuel, rail transit, and mineral trade across the entire eastern continent."

Kovacs entered the room, wiping his soot-blackened hands on a rag.

"To push all the way to Wasatch Gap and fight an entrenched warlord syndicate, Arthur, we’re going to need more than thirty-ton freight cars and fifty-caliber machine guns," Kovacs said. "Cole has armored sand skiffs with aircraft engines, scavenged artillery, and bio-mutated shock troops who don't feel pain."

Arthur Vance picked up his drafting pencil.

"I agree, Joseph," Arthur said softly. "Which is why we are upgrading our combat apparatus immediately."

Arthur flipped to an unrolled blueprint on his drafting easel. At the top of the parchment, written in bold, architectural lettering, was the title:

**CHIMERA FRAME MARK IV: BIOGENIC ALLOY & HIGH-PRESSURE DUAL-CYCLE EXPANSION CHASSIS.**

"The Mark III has served admirably," Arthur stated, gesturing to the exoskeleton armature resting on the wooden workbench. "However, its pneumatic operating pressure is capped at three thousand PSI, generating a peak kinetic force of two thousand four hundred pounds. Against standard chitin and unarmored targets, this is sufficient. Against Stage Four bio-mutated muscle density and armored raider skiffs, it lacks instantaneous shearing capacity."

He pointed to the mechanical schematics of the Mark IV:

"For the Mark IV, we are incorporating the newly harvested biogenic nickel-chromium-iron tusk alloy directly into the load-bearing forearm struts and kinetic chisel casing. The chisel itself will be upgraded to a hexagonal solid tungsten-carbide core sheathed in diamond-honed biogenic alloy. Furthermore, we will replace the single-stage nitrogen cylinder with a **Closed-Loop Steam-Pneumatic Hybrid Accumulator**."

Kovacs leaned in, his eyes widening as he traced the valve lines.

"Steam-pneumatic hybrid?" Kovacs repeated. "How are you generating flash steam inside a wearable exoskeleton arm without boiling your own flesh?"

"Flash-vaporization micro-coils powered by the locomotive’s star-iron induction shunts," Arthur explained with absolute clarity. "A micro-reservoir of distilled water is superheated to four hundred and fifty degrees Fahrenheit within three milliseconds via seventy-five thousand volts of starlight capacitance. When the solenoid trips, the expanding superheated steam acts against the secondary pneumatic piston face, compounding the nitrogen pressure from three thousand PSI to **Four Thousand Five Hundred PSI**."

Arthur paused, calculating the output in his head:

$$\text{Piston Area } A = \pi \cdot (1.0 \text{ in})^2 = 3.1416 \text{ in}^2$$

$$F_{\text{peak}} = P \cdot A = 4,500 \text{ PSI} \times 3.1416 \text{ in}^2 \approx 14,137 \text{ lbs} \approx 7.07 \text{ tons of linear kinetic impact}.$$

"Seven tons of instantaneous impact force," Captain Cross whispered, staring at the schematic. "That’s enough to punch clean through the front glacis plate of an armored personnel carrier."

"Or shatter the skull of a Stage Four mutant warlord," Eleanor added softly.

"Precisely," Arthur said, capping his pencil. "Joseph, take these blueprints to the precision tool room. Machine the cylinder sleeves to a tolerance of five ten-thousandths of an inch. Eleanor, I require your assistance in calibrating the starlight capacitor shunts. Rachel, issue orders to Company B: we depart for Fort Sterling and the Wasatch Frontier tomorrow at dawn."

Arthur Vance reached into his waistcoat, clicked open his gold-plated Elgin pocket watch, and noted the time.

*12:00 PM.*

"Preparation window: eighteen hours," Arthur concluded in his calm, unhurried cadence. "Let us begin the assembly."
