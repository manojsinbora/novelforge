# Chapter 67: The Rotary Snowplow Breakout

The blizzard that slammed into the Absaroka Range at midnight was an apocalyptic squall of sub-zero wind, volcanic ash, and blinding, wind-driven snow.

*Day 17 // 11:15 PM.*

In less than three hours, the temperature on the high mountain pass plunged from fifteen degrees Fahrenheit to **minus twenty-two degrees Fahrenheit**.

Across the ten-mile rail corridor connecting Jackson Hole to the Obsidian Ridge industrial spur, the howling mountain winds whipped the snow into massive, compacted drifts. In the cuts between the basalt cliffs, the snowpack rose eight, ten, and twelve feet deep—dense, wind-packed "sugar snow" mixed with heavy rhyolitic ash, capable of derailing any standard plow train that dared ram it.

Two miles down the mountain, at the staging siding, Master Foundryman Joseph Kovacs leaned out the cab window of *The Iron Vanguard*, his thick beard frosted with icicles.

Before coupling the rotary plow to the locomotive pilot, Kovacs and Corporal Davis had conducted a rigorous mechanical inspection under Arthur Vance’s written maintenance protocol:

They inspected the massive six-to-one bevel gear reduction set linking the reciprocating steam cylinders to the main twelve-inch rotor shaft, packing the cast-steel gear housing with forty pounds of graphite-impregnated grease. They checked the double-acting Stephenson valve gear, verifying zero steam blow-by across the piston valves, and tested the lubricator pumps feeding hot cylinder oil to the phosphor-bronze sleeve bearings.

"Centrifugal discharge force calculation," Kovacs muttered, reviewing Arthur's notes pinned to the gauge board:

$$F_c = m \cdot r \cdot \omega^2 \approx (25 \text{ lb}) \times (6.0 \text{ ft}) \times \left(\frac{150 \times 2\pi}{60}\right)^2 \approx 36,975 \text{ lbs}$$

"Over thirty-six thousand pounds of centrifugal force throwing the snow clear of the right-of-way! No snowdrift on God's earth can resist that kind of momentum!"

Beside him, Sergeant Major Bradley looked at the glowing dispatch lamp on the communications console:

"Message from Arthur at the Tartarus outer gate!" Bradley shouted over the screaming gale. "He’s holding the courtyard with Naomi Tsosie, but the Covenant’s wellhead demolition countdown is running! They need the thirty thousand pounds of barite mud tankers and the auxiliary steam lines at the gate within four hours, or they can’t seal the supercritical casing!"

Bradley pointed out the front glass at the black wall of snow burying the tracks:

"Look at that cut, Joseph! There’s twelve feet of compacted snow and volcanic ash packed solid between those rock walls! If we ram that drift with a standard wedge plow, the train will wedge solid like an axe in an oak log!"

Master Kovacs grinned, spitting into the snow and pulling his heavy fur mittens tight.

"Who said anything about a wedge plow, Sergeant Major?" Kovacs bellowed with a deep, booming laugh. "We’re not shoving that snow! We’re going to eat it!"

Kovacs gestured toward the front of the locomotive, where the crowning masterpiece of pre-Collapse winter railroading was coupled to the front pilot:

**The Leslie Steam Rotary Snowplow.**

It was a forty-ton armored steel car housing a colossal twelve-foot-diameter rotating cutting wheel. 

Fabricated from one-inch-thick rolled boiler plate, the wheel mounted ten massive, heavy steel scoop-blades, each fitted with reversible forged-alloy cutting knives. Behind the wheel sat a high-pressure, two-cylinder reciprocating steam engine, coupled directly to the rotary shaft and fed by six hundred PSI of superheated steam from *The Iron Vanguard’s* auxiliary steam generator car.

The rotary center journal bearing—a massive twelve-inch phosphor-bronze sleeve bearing—was lubricated by a continuous forced-feed mechanical lubricator pumping heavy steam-cylinder oil at two hundred degrees Fahrenheit to prevent freezing.

Kovacs climbed into the rotary plow’s forward operator cab, gripping the heavy steam throttle.

Beside him, Corporal Davis checked the delivery pressure from the steam car:

"Steam header pressure: five hundred and eighty PSI! Reversing gear locked forward! Chute cowl angled forty-five degrees to the right, throwing clear of the canyon wall!"

Arthur Vance’s written operating instructions lay pinned to the wooden console beside the steam gauge:

```
ROTARY SNOWPLOW THERMODYNAMIC OPERATING PARAMETERS:
- Compacted Snow Density: 25 lbs/cubic foot (mixed with volcanic ash).
- Wheel Diameter: 12.0 feet. Rotor Swept Area: 113.1 square feet.
- Operating Velocity: 6.0 miles per hour (8.8 feet/second).
- Mass Intake Rate: 8.8 ft/s x 113.1 sq ft x 25 lb/cu ft = 24,882 lbs/second.
- Kinetic Throwing Velocity: 120 feet/second via 150 RPM centrifugal discharge.
- Continuous Tractive Resistance: 32,000 lbs. Maintain Locomotive Throttle Notch 6.
```

"Twenty-five thousand pounds of snow per second!" Davis yelled, his eyes wide.

"Then let's feed the beast!" Kovacs roared.

He hauled the rotary steam throttle wide open.

*PSHHHHHHHH-CHUG-CHUG-CHUG-CHUG!*

The massive twin steam cylinders of the rotary engine barked rhythmic, concussive exhaust blasts into the freezing night.

The twelve-foot cutting wheel groaned, started to turn, and rapidly accelerated to **One Hundred and Fifty Revolutions Per Minute**. The ten steel scoop-blades blurred into a silver-and-black vortex of humming kinetic steel.

Behind the plow, the sixteen-cylinder diesels of *The Iron Vanguard* throttled up to Notch Six:

Sixty-five thousand pounds of tractive effort drove the 160-ton locomotive forward, shoving the roaring rotary plow directly into the twelve-foot snowdrift at six miles per hour.

*KER-BLAM-ROOOOOOOOOOOOOOOAR!*

The collision was magnificent.

The high-speed alloy knives of the rotary wheel bit into the packed, frozen snowdrift like a giant industrial buzzsaw. 

The spinning blades did not compress the snow; they carved it into microscopic ice crystals, scooping thousands of pounds of compacted snow and black volcanic ash into the wheel housing every second. Centrifugal force whipped the pulverized blizzard up through the adjustable steel discharge chute on the roof.

*FOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM!*

A colossal, roaring plume of white snow and black cinders erupted two hundred feet into the air, arcing in a majestic, unbroken parabolic stream far over the canyon cliffs and tumbling harmlessly into the gorge below.

Inside the locomotive cab, Sergeant Major Bradley watched the headlights carve through the darkness:

Where an impassable twelve-foot wall of snow had stood seconds before, a clean, pristine canyon of vertical white snowbanks opened up on either side of the rails. The flanged steel wheels of *The Iron Vanguard* clicked cleanly against the cold star-iron track, rolling forward without slowing an inch.

On the sharp cliffside horseshoe curves, the drop-off was terrifying: one thousand feet of sheer vertical drop to the frozen canyon floor below. But with thirty-two thousand pounds of continuous forward drawbar pull and the dynamic counter-balance of the spinning rotary wheel, the train remained glued to the rails.

*Milepost 138 cleared.*

*Milepost 140 cleared.*

*Milepost 142 cleared.*

At 1:30 AM, the train hit the most dangerous bottleneck of the pass: **The Hell-Gate Cut**.

Here, an avalanche of mixed snow, pine logs, and frozen volcanic ash had drifted twenty feet deep across four hundred yards of track.

The rotary wheel struck the tree trunks:

*CRUNCH-SNAP-ROAR!*

The forged-alloy knives sheared through eight-inch frozen pine logs, reducing wood and ice to matchsticks and sawdust, ejecting the debris through the roof chute in a roaring spray of brown woodchips and white snow.

The steam engine groaned; boiler pressure dipped to five hundred and twenty PSI; but Kovacs notched the steam injectors, maintaining cutting torque:

"Eat it up, you beauty!" Kovacs bellowed with joy, slapping the steel boiler casing. "Clear the line for the Chief!"

With a final, concussive heave of tractive power, *The Iron Vanguard* burst through the far side of Hell-Gate Cut into the open volcanic plateau.

Ahead, through the swirling snowfall, the steaming towers of Project Tartarus came into view, their orange flare stacks burning bright against the darkness.

At 2:15 AM, the rotary plow broke through the final snowbank directly outside the shattered gates of Project Tartarus.

Arthur Vance, standing inside the secured courtyard with the Chimera Frame Mark V gleaming on his arm, looked up from his pocket watch.

He watched the magnificent iron train roll smoothly onto the Tartarus siding, hauling the two heavy tankers carrying thirty thousand pounds of weighted barite mud and the auxiliary steam lines right to the wellhead threshold.

Arthur clicked open his gold-plated pocket watch.

*2:18 AM.*

"Total transit duration through ten-foot blizzard drifts: three hours, three minutes," Arthur Vance recorded in his ledger with his number-two pencil. "Rotary plow throughput: twelve point four tons of snow per second. Track clearance: one hundred percent. Mechanical casualties: zero."

Master Kovacs hopped down from the plow cab, laughing triumphantly into the snow, his beard coated in frost.

"We brought your mud, Arthur!" Kovacs shouted, slapping the side of the barite tanker. "And forty tons of high-pressure steam!"

Arthur gave the soot-stained blacksmith a crisp, admiring nod.

"Superb engineering execution, Joseph," Arthur said softly. "The supply line is secure."

Immediately, Arthur directed the engineering crews to hook up the preheating steam lines to the barite mud tankers:

"Inject low-pressure steam at fifteen PSI into the tanker thermal jackets," Arthur ordered. "Raise mud temperature to sixty-eight degrees Fahrenheit. If barite mud is injected into a wellhead at sub-zero temperatures, the fluid will suffer severe viscosity thickening, spiking pump discharge pressure past the burst rating of our delivery hoses."

The crew connected the heavy three-inch rubber hoses, the steam hissing through the jackets and warming the thick mineral slurry into fluid readiness.

Arthur pulled a handheld infrared pyrometer from his waistcoat, conducting a complete thermal scan of the rotary plow’s running gear:

"Rotary journal bearing temperature: one hundred and twelve degrees Fahrenheit," Arthur recorded, checking the phosphor-bronze housing. "Steam cylinder gland leakage: zero point zero percent. Cutter knife edge wear: zero point零three millimeters across all ten blades. The Leslie rotary plow has completed its design mission without structural compromise."

Corporal Davis stepped up to the mobile dispatch console on the train’s catwalk, tapping the key of the polar duplex telegraph:

```
HELL-GATE CUT CLEARED. 
MUD TANKERS DELIVERED TO PROJECT TARTARUS. 
WELLHEAD CONTAINMENT INFRASTRUCTURE ACTIVE. 
ADVANCING ON CENTRAL STEAM HALL.
```

The brass sounder clattered back in seconds:

```
MOUNT SENTINEL ACKNOWLEDGES. 
TECTONIC SWELLING CONTINUES. 
GODSPEED TO THE ARTIFICER.
```

Arthur turned to face the heavy blast doors of the Tartarus Central Machinery Hall, where the deep subterranean access shafts plunged toward the magma chamber.

Beside him, Eleanor stepped up, her green eyes glowing with tranquil golden starlight.

"The mud is here, Artie," Eleanor said softly, her breath clouding in the cold air. "The wellheads can be sealed. Now... let us deal with Slag-Hound Kenneth."

Arthur Vance adjusted his wire-rimmed spectacles, checked the six-thousand-PSI accumulator dials on the Chimera Frame Mark V, and drew his polycrystalline diamond chisel into battery.

He reached into his waistcoat, clicked open his gold-plated Elgin pocket watch, and noted the exact time:

*2:25 AM.*

"Total preparation window elapsed," Arthur recorded in his ledger. "All strategic reagents and motive units are in position. Accumulator pressure: six thousand PSI. Chemical mud preheated. The final assault on Project Tartarus commences immediately."

He looked at his team, gave a crisp nod, and stepped through the blast doors into the subterranean steam hall.

"The physics are aligned," Arthur Vance said calmly. "Let us proceed."
