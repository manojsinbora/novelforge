# Chapter 51: The Chimera Frame Mark IV

The smell of brine and crude oil blowing off the Great Salt Lake grew heavy as *The Iron Vanguard* emerged from the mouth of Wasatch Gap.

*Day 10 // 10:45 PM.*

Ahead lay the sprawling industrial expanse of the Promontory Point rail yard. Two miles of tangled steel tracks, rusted hopper cars, and soot-blackened water towers led toward the concrete perimeter walls of the Wasatch Petrochemical Complex. 

On the northern headland, the twin barrels of the 155-millimeter coastal artillery rifles were elevated into the night, their crews frantically stoking the coal-fired boilers of their traverse winches. But trapped beneath the eighty-foot limestone bluffs, the artillery was completely blind—the entire rail yard lay squarely within Arthur Vance’s calculated dead zone.

However, Ironfang Marcus Cole had not left the railyard undefended.

*CLANG-CLANG-CLANG!*

A screech of grinding steel gears and the deafening roar of an unmuffled supercharged marine diesel engine tore through the darkness.

Bursting through the doors of a locomotive maintenance shed at the western end of the yard was the Syndicate's ultimate ground weapon: 

**The Juggernaut "Goliath."**

It was a forty-five-ton tracked siege engine, built upon the chassis of a pre-Collapse Caterpillar D9 armored earthmover. 

Welded to its front was a massive, eight-inch-thick curved steel bulldozer blade, reinforced with structural I-beams and studded with six-foot hardened steel ramming spikes. The operator’s cabin was encased in three-inch laminated boiler plate with narrow vision slits of ballistic glass. Mounted in twin armored sponsons on the flanks were two high-speed 20-millimeter autocannons, spraying armor-piercing tracer rounds in wild, concussive bursts across the railyard.

The monstrous vehicle locked its heavy steel tracks onto the main rail line, throttling up its eight-hundred-horsepower twelve-cylinder diesel to maximum RPM.

With sixty tons of crushing kinetic momentum, the Goliath charged straight down Track Two, heading directly toward *The Iron Vanguard* on a head-on collision course at thirty miles per hour.

In the cab of the locomotive, Arthur Vance checked his pocket watch with unhurried precision.

*10:48 PM.*

"Target mass: forty-five metric tons," Arthur recorded in his mental logbook. "Velocity: forty-four feet per second. Kinetic energy: approximately one point三six megajoules. Impact with *The Iron Vanguard* will generate shock deceleration exceeding the shear strength of our front pilot truck bolster pins."

"Arthur!" Captain Cross shouted from the cab doorway, her knuckles white on her rifle sling. "Hit the emergency air! If that blade hits our plow at thirty miles an hour, the shock will derail both engines!"

"Deceleration would merely forfeit our tactical initiative," Arthur Vance responded calmly. He pulled the throttle lever back to notch one, reducing the locomotive's forward speed to an unhurried eight miles per hour.

He turned to Eleanor and Kovacs. "Joseph, maintain forward headway at eight miles per hour. Eleanor, stand by on the induction bus. I am executing field validation of the Mark IV chassis."

Arthur stepped out through the front vestibule door onto the locomotive's catwalk.

He had stripped away his wool field jacket. Strapped to his torso, right arm, and shoulder was the fully integrated **Chimera Frame Mark IV**.

Beneath the glare of *The Iron Vanguard’s* headlight, the Mark IV looked less like an exoskeleton and more like a surgical engine of destruction:

The load-bearing articulating struts were forged from the Salt-Crust Behemoth's biogenic nickel-chromium-iron tusk alloy, polished to a gunmetal sheen. Across the forearm ran the heavy twin cylinders of the **Closed-Loop Steam-Pneumatic Hybrid Accumulator**, its brass pressure dial steady at **Four Thousand Five Hundred PSI**. 

At the wrist assembly rested the four-inch hexagonal solid tungsten-carbide chisel, its edges diamond-honed to a molecular wedge angle of thirty-eight degrees.

On his left forearm was strapped the **Sovereign Aegis Induction Buckler**, its three star-iron concentric coils humming at five hundred and twenty-eight hertz, connected via a heavy flexible copper cable to the locomotive’s star-iron induction shunts.

The charging Juggernaut was eighty yards away.

Fifty yards.

Thirty yards.

The twin 20-millimeter autocannons on the Goliath raked the front of *The Iron Vanguard*. Heavy high-explosive shells exploded against the biogenic steel plow in blinding flashes of shrapnel and black smoke.

Arthur Vance did not flinch.

When the massive steel blade of the Juggernaut was barely fifteen feet from the locomotive's nose, Arthur vaulted off the catwalk.

His vulcanized rubber boots struck the top lip of *The Iron Vanguard’s* plow wedge, launching his body into the air with calculated athletic precision.

He cleared the ten-foot gap between the two charging vehicles, landing with a solid, cat-like crouch directly upon the curved steel glacis plate of the charging Juggernaut.

Inside the Goliath's cabin, the mutant driver saw Arthur land on his hood and screamed in terror, wildly hauling the steering levers to shake him loose.

Arthur’s left boot locked into the welded steel headlight bracket; his right knee braced against the engine air intake cowl.

Through his rubber safety goggles, Arthur’s analytical gaze instantly dissected the Goliath's mechanical architecture:

Directly beneath the armor cowl lay the engine's cast-iron cylinder head and the high-pressure fuel-injection manifold, operating at twenty-eight thousand PSI. The three-inch boiler plate protecting the engine was secured by six heavy one-and-a-half-inch alloy steel mounting bolts.

"Armor configuration: ASTM A36 structural plate," Arthur muttered aloud, his voice audible over the roar of the supercharged diesel. "Tensile yield: thirty-six thousand PSI. Failure mode: localized transverse shear."

Arthur planted his boots firmly upon the vibrating steel.

He raised his right arm, locking the **Chimera Frame Mark IV** directly perpendicular to the main armor seam above the engine block.

Inside the Mark IV's forearm accumulator, the micro-capacitors discharged seventy-five thousand volts across the flash-vaporization water chamber. The micro-droplet of distilled water flashed into four-hundred-and-fifty-degree superheated steam in three milliseconds, slamming against the primary pneumatic piston face.

The pressure gauge needle pegged past the red line: **Four Thousand Five Hundred PSI**.

Arthur drove his right fist downward.

He tripped the dual-action solenoid.

*KER-BOOOOOOOOOOOOM!*

The concussive shockwave was louder than an artillery shell.

**Fourteen Thousand One Hundred and Thirty-Seven Pounds of linear kinetic force** drove the hexagonal biogenic tungsten chisel forward.

*CRACK-SHATTER!*

The diamond-honed tungsten chisel did not merely penetrate the three-inch steel boiler plate—it punched through it like a hydraulic hole punch through tin foil.

The chisel drove eight inches deep, punching directly into the twelve-cylinder diesel engine's central cylinder block.

*CLANG-CRUNCH-KAPOW!*

The four-thousand-pound kinetic shock shattered the engine’s cast-iron head into a dozen jagged pieces. The primary camshaft snapped in half; the high-pressure common rail fuel line ruptured, spraying atomized diesel into the shattered crankcase; and six connecting rods seized simultaneously against the crankshaft in an explosive, catastrophic mechanical halt.

The supercharged diesel engine died instantly with a shuddering, violent clatter.

Deprived of motive power, the forty-five-ton Goliath skidded across the steel rails, its tracks locking solid in a spray of orange friction sparks, coming to a dead halt barely twelve feet in front of *The Iron Vanguard’s* biogenic plow.

Arthur Vance stood atop the smoking engine hood.

He rotated the Mark IV's wrist collar, disengaging the chisel with a smooth, pneumatic hiss.

He reached into his waistcoat, clicked open his gold-plated pocket watch, and noted the time.

*10:52 PM.*

"Total engagement duration: thirty-eight seconds," Arthur recorded in his calm, unhurried cadence. "Mark IV structural integrity: one hundred percent. Chisel deformation: zero point零one millimeters. Accumulator pressure recharge cycle: eighteen seconds."

He looked through the Goliath's ballistic vision slit.

Inside, the mutant driver and two gunners sat frozen in absolute, paralyzed terror, staring up at the quiet, bespectacled craftsman standing calmly upon their wrecked war machine like a phantom from an ancient age.

Arthur gave the vision slit a polite, professional tap with his tungsten chisel.

"Gentlemen," Arthur said through the ventilation grille. "Your engine block is mechanically totaled. Power down your weapon systems and exit the vehicle with your hands behind your heads."

The cabin hatch popped open within five seconds. The three mutant crewmen tumbled out onto the track ballast, face-down with their hands clamped over their necks.

Captain Cross and Sergeant Major Bradley dropped down from the locomotive catwalk, zip-tying the prisoners while Company B riflemen fanned out across the rail yard switches.

Kovacs walked over, his eyes wide as he stared at the clean, four-inch hexagonal puncture wound punched clean through three inches of solid armor plate.

"God almighty, Arthur," Kovacs breathed, whistling softly. "Fourteen thousand pounds of force... that chisel went through three-inch plate like warm butter."

"Basic kinetic physics, Joseph," Arthur replied, wiping a speck of diesel oil from his spectacles with his linen handkerchief. "Kinetic energy concentrated over a minimal surface area yields localized shear stresses that exceed the ultimate tensile strength of carbon steel by a factor of four. The material has no choice but to displace."

*Day 10 // 11:20 PM.*

While Company B secured the perimeter rail switches, Arthur Vance interrogated the captured Goliath driver inside the locomotive’s mobile machine shop.

The driver, a scarred raider named Caleb whose forearms had been grafted with crudely bolted steel reinforcement plates, sat shivering on a wooden stool, staring at Arthur's drafting instruments.

"Where is the scuttling mechanism, Caleb?" Arthur asked, his voice deadpan and conversational. "An industrial complex of this magnitude under the command of a paranoid executive director invariably incorporates an emergency self-destruct protocol."

Caleb swallowed hard, his eyes darting to the chisel resting on Arthur’s arm.

"The cracking unit..." Caleb stammered. "Ironfang rigged it three months ago. He ran high-pressure explosive slurry lines into the catalyst beds. If anyone breaches the inner citadel, he can pull a master pneumatic release valve in the main control room. It dumps forty tons of molten sulfur and burning methane into the platinum reactors. The whole refinery will go up like a small sun."

"And the workers?" Captain Cross demanded, stepping into the light.

"Six hundred slaves... technicians, distillers, families," Caleb whispered. "They're locked in Sub-Level Two of the lube-oil blending building. If the cracking towers blow, the toxic hydrogen sulfide fumes will kill every soul down there in less than two minutes."

Arthur turned to his tactical chalkboard, rapidly sketching a cross-section of the refinery:

"The continuous catalytic reforming unit operates under an internal pressure of three hundred and fifty PSI at nine hundred and fifty degrees Fahrenheit," Arthur noted. "The emergency scuttling manifold relies on pneumatic differential pressure. If we cut the main instrument air supply line leading from the compressor house, the fail-safe spring-return actuators on the reactor valves will automatically trip into the closed, safe position."

He looked at Eleanor, who stood beside the door, her green eyes fixed on the refinery skyline.

"Eleanor," Arthur said gently. "What does your starlight resonance detect?"

Eleanor closed her eyes, placing her palm against the steel hull of the train.

"The air in the cracking towers is thick with burning hydrocarbons," Eleanor said softly. "The captive workers are alive in Sub-Level Two. They're praying for a miracle. And in the catalyst vault... Silas is waiting. He’s standing right in front of the platinum reactors. His heart beats slow... like a giant stone hammer hitting an anvil."

Arthur nodded, wiping his hands on a clean cloth.

"We will execute a two-pronged infiltration," Arthur commanded. "Captain Cross and Bradley will lead Company B into Sub-Level Two via the stormwater runoff conduits to secure and evacuate the six hundred civilian technicians. Simultaneously, Eleanor and I will enter the catalytic cracking complex via the overhead steam pipe trestles to neutralize Silas, sever the instrument air lines, and secure the platinum catalyst."

Arthur Vance pulled out his pocket watch.

*11:35 PM.*

"Operation timeline: forty-five minutes to ingress," Arthur concluded. "Check your respirators and night optics. We take the refinery."
