# Chapter 61: The Ascent to the High Divide

The twin sixteen-cylinder De La Vergne diesels of the Baldwin-Westinghouse DR-12-8-1500 thrummed with a heavy, deliberate rumble that shook the frozen gravel ballast.

*Day 16 // 7:15 AM.*

On Track One of the Promontory Point marshalling yard, *The Iron Vanguard* stood prepared for the most punishing geographical ascent on the North American continent: the push north and east into the jagged canyons of the Snake River defile, climbing toward the Continental Divide.

The morning air had turned biting cold—twenty-eight degrees Fahrenheit. Crystals of hoarfrost coated the locomotive’s olive-drab flanks and the biogenic nickel-chromium steel plow bolted to its pilot truck. 

Coupled behind the 160-ton locomotive was an armored expeditionary unit train of eight specialized cars:

1. **The Heavy Auxiliary Tender:** Holding sixteen thousand gallons of winterized Wasatch diesel and ten thousand gallons of treated boiler feedwater.
2. **The Flak Car:** Mounting twin Browning M2 .50-caliber heavy machine guns in armored cupolas, crewed by veteran marksmen of Company B.
3. **The Mobile Metallurgy & Chemistry Workshop:** Equipped with Arthur’s precision lathe, chemical glassware hoods, and electric resistance furnace.
4. **The High-Pressure Steam Generator Car:** Housing an auxiliary flash-steam boiler to feed rotary snowplows and track-deicing manifolds.
5. **Two 50-Ton Boxcars:** Carrying sixty tons of Ceres Oasis wheat, dried citrus, and precision machine tools from Bunker 07.
6. **Two Armored Crew Coaches:** Accommodating forty riflemen under Captain Rachel Cross, Sergeant Major Hank Bradley, and Master Foundryman Joseph Kovacs.

Arthur Vance stood upon the ballast beside the rear drive truck, wearing his heavy wool field coat over his waistcoat, thick cowhide gloves, and clear rubber safety goggles. 

In his hand, he held an ultrasonic thickness gauge, pressing the transducer probe against the locomotive’s twelve forged-steel drive wheels:

"Flange wear across all twelve axles: zero point zero two inches," Arthur announced in his calm, measured baritone to Master Kovacs and Corporal Davis. "Tread contour is well within standard American Railway Association specifications. Wheel-to-rail adhesion coefficient in dry conditions: zero point二five. With high-pressure sanding jets active on grades exceeding two percent, adhesion will rise to zero point三二."

Kovacs blew into his cupped hands, his thick beard dusted with white frost.

"She’ll need every ounce of that adhesion, Arthur," Kovacs rumbled, pointing a thick thumb north toward the snow-choked gap in the Wasatch Range. "The old Oregon Short Line grade climbs through the Snake River gorge at an average of two point four percent, with ruling grades reaching two point eight five percent on the high horseshoe curves. You’re talking about lifting seven hundred tons of train four thousand vertical feet into the clouds."

"A two point eight five percent grade exerts a gravitational resistance of fifty-seven pounds per gross ton," Arthur calculated without hesitation, his pencil scribbling the equations into his open leather ledger:

$$R_{\text{grade}} = 20 \text{ lb/ton} \times 2.85 = 57.0 \text{ lb/ton}$$

$$R_{\text{train}} = R_{\text{friction}} + R_{\text{grade}} = (5.0 + 57.0) \text{ lb/ton} \times 700 \text{ tons} = 43,400 \text{ lbs of tractive resistance}$$

Arthur looked at Kovacs through his spectacles.

"The Baldwin-Westinghouse DR-12-8-1500 generates sixty-five thousand pounds of starting tractive effort and forty-eight thousand pounds of continuous tractive effort at twelve miles per hour," Arthur concluded. "Our tractive margin of safety is ten point six percent. The locomotives will not stall."

Elena Rostova walked up from the fuel rack, carrying a sample flask of pale straw-colored liquid:

"Arthur, we’ve winterized all sixteen thousand gallons of diesel fuel in the auxiliary tender," Rostova reported, adjusting her fur-lined parka. "Standard summer diesel contains long-chain paraffin waxes that crystallize into a thick gel at twenty degrees Fahrenheit, plugging fuel filters and starving the injectors. We’ve blended this batch with thirty percent light kerosene fraction and two percent poly-alpha-olefin pour-point depressant. The fuel pour-point is now minus thirty-eight degrees Fahrenheit. Even in a blizzard at eight thousand feet, your fuel will flow like water."

"Optimal chemical preparation, Elena," Arthur approved, testing the fuel viscosity with a falling-ball viscometer. "Thank you."

Corporal Davis rushed out from the Promontory station telegraph office, holding a fresh yellow dispatch tape.

"Chief Vance! Priority-Alpha wire from Mount Sentinel!" Davis shouted, his breath clouding in the cold. "Dr. Sean Chen reports massive seismic disturbances across the northern grid!"

Arthur took the dispatch tape, reading the typed transcription:

```

TO: CHIEF ARTIFICER ARTHUR VANCE // PROMONTORY TERMINAL
DATE: DAY 16 // 7:10 AM
```

Arthur Vance read the dispatch twice, his sharp gray eyes narrowing with cold analytical focus.

He reached into his waistcoat, clicked open his gold-plated Elgin pocket watch, and noted the time.

*7:20 AM.*

"The physics are unmistakable," Arthur stated to Captain Cross and Eleanor, who had stepped up to the locomotive pilot. "The Yellowstone Caldera represents the largest subterranean magma reservoir on the continent—over ten thousand cubic kilometers of molten rhyolitic magma under lithostatic pressure exceeding forty thousand pounds per square inch."

He turned to his sister, who was looking north toward the mountain wall.

"Anchor Node Five at Yellowstone is the central thermal pressure-relief and tectonic anchor of the First Dynasty planetary grid," Arthur explained. "Its five-hundred-and-twenty-eight-hertz field was designed to act as an electro-gravitic containment blanket, preventing deep-mantle plumes from breaching the crust. When the Covenant or tectonic drift destabilized its resonance to four hundred and twelve hertz, the containment field began converting kinetic vacuum energy directly into thermal heat."

Eleanor pressed her hand against her chest, where her Starlight Sovereign core hummed with warm, peaceful equilibrium. Her emerald eyes widened as she attuned her resonance toward the northern horizon.

"I can feel it, Artie," Eleanor whispered, her voice tight with awe and dread. "It’s like an immense subterranean furnace buried beneath five miles of rock. It’s choking on its own fire. And around it... there are men shouting into the ash. They're singing hymns to the volcano. They want it to erupt."

"Religious fanaticism born from technological ignorance," Captain Cross remarked grimly, chambering a round into her M4 carbine. "If that caldera blows, the ash cloud will bury the western hemisphere under six feet of toxic volcanic dust, blot out the sun for five years, and snuff out every farm in the Confederation."

"Which makes our tactical vector absolute," Arthur stated. "We will not permit a caldera detonation."

Arthur unrolled a topographical chart across an empty ammunition crate, pointing to the winding route:

"From Promontory Point, the Oregon Short Line rail corridor follows the Bear River north into Idaho, crosses the Snake River Basin at Pocatello, and ascends through the Snake River Canyon to Jackson Hole and the southern rim of the Yellowstone Plateau. Distance: two hundred and ten miles."

He turned to his drafting pad, sketching a preliminary cross-section of an upgraded combat frame:

"To operate in a superheated geothermal environment where ambient temperatures exceed six hundred degrees Fahrenheit and sulfuric acid steam is pervasive, the Chimera Frame Mark IV is thermally insufficient," Arthur observed. "Its rubber hydraulic seals and uninsulated pneumatic cylinders would suffer thermal degradation. While underway on the train, I will construct the **Chimera Frame Mark V: Cryo-Thermal Expansion Chassis**. We will incorporate vacuum-jacketed silica-aerogel insulation, high-temperature graphite packing, and increase accumulator working pressure to **Six Thousand PSI**."

He looked up at Sergeant Major Bradley and Captain Cross:

"Sergeant Major, issue high-altitude cold-weather gear to all personnel. We will encounter temperatures dropping to minus fifteen degrees Fahrenheit and snowdrifts up to ten feet deep. Kovacs, prime the track-deicing manifolds and check the steam boiler injectors."

"Understood, Arthur!" Kovacs barked, turning to the crew.

Arthur climbed the steel ladder into the cab of *The Iron Vanguard*, his rubber boots clicking firmly on the diamond-plate deck. He took his seat at the control stand, resting his hand upon the polished brass throttle.

He clicked open his pocket watch.

*7:30 AM.*

"Track clearance verified. Main reservoir: one hundred and forty PSI. Trainline brake pipe: ninety PSI."

Arthur pulled the throttle into notch two.

*WHHHHHOOOOOOOOO-WHHHHOOOOOOOOO!*

The great five-chime horn roared into the crisp mountain morning.

With sixty-five thousand pounds of tractive effort, *The Iron Vanguard* leaned into the grade, hauling seven hundred tons of steel, fuel, and men north toward the smoking peaks of the Continental Divide.

***

*Day 16 // 12:15 PM.*

By midday, the desert salt flats had vanished completely, replaced by the towering limestone bluffs of the Bear River defile and dense forests of snow-covered Douglas firs.

Inside the heated locomotive cab, Arthur Vance stood with one hand on the air brake valve and his eyes fixed on the rail alignment ahead.

The track was climbing steadily: past five thousand feet elevation, the air grew thinner and sharper. Through the front glass, patches of packed snow clung to the ties. Whenever the locomotive’s front drive trucks sensed a micro-slip on icy railheads, Arthur tripped the pneumatic sanding foot-pedal, spraying hot, dry silica sand directly beneath the front tires to maintain tractive friction.

On the auxiliary radio console, Corporal Davis passed a message from Elena Rostova back at Wasatch Depot:

"Refinery report: first shipment of thirty thousand gallons of diesel reached Fort Sterling on schedule. All nine hubs report zero interruptions on the telegraph network."

Arthur nodded, keeping his eyes on the track curve ahead.

Beside him, Eleanor sat on the brakeman's jump-seat, sipping hot tea from a tin mug. She pointed out the window to a high granite ridge two miles ahead, where a thin plume of pale yellow volcanic gas curled lazily into the gray winter sky.

"Sulfur dioxide," Arthur noted, checking the wind vector. "Atmospheric concentration: twelve parts per million. We are entering the outer geothermal influence zone."

He pulled out his gold pocket watch.

*12:20 PM.*

He opened his leather ledger, recording the journey’s progress with his number-two pencil:

*Elapsed travel time: 4 hours, 50 minutes.*  
*Mainline distance covered: 82.4 miles.*  
*Current elevation: 5,420 feet above sea level.*  
*Average grade: 2.15%.*  
*Fuel consumed: 340 gallons of winterized diesel.*  
*Locomotive engine temperature: 182°F (Optimal).*  
*Next waypoint: The Frozen Gorge of the Snake River.*

Arthur closed the ledger with a sharp click, adjusted his spectacles, and notched the throttle to notch four.

The great diesel engines roared, driving the train deeper into the frozen northern canyons.
