# Chapter 20: The Battle of High Rock

The iron wheels of Depot Shunter Number 7 ground to a halt fifty yards outside the southern perimeter of High Rock Commune with a heavy, pressurized hiss of train-line air.

*6:05 PM.*

The twilight sun was sinking behind the dark pine ridges of the Blackwood Mountains, casting a blood-red glow over the smoking fields. 

In the center of the five-hundred-acre commune stood the **Great Granary**—a colossal, two-story structure constructed from dry-stacked mountain fieldstone and heavy hand-hewn cedar timbers. 

Beneath its foundation, the ground was boiling.

Dozens of conical trenches, each six feet wide, had been gouged into the fertile black soil around the stone foundation walls. From these trenches erupted plumes of steaming green acid vapor, accompanied by a rhythmic, sickening crunching sound that vibrated right through Arthur’s boots:

*CRUNCH-GRIND, CRUNCH-GRIND.*

The fieldstone footings of the south wall had already settled six inches into the churned earth. Two of the massive cedar corner posts were splintering under uneven stress, and inside the barn, two hundred voices were screaming in terror as cracks raced across the wooden floorboards.

Dr. Ward scrambled down the locomotive pilot ladder, his medical bag slung over his shoulder, his eyes wide with horror. "Arthur! The foundation is being undermined from below! If that south wall gives way, the roof collapses into the pit!"

Beside him, Eleanor stepped onto the gravel ballast. 

She closed her eyes, extending both hands toward the churning ground. The sapphire glyphs along her wrists flared with intense, warning brilliance.

"Arthur," Eleanor whispered, her voice tight with urgency. "It's not just a swarm. There’s something massive beneath the center of the barn. A Brood-Mother... its body is thirty feet long, covered in thick calcified plates. It’s chewing out the bedrock beneath the foundation to drop the building into its nest!"

Arthur Vance didn't flinch. 

He unlatched the leather straps of his toolbag, checked the pressure gauges on the **Chimera Frame Mark II**, and looked at the four-inch steel rails beneath the locomotive’s thirty-three-inch drive wheels.

"Subterranean detritivores possess zero optic vision," Arthur analyzed aloud, his voice flat, steady, and clinical. "Their predatory orientation relies exclusively on tactile seismoreception—vibrational frequencies transmitted through soil and rock. They attack the barn because two hundred human heartbeats generate a continuous low-frequency kinetic signature in the timber floor."

He turned to Miller in the locomotive cab.

"Miller! Disconnect the locomotive’s auxiliary traction motor isolation switch! Lock the air brakes on all cars, open the rail sanders, and rev the Caterpillar diesels to Notch Eight!"

Miller blinked in confusion. "Notch Eight?! With the brakes locked?! We'll burn through the brake shoes!"

"We aren't trying to move," Arthur said. "We are transforming forty-four tons of cast iron and eight hundred horsepower into a seismic tuning fork. The vibrations traveling through sixty-pound steel rails will generate an acoustic signature fifty times greater than the barn."

Miller threw the switches, grabbed the brass throttle stand, and hauled it to full battle power.

*WROOOOOOOOOOOOM!*

The twin sixteen-liter diesels howled like a pair of caged lions. 

The heavy direct-current traction generators surged, sending massive electric torque into the locked wheel motors. The thirty-three-inch steel drive wheels strained against the iron brake shoes, vibrating against the rails with a deafening, bone-shattering mechanical thrum that sent shockwaves rippling through the granite bedrock for half a mile:

*THUM-THUM-THUM-THUM-THUM!*

The effect on the battlefield was instantaneous.

Around the cracking foundations of the great barn, the churning of the earth suddenly stopped.

From dozens of smoking trenches in the pasture, grotesque armored heads emerged from the soil. The **Grave-Chitin Burrowers**—dozens of them, their rotating calcified mandibles dripping with green bile—turned their blind snouts away from the barn.

To their sensitive subterranean vibrational receptors, the localized rumbling of two hundred trapped humans was suddenly drowned out by the colossal, seismic heartbeat of forty-four tons of roaring diesel iron fifty yards away.

With a collective, wet chittering shriek, the swarm abandoned the granary.

Over forty armored burrowers tore through the topsoil, converging on the railroad tracks like a swarm of army ants marching toward a slaughter.

"Davis! The irrigation ditch!" Arthur commanded.

Running parallel to the rail line, five paces from the tracks, was a concrete irrigation canal feeding fresh water from the Blackwood River to the vegetable fields.

Davis sprinted to the flatbed car, grabbed a heavy fifty-foot length of bare copper grounding cable Arthur had prepared, and jammed the brass clamp directly into the positive busbar of the locomotive's 400-volt direct-current auxiliary generator.

He hurled the bare copper cable straight into the water of the irrigation canal.

*BZZZZZZZZZT!*

Four hundred volts of direct current flooded through the running water.

The lead wave of burrowers—over thirty creatures—scrambled through the wet ditch to reach the tracks.

The second their chitin-plated legs touched the electrified water, their biological nervous systems convulsed. 

Four hundred volts bridged through their moist, acidic shell joints. Their muscles locked in violent, fatal spasms; their digestive acid boiled in their stomachs; and over two dozen beasts flipped onto their armored backs, paralyzed and dead in the foaming water within ten seconds.

"The swarm is broken!" Davis cheered, racking his shotgun. "Look at 'em fry!"

"Hold your fire," Arthur said softly.

The ground between the pasture and the tracks suddenly heaved upward like an exploding volcano.

*KABOOOOM.*

A sixty-foot geyser of black soil and shattered irrigation pipe erupted into the air.

Emerging from the deep subterranean shale was the **Brood-Mother**.

She was a subterranean nightmare thirty feet long and six feet wide—an ancient Tier 2 Peak leviathan whose segmented body was encased in thick, overlapping plates of chalk-white calcified chitin eight inches thick. 

Her massive head was dominated by eight concentric rings of rotating, diamond-hard bone drill teeth that spun with the deafening roar of an industrial tunneling machine:

*WHEEEEEEE-CRUNCH-CHURN!*

From her throat poured a steaming, geyser-like torrent of concentrated green digestive acid that melted the iron railroad fence ten yards away like wax under a blowtorch.

The Brood-Mother sensed the locomotive. 

She coiled her massive, segmented body in the mud, raised her colossal drill-head into the air, and surged forward toward the tracks to derail the iron train.

Arthur Vance stepped down from the pilot ladder.

He didn't run. He didn't flinch.

He walked out onto the wooden rail ties, stood dead center between the rails twenty paces in front of the locomotive, and planted his boots firmly into the crushed ballast.

*SNICK-CLACK.*

On his left arm, the **Sovereign Aegis** buckler expanded, locking its Star-Chitin plates into an impenetrable slate-gray shield.

On his right arm, the twin pneumatic cylinders of the **Kinetic Pile-Driver** pressurized to twenty-four hundred PSI, its two-inch tungsten-carbide chisel humming with thirty-five thousand volts of starlight lightning.

Beside him, Eleanor stepped to the edge of the flatbed. 

She reached into the air with both hands, channeling her stabilized bio-current directly into Arthur’s bracer.

*HUMMMMMMM.*

A blinding ring of sapphire light encircled Arthur’s feet, illuminating the dark valley in a halo of pure celestial harmony.

The Brood-Mother lunged.

Thirty tons of armored chitin and spinning bone drills hurtled toward Arthur at twenty miles per hour, aiming to grind him and the tracks into dust.

*Distance: twenty feet. Ten feet. Five feet.*

Arthur waited until the monster's rotating drill jaws were two feet from his face, exposing the soft, unarmored cartilage seam where her dorsal armor met her primary neural ganglion.

Arthur struck.

*PSSSHT-CRACK! PSSSHT-CRACK!*

The pneumatic pile-driver fired with explosive, machine-gun rapidity.

Twelve hundred pounds of linear impact force drove the hardened tungsten-carbide chisel through the creature's eight-inch calcified neck plate like a railroad spike through dry pine.

*CRUUUUUNCH.*

The armored plate shattered in an explosion of white bone shards. The chisel sank six inches deep into the beast's primary braincase.

Simultaneously, Arthur depressed the thumb switch and held it down.

"Sovereign discharge: maximum," Arthur whispered.

*BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM!*

Thirty-five thousand volts of starlight-amplified celestial lightning exploded through the Brood-Mother's spinal cord.

A blinding pillar of sapphire and gold light erupted from the creature's shattered neck, illuminating the entire five-hundred-acre commune like midday.

The Brood-Mother's spinning drill jaws gave a final, violent shriek as her central nervous system vaporized into ash. Every segment along her thirty-foot body arched rigid in the air, shuddered once, and crashed sideways across the pasture with an earth-shaking thud that sent dust billowing fifty feet into the evening air.

The beast was dead.

Silence descended upon High Rock Commune.

The roaring Caterpillar diesels in the switcher locomotive idled down with a gentle, purring chug: *chug-thump, chug-thump.* The cool mountain wind rustled through the tall grass, carrying away the bitter scent of ozone and acid smoke.

Arthur lowered his right arm.

He pulled a clean cotton handkerchief from his waistcoat pocket, wiped a speck of white bone dust from his clear rubber safety goggles, and clicked open his brass pocket watch.

*6:28 PM.*

"Engagement duration: twenty-three minutes," Arthur stated calmly to the empty tracks. "Total ammunition expenditure: zero rounds. Subterranean threat: neutralized."

Behind the shattered fences, the heavy timber doors of the great granary slowly creaked open.

Two hundred men, women, and children filtered out into the twilight, their faces pale, soot-streaked, and wet with disbelieving tears.

Leading them was an elderly man in a sheepskin vest and mud-spattered boots, clutching a heavy Winchester rifle with trembling hands. He had silver hair, a weathered face lined with decades of mountain soil, and sharp gray eyes that looked astonishingly familiar.

It was **Silas Vance**—the founder of the commune and the cousin of Arthur’s late father.

Silas walked slowly through the smoking pasture, staring at the thirty-foot carcass of the Brood-Mother, then at the smoking iron locomotive, and finally up at Arthur Vance standing in his neat gray archivist's smock with dual gauntlets of alien chitin and star-iron glinting on his arms.

Silas stopped three paces away, his jaw trembling as he lowered his rifle into the grass.

"Arthur..." the old man whispered, his voice cracking with awe and raw emotion. "Edward's boy... the librarian. They said... the radio said a Ghost of the Mountain was fighting Marcus Cole with lightning..."

"The radio reports were prone to unverified hyperbole," Arthur said smoothly, adjusting his wire-rimmed spectacles. "I am merely the municipal assistant. Your agricultural facilities were experiencing an acute structural and biological infestation. We had thirty minutes before dinner, so we cleared the obstruction."

Silas let out a wild, breathless laugh of sheer relief, wiping his sleeve across his eyes, before stepping forward and clapping Arthur’s shoulder with both calloused hands.

"You saved us, boy," Silas said, looking back at the cheering, weeping crowd of two hundred farmers behind him. "Every silo is intact. Fifty tons of grain, thirty greenhouses of seed potatoes, and our fresh dairy stock. High Rock is yours, Arthur. Anything Bunker Zero-Seven needs, it's yours."

"A mutual resource exchange will be beneficial," Arthur agreed, nodding once. "Fifty tons of flour and seed stock in exchange for sixty kilowatts of geothermal electrical distribution and medical stabilizers."

Silas looked north toward the dark, towering gorge where the Blackwood River flowed down from the high peaks.

"Arthur," Silas said, his voice dropping into a serious, conspiratorial whisper. "If you're heading north... you need to know about the dam."

Arthur’s eyes sharpened. "The Iron Ridge Hydro-Dam."

"Three days ago, the river changed," Silas said, pointing toward the rushing rapids. "It started glowing blue at night. And our shortwave receiver picked up automated military telemetry coming from the dam's powerhouse. Someone is inside that dam, Arthur. Someone with heavy armor and pre-cataclysmic access codes."

Arthur looked north toward the rugged, snow-capped peaks of the mountain divide.

The **Second Star-Iron Pylon** was waiting.

Arthur reached into his waistcoat, touched the small copper grounding charm Eleanor had woven for him, and clicked his brass pocket watch shut.

*7:28 PM.*

He looked back at Eleanor, Dr. Ward, and Miller, his gray eyes shining with calm, brilliant purpose in the starlight.

"Pack the seed grain into Car 42," Arthur Vance said.

"Tomorrow morning, we take the iron train to the dam."
