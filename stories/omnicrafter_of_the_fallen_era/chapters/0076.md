# Chapter 76: Descent into the Bighorn Basin

The eastern descent from the Continental Divide began under an expanse of slate-gray winter sky that stretched unbroken toward the infinite horizon of the Great Plains.

*Day 21 // 6:15 AM.*

At an altitude of eight thousand feet, the air was razor-thin and bitterly cold, registering minus twenty-four degrees Fahrenheit on the external bimetallic dial thermometers mounted to the lead pilot car of the armored train *Prometheus*. 

The transition from the rugged granite sanctuary of the high Rockies down toward the vast, windswept plains of the east was marked by a dramatic shift in both topography and atmospheric pressure. Barometric pressure climbed steadily from twenty-two point four inches of mercury at Togwotee Pass to twenty-six point one inches as the train descended through the foothills of the Wind River Range into the upper reaches of the Bighorn Basin.

Arthur Vance sat at the polished walnut drafting desk in the forward observation coach, his brass pocket watch open beside a fresh, unbound folio of twenty-four-pound rag paper.

With a precision-sharpened mechanical pencil, Arthur drew a clean horizontal reference datum across the page, charting the vertical track profile of the abandoned pre-Collapse Chicago, Burlington and Quincy railroad right-of-way that wound down the canyon:

$$\text{Grade Percentage } G = \frac{\Delta h}{L} \times 100$$

$$\Delta h = 3,420\text{ ft}, \quad L = 142,500\text{ ft} \quad (27.0\text{ miles})$$

$$G = \frac{3,420}{142,500} \times 100 = 2.40\%$$

"A sustained two-point-four-percent descending grade over twenty-seven continuous statute miles," Arthur murmured aloud, his voice steady beneath his wool scarf. "Manageable under standard pneumatic braking protocols, provided wheel-tread thermal dissipation does not exceed seventy-five thousand foot-pounds per minute per brake shoe."

Naomi Tsosie’s assistant engineer, a skilled Shoshone mechanic named Daniel Teton, leaned over the secondary console, adjusting the lubricator drip valves on the Westinghouse air pump.

"We ran the pilot scout car five miles ahead of the *Prometheus* at dawn, Arthur," Daniel reported, tapping the telegraph sounder. "The roadbed is solid pre-Collapse crushed granite ballast, but the steel rails from the old era are in terrible shape. Three centuries of freeze-thaw cycles have thrown the alignment out of true. We've got frost heaves pushing track joints up by three inches every half-mile, and the joint bar bolts are eighty percent rusted through."

"Which is why the forward tracklayer car is leading the consist," Arthur replied, not looking up from his calculations. "Master Kovacs’ crews will replace sub-standard rail segments with our one-hundred-and-thirty-six-pound star-iron stock as we descend. We do not advance faster than we can guarantee a zero-derailment margin of safety."

Beside the drafting desk stood Eleanor Vance.

She wore a tailored winter traveling cloak of deep charcoal wool lined with insulated rabbit pelt, clasped at the throat by a polished star-iron brooch. Her Mid-Tier Sovereign starlight radiated a soft, ambient glow through the coach, keeping the interior temperature at an even sixty-eight degrees Fahrenheit despite the howling sub-zero blizzard battering the triple-glazed armored windows.

Eleanor placed a gentle hand on the glass pane, looking out over the endless expanse of the Bighorn Basin.

"It feels so empty out there, Arthur," Eleanor whispered softly. "In the mountains, the stone was alive with the pulse of the pylon. Even when Anchor Node Five was suffering at four hundred and twelve Hertz, you could feel the immense weight of the earth humming beneath your feet. But down there... the plains feel quiet. Almost abandoned."

"An optical and acoustic illusion caused by topographical flatness and low population density," Arthur explained, rotating his drafting triangle. "The Bighorn Basin encompasses approximately nine thousand square miles of semi-arid plateau, bounded by the Absaroka Range to the west and the Bighorn Mountains to the east. Historically, it supported extensive petroleum extraction, bentonite mining, and livestock grazing. Following the Collapse, lack of centralized irrigation and the collapse of the fuel distribution networks forced the population into nomadic pastoralism or concentrated defensive enclaves along the river bottoms."

Arthur checked his pocket watch.

*6:48 AM.*

"Speed is currently eight point five miles per hour. We will reach the first established water stop at the confluence of the Greybull and Bighorn rivers in approximately four hours and twenty minutes."

The armored train *Prometheus* crawled down the eastern mountain pass with steady, iron majesty.

At the head of the formation, the massive wedge snowplow shoved eight-foot drifts of wind-crusted powder off the tracks, sending sparkling white geysers cascading fifty feet down into the rocky gorges. Directly behind the plow, the hydraulic track-straightening carriage engaged: heavy hydraulic tamping tines plunged into the frozen ballast, vibrating at sixty Hertz to shatter the frost ice and re-seat the oak cross-ties, while dual star-iron rollers pressed the rails back into absolute standard-gauge alignment.

Every five hundred yards, where ancient carbon-steel rails were cracked or sheared by thermal contraction, the train’s onboard gantry crane swung out a thirty-nine-foot section of fresh star-iron rail from Vulcan Junction. Confederation tracklayers, operating under the rapid rhythmic commands of Sergeant Major Hank Bradley, drove treated star-iron spikes into the ties with rhythmic, synchronized strikes of twenty-pound sledgehammers:

*CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!*

"Drive 'em true, boys!" Bradley’s gravelly voice bellowed across the snowy canyon. "Standard gauge! Four feet eight and a half inches! If a caliper slips by a sixteenth of an inch, you'll answer to the Archivist and his micrometer!"

Inside the command car, Arthur Vance stepped into his mobile workshop to inspect the **Chimera Frame Mark V**.

The frame stood mounted on its heavy oak maintenance rig. Arthur methodically examined every titanium-niobium strut, applying a thin film of low-temperature fluorosilicone grease to the needle bearings of the hip and shoulder gimbals. He connected a portable hand pump to the Closed-Loop Steam-Pneumatic Hybrid Accumulator, testing the pressure-relief valve seals:

"Accumulator holding steady at six thousand PSI," Arthur recorded in his maintenance log. "Zero seal creep detected across seventy-two hours of sub-zero thermal cycling. Vacuum-jacketed aerogel blankets show zero interstitial degradation. The chassis is fully operational for field deployment."

Arthur slung the control harness over his shoulders, buckling the padded leather chest straps and linking the neural inductive telemetry leads to the collar of his waistcoat. The frame hummed softly against his spine, its bio-mechanical feedback loops seamlessly syncing with his natural posture.

At 10:15 AM, as the train emerged from the mouth of the Wind River Canyon onto the broad alluvial plains of the Bighorn Basin, the telegraph sounder in the command car began chattering in rapid, erratic bursts:

*DIT-DIT-DIT... DAH-DAH-DAH... DIT-DIT-DIT...*

Captain Rachel Cross stepped swiftly into the compartment, her snow-dusted parka unzipped to reveal her tactical webbing and sidearm.

"Arthur, Eleanor," Cross said, holding a freshly transcribed telegraph slip. "The forward scout patrol on the rail-speeder just intercepted an urgent radio broadcast on the low-band emergency distress channel. Frequency one-hundred-and-fifty-six point eight megahertz."

Arthur reached for his headset and flipped the audio selector switch:

Through a wall of static and atmospheric crackle, a desperate human voice cut through the speaker:

*‘...Mayday, mayday! This is Station Forty-Two at the Greybull crossing... We are under heavy mechanized attack! Raiders from the eastern river trail... They have six armed technicals and a tracked steam crawler... They breached the outer stockade! We have forty women and children trapped in the grain elevator! If anyone can hear this, please... they’re torching the fuel tanks!’*

A deafening roar of automatic gunfire and an explosion shook the transmission, followed by the screech of grinding steel treads, before the radio link went dead.

Eleanor stepped forward, her violet eyes hardening with fierce resolve. "Station Forty-Two... how far ahead are they, Captain?"

"Six miles down the track, right at the river bridge," Cross answered, checking her map. "At our current tracklaying speed of eight miles per hour, the *Prometheus* won't get there for forty-five minutes. That grain elevator will be a funeral pyre in fifteen."

Arthur Vance stood up from the drafting table.

He unhitched the Chimera Frame Mark V from its stationary rig, testing the leg locks with two sharp, metallic clicks against the steel deck.

"Tracklaying speed is insufficient for tactical crisis intervention," Arthur said calmly. "However, the high-speed armored rail-speeder car coupled behind the tender possesses a twin-turbocharged Cummins diesel engine capable of fifty-five miles per hour on cleared track."

Arthur pulled his gold pocket watch, clicked the hunter case open, and checked the second hand.

*10:22 AM.*

"Distance: six point two miles. At forty-eight miles per hour, transit duration will be seven minutes and forty-five seconds. Mark."

Arthur snapped the pocket watch shut and slipped it into his vest.

"Captain Cross, assemble your four best marksmen with scoped semi-automatic rifles. Eleanor, prepare your starlight dispersion shield to suppress incendiary munitions. We will deploy on the rail-speeder immediately and resolve this breach of regional peace."

Within ninety seconds, the high-speed armored rail-speeder detached from the rear of the *Prometheus*. 

Its diesel engine roared to life with a throaty, turbocharged growl. The lightweight eight-wheel speeder accelerated down the newly tamped rails, its dual star-iron cowcatchers parting the light powdery snow on the tracks as it surged toward Greybull crossing at forty-eight miles per hour.

Through the forward armored vision slits, Arthur observed the smoke plumes rising against the gray winter sky.

At five miles, the burning stockade of Station Forty-Two came into sharp tactical focus.

The settlement was built around a massive pre-Collapse reinforced-concrete grain elevator and an old wooden railroad water tower beside the frozen Greybull River. The perimeter timber wall was shattered, crushed inward by the treads of an ugly, smoke-belching twelve-ton tracked steam crawler. Encircling the grain elevator were six rusted four-wheel-drive technicals—pickup trucks retrofitted with boilerplate steel cab armor and bed-mounted heavy machine guns.

Dozens of raiders clad in grease-stained wolf pelts and scavenged sheet metal were lobbing bottles of flaming kerosene against the heavy steel doors of the grain elevator.

"They're trying to cook them inside," Cross growled, racking the bolt of her heavy scoped precision rifle. "Sergeant Bradley, target the gunners on the pickup beds! Fire on my mark!"

"Hold fire on the vehicles," Arthur interrupted calmly. He reached for the pneumatic throttle of the rail-speeder’s air brakes. "A vehicle is a collection of repairable spare parts. Kinetic destruction of their prime movers is wasteful. Eleanor, deploy your starlight vortex to extinguish the incendiaries. Captain Cross, disable their tires and radiators with subsonic rifle fire. I will address the steam crawler."

At four hundred yards from the crossing, the rail-speeder sounded its high-frequency pneumatic horn:

*HONK-HONK!*

The raiders whipped around in shock, staring wide-eyed at the armored silver rail-speeder bearing down upon them on tracks they believed to have been dead for centuries.

"What in the hell is that?!" one of the raider technical gunners screamed, swinging a rusted fifty-caliber Browning machine gun toward the tracks.

Before the gunner could pull the butterfly trigger, Captain Cross and her snipers fired:

*PHUT! PHUT! PHUT!*

Four suppressed shots rang out. The radiator blocks and front tires of two technicals ruptured with violent hissings of coolant and shredding rubber. The vehicles slumped uselessly into the snowdrifts, their engines seizing within seconds.

Simultaneously, Eleanor stepped out onto the open front platform of the speeding rail-speeder.

She raised both hands toward the burning grain elevator.

*SHUUUUU-VOOOOM!*

A brilliant, shimmering wave of 528 Hz starlight cascaded across the snowy courtyard. The moment the coherent golden field enveloped the burning timber doors, the combustion reaction was starved of ionized activation energy. The roaring kerosene flames sputtered, flickered, and died in less than two seconds, smothered into harmless white steam.

"What kind of sorcery is this?!" the raider chieftain roared from the cupola of the tracked steam crawler. He slammed his fist down on the traverse lever, swinging a heavy four-inch smoothbore demolition cannon toward the approaching rail-speeder. "Blow that train car to scrap!"

The rail-speeder screeched to a halt eighty feet from the crawler.

Arthur Vance did not wait for the dust to settle.

He vaulted over the low railing of the speeder, his boots hitting the packed snow with a muted crunch.

The **Chimera Frame Mark V** engaged with an explosive burst of pneumatic torque. The Ti-45Nb-5Zr leg struts launched Arthur across the sixty feet of snow in two breathtaking strides.

The raider chieftain fired his demolition cannon:

*BOOOOOM!*

A five-pound canister of black powder and scrap metal erupted from the cannon barrel.

Arthur did not dive. He angled the **Sovereign Aegis buckler** on his left forearm.

*VZZZZT-CLANG!*

The electromagnetic induction coils of the Aegis flared bright blue, deflecting the hail of shrapnel harmlessly into the frozen mud while harvesting forty-five hundred joules of kinetic energy directly into the frame's storage capacitors.

Arthur closed the remaining fifteen feet in a split second.

He stepped directly beside the crawler's left front drive sprocket—a massive cast-iron wheel bearing the full mechanical load of the vehicle's caterpillar tracks.

"Cast iron possesses low fracture toughness under sub-zero temperatures," Arthur stated aloud, his voice clinical and calm beneath his respirator. "Applying six thousand PSI of localized shear impact."

Arthur leveled the hexagonal biogenic tungsten chisel of the Mark V directly against the drive axle hub.

He pulled the trigger.

*KABOOOOOM!*

The Closed-Loop Accumulator discharged eighteen thousand eight hundred and fifty pounds of kinetic force. The polycrystalline diamond compact (PDC) chisel struck the frozen cast-iron hub with an ear-splitting crack.

*SHATTER-CRUNCH!*

The entire drive sprocket shattered into six jagged chunks of brittle metal. 

The heavy steel crawler track detached with a violent, whipping snap, uncoiling across the snow like a dead snake. Deprived of drive tension, the twelve-ton steam crawler lurched violently to the left, buried its nose into the frozen ditch, and stalled with a loud hiss of blowing boiler safety valves.

Arthur reached through the open access hatch, pulled the primary boiler fuel-cutoff valve closed with his bare left hand, and shut down the engine.

Silence descended over the courtyard of Station Forty-Two.

The remaining raiders dropped their weapons, falling to their knees in the snow in trembling terror as they stared at the charcoal-suited archivist whose mechanical arm had just dismantled a twelve-ton war machine in three seconds.

"The Ghost Artificer..." one of the technical drivers stammered, his teeth chattering from fear and the sub-zero cold. "It's the Ghost from the mountains..."

Arthur Vance took his pocket watch from his waistcoat, clicked the case open, and noted the time.

*10:29:45 AM.*

"Total engagement duration: seven minutes and forty-five seconds," Arthur recorded in his brass-bound ledger with his mechanical pencil. "Casualties: zero fatalities. Raider equipment immobilized: six light transport vehicles, one steam tractor. Structural integrity of grain elevator: one hundred percent preserved."

Arthur clicked his pencil shut and looked at the shivering raiders.

"Drop your belt knives and ammunition pouches into the center of the yard," Arthur instructed evenly. "You will clear the snow from the railroad roadbed under the supervision of Captain Cross. Constructive labor begins at once."

The heavy reinforced doors of the grain elevator creaked open.

Forty frightened, weeping settlers—farmers, children, and elderly elders—stepped out into the sunlight. At their head was a weathered farmer in a sheepskin coat, staring in utter disbelief at the golden-lit girl and the calm archivist who had saved their lives.

"Who... who are you people?" the farmer whispered, his voice trembling.

Eleanor stepped forward, extending a warm, comforting hand. Her starlight glowed with gentle reassurance.

"We are the Western Confederation," Eleanor said with a warm smile. "And the railway has returned."
