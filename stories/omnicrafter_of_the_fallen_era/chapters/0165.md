# Chapter 165: The Abyssal Accord

The soaring Beaux-Arts barrel vault of Denver Union Station rose sixty-eight feet above the polished terrazzo concourse, its historic limestone arches reinforced by ribbed star-iron gussets and high-tensile tie-rods sunk twelve feet into Colorado pink granite. Originally constructed in 1881 and rebuilt after the fire of 1894, the grand passenger hall had stood for decades as the historic gateway to the American West; now, stripped of post-calamity rubble, retrofitted with crystalline zero-point luminescent fixtures, and linked to eighteen standard-gauge classification tracks, it functioned as the supreme continental fulcrum of North America. Through the towering east-facing arched windows, morning sunlight spilled across sixty thousand square feet of bustling logistical concourse, illuminating the arriving delegations of nineteen regional commonwealths.

Outside, in the sprawling rail yards where the high plains of the South Platte River met the abrupt, razor-sharp uplift of the Front Range, the rhythmic mechanical percussion of arriving freight consists rattled the frosted switches. For twenty-two years, the Continental Divide had stood as an impassable physical barrier—a frozen, jagged wall of granite and fractured void caverns that split the continent into two dying halves. Western human redoubts in the Pacific Northwest and the Great Basin had starved for industrial star-iron, machine lubricants, and electrical conduits; eastern enclaves in the Midwest and Atlantic tidewaters had suffocated under deep-crust seismic upheavals and freezing Siberian blizzards. 

Now, through the newly bored Moffat and Berthoud deep-level star-iron tunnels, those severed halves were fused. Two four-thousand-horsepower zero-point locomotives—the *Archival Sovereign* from the Pacific coast and the *Atlantic Pioneer* from the Chesapeake tidewaters—stood buffer-to-buffer on Track One, their pressurized pneumatic lines coupled into an unbroken transcontinental circuit.
