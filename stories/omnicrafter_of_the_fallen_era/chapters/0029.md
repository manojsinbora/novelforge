# Chapter 29: The Ascent into the Sky-Rift

The wind on the exterior catwalk of Sentinel Observatory roared like an uncaged jet engine.

*Day 4 // 1:24 PM.*

Sheet ice two inches thick coated the galvanized steel mesh of the high walkway. Sixty feet below lay the rim of Crater Stellaris, glowing with the ominous, boiling violet luminescence of ungrounded star-iron. Overhead, the storm vortex had tightened into a blinding spiral of ionizing gas, dropping temperatures to minus twenty-two degrees Fahrenheit.

Arthur Vance stood at the catwalk’s outer observation parapet, his boots clamped to the steel grating with serrated crampons. 

Lined along the railing behind him was four hundred feet of four-ought **copper welding cable**, spliced directly into the observatory’s three-inch solid copper lightning grounding grid. The business end of the cable was clamped with a heavy bronze lug into the underside of Arthur’s left-arm **Sovereign Aegis** buckler.

Beside Arthur, Eleanor stood braced against the gale. The Starlight Sovereign resonance within her had reached a pitch so high and pure that faint golden auroral rings orbited her hands, vibrating in direct counter-phase to the storm above.

"It has sensed us, Arthur," Eleanor said, her voice carrying an uncanny acoustic clarity over the screaming blizzard. "It doesn't just eat lightning. It feels the pylon's hunger. It thinks we are trying to steal its food."

Arthur reached into his coat, clicked open his pocket watch with his gloved thumb, and checked the second hand.

*1:27 PM.*

"A predatory response rooted in metabolic territoriality," Arthur said evenly. "Its reliance on atmospheric plasma makes its behavior predictable. When it dives, it will follow the highest electrical potential gradient."

From the roof of the loading bay eighty yards to their left, Captain Rachel Cross gave the hand signal.

Sergeant Marcus Cole racked the charging handle of the M2 Browning .50-caliber machine gun mounted on the lead Humvee's armored ring.

"Browning hot!" Cole yelled across the radio link. "Belt loaded: four armor-piercing incendiary rounds to one tracer! Give the word, Vance!"

"Engage the wing roots," Arthur ordered into his throat mic. "Disrupt its aerodynamic balance. Force it into linear descent."

*THUD-THUD-THUD-THUD-THUD!*

The heavy M2 opened up with a deafening, rhythmic hammering that echoed off the granite peaks like rolling thunder. White-hot lines of .50-caliber incendiary tracers carved through the blizzard, slamming into the upper dome where the **Sky-Rift Wyrm** was coiled around the telescope aperture.

The half-inch tungsten-core penetrators detonated in brilliant magnesium flashes against the wyrm's midnight-blue carapace. While the thick dorsal plates resisted total penetration, the concussive kinetic energy sheared through the thin membranous tissue between the creature’s second and third star-filament wings.

*SKRRREEEEEEE-OOMMM!*

The wyrm unleashed an ear-shattering acoustic shriek that vibrated the fillings in Cole's teeth.

Uncoiling its forty-foot serpentine mass from the dome, the apex beast launched itself into the screaming gale. Its remaining three functional wings beat with furious electromagnetic force, generating a halo of crackling static that vaporized the falling snowflakes into clouds of superheated steam.

Its four luminous violet eyes locked onto the observation catwalk. 

More specifically, onto Eleanor’s golden aura and the glowing star-iron induction rings on Arthur’s arm.

"It's diving!" Cross shouted. *"Cole, hold fire—it's too fast!"*

The wyrm plummeted out of the sky vortex like an obsidian spear. Between its four-foot mandibles, a ball of concentrated, ionized celestial plasma swelled to blinding brilliance—exceeding **one hundred thousand volts** of destructive atmospheric arc.

It opened its maw to incinerate the catwalk and everyone on it.

Arthur did not retreat. He stepped half a pace forward, planted his right boot against the heavy structural steel girder, and raised the **Sovereign Aegis**.

"Eleanor," Arthur said calmly. "Phase synchronization. Now."

Eleanor pressed her right palm firmly against Arthur’s shoulder plate. Her golden Starlight Sovereign resonance flooded through Arthur’s nervous system, interlocking seamlessly with the three concentric star-iron rings embedded in his buckler.

*VZZZZT-WHIRRRRRRR!*

The star-iron rings spun into magnetic resonance, creating a dense, toroidal field of negative electromagnetic flux.

The Sky-Rift Wyrm unleashed its plasma breath.

*BOOOOOOOOOOM!*

A blinding torrent of violet celestial lightning struck the Sovereign Aegis with the force of an artillery shell.

The wooden catwalk timbers beneath Arthur charred to ash in an instant. The temperature around them spiked by two hundred degrees. But the plasma did not vaporize Arthur.

Instead, the toroidal field of the three star-iron rings captured the high-voltage arc like an electromagnetic lens. Eighty percent of the immense electrical surge was dumped straight down the four-ought copper grounding cable, flashing harmlessly into the mountain’s subterranean bedrock grid in a brilliant fountain of blue sparks.

The remaining twenty percent—a staggering **Seventy-Five Thousand Volts**—surged into the Chimera Frame Mark III's internal storage cells, overloading the capacitor banks to maximum theoretical capacity.

The buckler hissed with white-hot induction heat, kept from melting Arthur’s arm only by the three-millimeter layer of silica aerogel cryo-insulation lining his gauntlet.

"Charge saturation achieved: one hundred and two percent," Arthur reported, his breathing steady, his eyes cold and analytical through his safety goggles. "Dielectric limits near breakdown."

The wyrm, astonished that its lethal breath had failed to incinerate the tiny mammal, closed the remaining twenty feet in a feral kinetic plunge, its diamond-chitin cranial crest cocked forward to crush Arthur’s ribcage like dry pine twigs.

Arthur saw the micro-fractures along the cranial crest—cracks formed by Cole’s .50-caliber incendiary impacts and brittle thermal contraction in the sub-zero wind.

"Vector intercept: zero point two seconds," Arthur muttered.

He didn't dodge. He ducked under the snapping mandibular scythes, pivoted on his left heel, and drove his right fist straight up into the creature's fractured cranial suture.

He tripped the dual pneumatic release.

*PSSSHT-KER-BLAM!*

The twin nitrogen cylinders dumped three thousand PSI of stored pneumatic energy in four milliseconds. The four-inch solid tungsten-carbide chisel shot forward with **two thousand four hundred pounds** of linear kinetic force, driving directly into the fracture like an industrial rock-drill.

*CRACK!*

The diamond-chitin crest split open to the bone.

Simultaneously, Arthur closed the circuit between his left-arm Sovereign Aegis and the pile-driver’s tungsten tip.

"Discharge," Arthur Vance whispered.

*BZZZZZZZZT-CRACKLE-BANG!*

Seventy-five thousand volts of starlight-amplified celestial lightning emptied from the Mark III’s overloaded capacitors directly into the wyrm’s primary cranial nerve cluster.

Basic neuro-electrophysiology was merciless.

The wyrm’s biological nervous system was calibrated for micro-volt bio-synapses. When seventy-five thousand volts of reverse-polarity current tore through its cranial ganglia, every motor nerve in its forty-foot body detonated in a violent cascade of dielectric breakdown.

The creature’s four violet eyes blew out in twin flashes of white light. Its four wings snapped rigid, seized by tetanic muscular spasm, and its serpentine body went instantly limp.

Carried by its forward momentum, the dead leviathan crashed through the catwalk railing, somersaulted fifty feet through the sub-zero air, and slammed into the jagged basalt floor of Crater Stellaris with a concussive, bone-splintering impact that shook the entire mountain peak.

Black blood and crystalline ice steamed against the frozen crater stones.

The catwalk fell dead quiet, save for the howling blizzard and the deep, electric hum of the sky rift.

Arthur stood at the edge of the sheared steel railing. A thin curl of ozone-scented vapor drifted from the tungsten chisel on his right gauntlet. 

He reached into his waistcoat, pulled out his brass pocket watch, clicked it open, and verified the time.

*1:31 PM.*

"Total engagement duration: four minutes, twelve seconds," Arthur said, his tone as matter-of-fact as if he had just turned a shaft on his South Bend lathe. 

He glanced back at Eleanor, who was breathing hard, her golden eyes wide with awe.

"The predator is neutralized," Arthur Vance said, adjusting his spectacles. "Now, let us descend into the crater and finish the circuit."
