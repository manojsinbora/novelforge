# Chapter 29: The Ascent into the Sky-Rift

The wind on the exterior catwalk of Sentinel Observatory roared like an uncaged jet engine.

*Day 4 // 1:18 PM.*

Sheet ice two inches thick coated the galvanized steel mesh of the high walkway. Sixty feet below lay the rim of Crater Stellaris, glowing with the ominous, boiling violet luminescence of ungrounded star-iron. Overhead, the storm vortex had tightened into a blinding spiral of ionizing gas, dropping ambient temperatures to a brutal minus twenty-two degrees Fahrenheit. With the fifty-knot gale whipping across the ridge, the wind chill was calculated at minus forty-five.

Arthur Vance knelt on the frozen steel grating, his rubber safety goggles shielding his eyes from flying ice pellets. In his gloved hands, he held the test leads of a vintage Simpson 260 analog multimeter.

He touched the black negative probe to the observatory’s three-inch solid copper lightning down-conductor, and the red positive probe to the bronze grounding lug clamped to his left-arm **Sovereign Aegis** buckler.

The black needle on the mirrored scale flicked cleanly to the left.

"Contact resistance: zero point zero one ohms," Arthur announced into his throat mic, his breath pluming like white smoke. "Grounding continuity to the subterranean basalt grid verified. The circuit will tolerate up to one hundred thousand amperes of transient surge without conductor vaporisation."

Beside Arthur, Eleanor stood braced against the howling gale, her sheepskin collar pulled tight around her chin. Her Starlight Sovereign resonance had reached a pitch so high and pure that faint golden auroral rings orbited her wrists, vibrating in direct counter-phase to the churning violet vortex above.

"It has sensed us, Arthur," Eleanor shouted over the screaming gale, her voice carrying an uncanny acoustic clarity that pierced through the wind. "It doesn't just eat lightning. It feels the pylon's hunger. It thinks we are trying to steal its food."

Arthur reached into his heavy wool coat, clicked open his pocket watch with his gloved thumb, and checked the second hand.

*1:24 PM.*

"A predatory response rooted in metabolic territoriality," Arthur said evenly, snapping the watch shut. "Its reliance on atmospheric plasma makes its behavior predictable. When it dives, it will follow the highest electrical potential gradient. We will make ourselves that gradient."

Eighty yards to their left, positioned on the reinforced concrete loading ramp of the observatory, Captain Rachel Cross stood in the commander's hatch of the lead Humvee. Beside her, Sergeant Marcus Cole was locked into the gun ring behind the M2 Browning .50-caliber machine gun.

Cole had spent the last twenty minutes carefully adjusting the weapon’s headspace and timing using steel feeler gauges, ensuring the heavy bolt would cycle cleanly despite the freezing chill. The weapon's moving parts had been stripped of military grease and treated with low-temperature silicone lubricant.

"Browning hot and calibrated, Vance!" Cole’s voice crackled through the bone-conduction headset. "Belt loaded: two hundred rounds of linked armor-piercing incendiary with red tracers! She’s moving on the dome!"

High above the catwalk, coiled around the observatory’s exterior telescope shutter, the **Sky-Rift Wyrm** shifted its colossal bulk.

It was nearly forty feet long, an aerodynamic marvel of celestial apex evolution. Its midnight-blue carapace absorbed the ambient light like powdered anthracite, glistening with diamond-hard rime frost. Four vast, membranous wings—each ribbed with flexible filaments of raw, conductive star-iron—stretched fifteen feet across, beating with a hypnotic, rhythmic hum that generated localized static discharges.

The beast raised its elongated head, sniffing the thin alpine air. Between its four-foot curved mandibles, arcs of violet lightning crackled and pooled, feeding on the surplus ionospheric voltage leaking from the crater below.

Its four luminous violet eyes locked onto the catwalk.

"It sees Eleanor's resonance," Cross radioed. "Target is uncoiling!"

"Sergeant Cole," Arthur ordered calmly. "Engage the wing roots. Do not aim for the dorsal armor; your fifty-caliber penetrators will deflect. Sever the membranous wing webbing to force a linear descent vector."

"Copy that! Firing!"

*THUD-THUD-THUD-THUD-THUD!*

The heavy Browning opened up with a deafening, rhythmic hammering that echoed off the frozen granite peaks like rolling artillery fire.

Red-hot streams of .50-caliber incendiary tracers carved through the blizzard, streaking across the eighty-yard gap and slamming into the wyrm’s left wing junctions.

*CRACK-FLASH! CRACK-FLASH!*

The half-inch tungsten-carbide penetrators struck with explosive kinetic fury. While the heavy scales on the beast's chest deflected three of the rounds in showers of white sparks, two incendiary rounds tore clean through the thin star-filament membrane between its primary and secondary wings. The magnesium incendiary filler detonated inside the wing root, scorching the bio-metallic tissue into black ash.

*SKRRREEEEEEE-OOMMM!*

The wyrm unleashed an ear-shattering acoustic shriek that vibrated the fillings in Cole's teeth and shattered two exterior windowpanes on the lower laboratory wall.

Enraged, the apex leviathan launched itself from the steel dome into the raging blizzard.

With one wing crippled, its flight was no longer an effortless glide—it was a furious, spiraling descent. Its remaining three wings beat with violent electromagnetic power, vaporizing the falling snow into clouds of hissing steam.

The wyrm banked sharply, banking directly toward the observation catwalk at ninety miles per hour.

As it closed the distance, its throat sac expanded like a bellows. A blinding sphere of concentrated, ionized celestial plasma swelled between its open jaws—building toward a catastrophic discharge of over **one hundred thousand volts**.

"Arthur! It's going to fire!" Eleanor cried, bracing her boots against the icy grating.

"Eleanor. Step behind my left shoulder," Arthur commanded, his voice cold, steady, and immovable as granite. "Place both palms against the Sovereign Aegis grounding ring. Harmonize your frequency with the River Pylon induction coil."

Eleanor pressed both bare palms against the rear induction plate of Arthur’s buckler. Her golden Starlight Sovereign resonance surged through the copper circuits, interlocking with the three concentric rings of star-iron inlaid on the shield's face.

*VZZZZZZT-WHIRRRRRRR!*

The three star-iron rings spun into magnetic resonance, casting a shimmering, toroidal field of negative electromagnetic flux two feet in front of Arthur's arm.

The Sky-Rift Wyrm unleashed its plasma breath.

*BOOOOOOOOOOM!*

A blinding torrent of violet celestial lightning erupted from the beast's maw, striking the Sovereign Aegis with the concussive fury of a heavy artillery shell.

The light was blinding—a searing magnesium-white flare that turned the surrounding blizzard into day. The heat was instantaneous; wooden handrails ten feet away charred to black charcoal, and the ice on the catwalk vaporized into a cloud of boiling fog.

Yet Arthur Vance did not yield an inch.

His boots, locked into the manganese-steel crampons, held firm against the grating. Beneath his heavy wool coat, the silica aerogel cryo-insulation absorbed the blistering heat, keeping his forearm skin at a safe sixty-five degrees.

More importantly, the laws of electromagnetism held true.

The negative flux generated by Eleanor’s resonance captured the one-hundred-thousand-volt arc like an electromagnetic funnel. Eighty thousand volts of the colossal surge were shunted directly into the four-ought copper welding cable, flashing down into the mountain's bedrock basalt in a blinding fountain of electric blue ground sparks.

The remaining twenty thousand volts surged directly into the Chimera Frame Mark III's internal storage cells.

On Arthur’s left wrist, the analog voltmeters swung past their limits. The four oil-filled storage capacitors hissed with inductive power, storing a staggering **Seventy-Five Thousand Volts** of starlight-amplified celestial lightning.

"Energy absorption complete," Arthur recorded aloud, blinking away the flash-afterimage. "Capacitors saturated at one hundred and four percent. Dielectric limits holding."

The wyrm, shocked that its lethal plasma breath had failed to incinerate the tiny mammal, closed the remaining fifteen feet in a furious physical lunge.

It snapped its four-foot mandibles, lowering its massive, diamond-chitin cranial crest to crush Arthur and Eleanor against the reinforced concrete wall of the observatory.

Arthur saw the micro-fractures along the creature's cranial suture—fine hairline cracks produced by Cole’s .50-caliber incendiary rounds and the rapid thermal contraction of the sub-zero wind.

"Vector intercept: zero point two seconds," Arthur muttered.

He didn't leap back. He dropped to his left knee, letting the snapping mandibles whistle an inch above his woolen cap, and drove his right fist upward into the center of the cranial fracture.

He tripped the twin pneumatic solenoid.

*PSSSHT-KER-BLAM!*

Three thousand PSI of compressed nitrogen dumped into the Mark III's twin cylinders in four milliseconds. The four-inch solid tungsten-carbide chisel shot forward with **two thousand four hundred pounds** of linear kinetic force, driving directly into the fracture like an industrial demolition drill.

*CRACK-SHATTER!*

The diamond-chitin crest split open to the bone, driving the tungsten chisel deep into the wyrm's primary cranial nerve cluster.

Simultaneously, Arthur closed the mechanical discharge switch between his left-arm Sovereign Aegis and the pile-driver's tungsten chisel.

"Discharge," Arthur Vance whispered.

*BZZZZZZZZT-CRACKLE-BANG!*

Seventy-five thousand volts of stored celestial lightning dumped from the Mark III’s overloaded capacitors directly into the exposed cranial nerve cluster.

Basic neuro-electrophysiology was merciless.

The wyrm’s nervous system, evolved for micro-volt bio-synapses, stood no chance against seventy-five thousand volts of reverse-polarity current. Every motor neuron in its forty-foot body detonated in a violent cascade of dielectric breakdown.

The creature’s four violet eyes blew out in twin flashes of white light. Its wings snapped rigid in a violent tetanic muscular spasm, and its serpentine body went instantly limp.

Carried by its forty-ton kinetic momentum, the dead leviathan crashed through the catwalk railing, sheared two structural iron stanchions, and tumbled fifty feet through the freezing air.

It slammed into the jagged basalt floor of Crater Stellaris with a concussive impact that shook the entire mountain peak.

Black blood and crystalline ice steamed against the frozen crater stones.

The catwalk fell dead silent.

The only sounds remaining were the howling blizzard, the steady idle of the Humvee's diesel engine eighty yards away, and the deep, electric hum of the sky rift.

Arthur stood slowly, rolling his right shoulder. A thin curl of ozone-scented smoke drifted from the tungsten chisel on his right gauntlet.

He reached into his waistcoat, pulled out his brass pocket watch, clicked it open, and verified the time.

*1:32 PM.*

"Total combat engagement: four minutes, fourteen seconds," Arthur said, his tone as matter-of-fact as if he had just turned a shaft on his South Bend lathe.

He glanced back at Eleanor, who was breathing hard, her hands trembling slightly as the golden glow around her fingers slowly subsided.

"Are you injured, Eleanor?" Arthur asked.

"No," Eleanor whispered, looking down over the sheared railing at the motionless leviathan in the crater. "Arthur... you just killed a god of the storm."

"It was not a god, Eleanor," Arthur Vance corrected gently, wiping a speck of soot from his spectacles. "It was an aerofauna with an unshielded neural cluster and an inadequate grounding strategy."

He adjusted his wool coat and looked toward the crater floor.

"Captain Cross," Arthur called into his mic. "The perimeter is clear. Bring down the Cadweld molds and the copper cable. It is time to finish the circuit."
