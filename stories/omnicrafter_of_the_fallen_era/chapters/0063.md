# Chapter 63: The Shoshone High-Pass Commune

The mountain air of Jackson Hole was crisp, sweet with the scent of cedar smoke, and charged with the faint, sulfurous tang of geothermal steam rising from hot springs in the snow.

*Day 16 // 8:30 PM.*

*The Iron Vanguard* rolled slowly into the fortified rail siding of the **Shoshone High-Pass Commune**, its massive biogenic plow clearing a two-foot snowbank from the ties with a soft, steady hiss.

Surrounding the siding was a remarkable mountain redoubt: three hundred and fifty residents inhabited thick-walled log-and-granite lodges nestled at the foot of the snow-mantled Grand Teton. 

Unlike the dying, frozen ruins found elsewhere in the high Rockies, this settlement was warmly illuminated by strings of yellow incandescent bulbs. Between the houses ran insulated overhead steel pipes wrapped in horsehair felt, carrying hot geothermal water from natural fumaroles to melt the snow from stone pathways and heat fifty acres of glass-topped subterranean vegetable greenhouses.

Standing on the timber unloading platform was the commune’s leader: **Master Engineer Naomi Tsosie**.

She was thirty-eight years old, tall, with keen dark eyes, high cheekbones, and thick black hair braided with copper wire. Over her heavy wool work trousers, she wore a thick sheepskin coat, a pair of brass flip-up gas welding goggles pushed onto her forehead, and a heavy leather tool belt bristling with brass pipe calipers, a geologist’s hammer, and a slide rule.

Beside her stood twenty armed mountain scouts on snowshoes, carrying bolt-action rifles and heavy ice axes.

When Arthur Vance stepped down from the locomotive cab with his leather ledger and drafting satchel, Naomi Tsosie stepped forward, looking him up and down with sharp, critical scrutiny.

"You're the one," Tsosie said, her voice clear and resonant against the cold night. "The telegraph wire from Promontory said the 'Ghost Artificer' was bringing seven hundred tons of steel up the Snake River gorge. I expected an eight-foot mutant in iron armor. Instead, you look like a county tax clerk who wandered into a blizzard."

"I am Arthur Vance, Municipal Archivist and Master Craftsman of the New Horizon Confederation," Arthur replied in his deadpan, matter-of-fact baritone, adjusting his spectacles. "A tax clerk lacks training in thermodynamics and machine tooling. However, an archivist understands both historical engineering records and applied mechanics."

Beside him, Eleanor stepped down, smiling warmly. "And I'm Eleanor. We brought sixty tons of Ceres wheat, fresh oranges, and winterized diesel."

Tsosie’s sharp gaze softened as she looked at Eleanor. Her eyes widened slightly as she sensed the gentle, harmonious golden starlight aura radiating from the girl.

"Fresh citrus," Tsosie murmured, a faint, appreciative smile touching her lips. "My scouts have been eating dried elk jerky and pine-needle tea since November. Come inside, Arthur Vance. We have matters of life and extinction to discuss."

***

*Day 16 // 9:45 PM.*

The council hall of the commune was a cavernous timber lodge built over an active geothermal steam vent. Beneath the heavy oak floorboards, steam radiators hissed with comforting warmth.

Spread across the central pine trestle table were geological cross-sections, core-sample drill logs, and seismic telemetry printouts spanning ten years of deep-well geothermal monitoring.

Naomi Tsosie unrolled a massive topographical map of the **Yellowstone Caldera Plateau**, weighing down the corners with chunks of black volcanic obsidian:

"Look at these reservoir pressure curves, Arthur," Tsosie said, tapping a brass stylus against the charts. "Our commune operates sixteen five-thousand-foot geothermal test boreholes around the southern rim of the caldera. Under normal equilibrium, hydrostatic pressure in the hydrothermal caprock stays steady at eight hundred pounds per square inch at four hundred degrees Fahrenheit."

She pointed to the red trend-line soaring exponentially off the chart:

"In the last fourteen days, deep reservoir pressure has spiked to **Two Thousand Four Hundred PSI**. The ground beneath the Norris Geyser Basin and Mallard Lake resurgent dome has uplifted eighteen centimeters. That isn't normal thermal fluctuation. The ancient magma chamber—ten thousand cubic kilometers of molten rhyolitic rock—is rising toward the surface."

"The seismic telemetry from Mount Sentinel confirms the diagnosis," Arthur said, consulting his notebook. "The First Dynasty Anchor Node Five, situated in the deep magma vault beneath the caldera, has suffered resonant drift. Its stabilizing frequency has slipped from five hundred and twenty-eight hertz down to four hundred and twelve hertz."

"You know about the Anchor?" Tsosie asked, staring at Arthur in profound shock.

"We synchronized Anchor Node Four beneath the Great Salt Lake four days ago," Arthur answered calmly. "The First Dynasty anchors form a planetary harmonic containment grid designed to suppress deep-mantle plumes and miasma fissures. When Anchor Five drifted out of harmonic lock, its zero-point field collapsed, converting vacuum potential directly into thermal overpressure within the magma chamber."

"And it gets worse," Tsosie added, her jaw tightening with bitter fury. "The caldera didn't drift on its own. It was sabotaged."

"The Ashen Covenant," Captain Rachel Cross noted, stepping to the table with her arms crossed.

"Yes," Tsosie spat. "Two hundred fire-mutated zealots and raiders, led by a madman who calls himself **Pyromancer Ignis**."

Tsosie pulled out a pre-Collapse personnel dossier stamped *DEPARTMENT OF ENERGY — PROJECT TARTARUS*:

"Before the Collapse, his name was Dr. Ronald Caldwell. He was the chief geophysicist on Project Tartarus—the federal deep-drilling installation built directly into the southern caldera rim at Obsidian Ridge. When the miasma hit fifteen years ago, Caldwell didn't seek shelter. He exposed himself to the geothermal vents, mutated his physiology with molten-silicate serums, and founded the Ashen Covenant. He believes humanity is a disease and that the Yellowstone supervolcano is the 'Cleansing Pyre' destined to wipe the continent clean in fire and ash."

"What is his tactical disposition at Project Tartarus?" Sergeant Major Bradley asked, leaning over the map.

"The Covenant has fortified the entire Tartarus complex," Tsosie explained. "They’ve built three heavy armored steam crawlers—fifty-ton tracked tractors sheathed in two-inch obsidian slag armor, armed with high-pressure geothermal steam cannons and heavy mortars. Worse, Ignis has rigged twenty thousand pounds of military-grade hexogen wellhead demolition charges directly to the Tartarus supercritical steam production wells."

Arthur Vance leaned in, studying the piping schematics of Project Tartarus with laser focus:

"Project Tartarus utilizes six supercritical geothermal production wells drilled twelve thousand feet into the caldera caprock," Arthur observed. "Each wellhead operates at thirty-eight hundred PSI. If Ignis detonates high-explosive charges at those wellheads, the sudden, violent depressurization of the hydrothermal caprock will trigger an instantaneous **Phreatomagmatic Flash-Boiling Cascade**:

$$\Delta P_{\text{sudden}} \rightarrow \text{Instantaneous superheated water flash to steam} \rightarrow \Delta V \approx 1,600\text{x}$$

"A volume expansion of sixteen hundred times across thirty cubic miles of superheated aquifer will shatter the remaining geological caprock. The underlying magma chamber will breach within ninety seconds. A Category Eight supervolcanic eruption will occur, ejecting two thousand cubic kilometers of ash into the stratosphere."

The council hall fell dead silent.

Eleanor stepped to the map, placing her palm gently over the red marker representing Obsidian Ridge.

Her green eyes shimmered with soft, golden starlight. 

"Artie," Eleanor said softly, her voice carrying an eerie, harmonic certainty. "Ignis has set a countdown. His men are chanting around the wellheads. He plans to detonate the charges at noon on Day 18—the winter solstice. We have thirty-six hours before he pulls the trigger."

Arthur Vance stood upright, clicking open his gold-plated Elgin pocket watch.

*10:14 PM.*

"Thirty-six hours," Arthur noted calmly. "Sufficient time to execute our industrial and tactical countermeasures."

He looked at Naomi Tsosie.

"Master Engineer Tsosie. The New Horizon Confederation offers the Shoshone Commune full accession as the **Tenth Sovereign Hub**. We guarantee sixty tons of grain and diesel monthly, immediate integration into our transcontinental rail and telegraph grid, and mutual defense. In return, we require your deep-well drilling crews, your knowledge of the Tartarus tunnel network, and two high-pressure steam crawlers."

Naomi Tsosie looked at Arthur Vance, then took him into the commune's heavy drilling workshop to inspect their assets:

Rows of massive, ten-inch polycrystalline diamond compact (PDC) drilling bits gleamed in wooden racks, beside high-pressure blowout preventer stacks rated to ten thousand PSI:

"We have two heavy steam crawler tractors," Tsosie explained, patting the thick steel tread of a sixty-ton machine. "They run on closed-loop geothermal steam boilers, capable of operating in heavy volcanic ash without choking air filters. And my crew has thirty thousand pounds of barium sulfate weighted drilling mud to kill high-pressure well blowouts."

"Weighted barite mud," Arthur observed with deep professional satisfaction. "Specific gravity: two point two. Exactly what we require to flood the Tartarus wellheads and prevent hydrothermal flash-boiling if the Covenant damages the master valves."

Naomi Tsosie unbuckled her geologist’s hammer, placed it firmly on the map, and extended her calloused hand to Arthur.

"You have a deal, Arthur Vance," Tsosie said firmly. "Let’s go extinguish this pyre."

Arthur took her hand with a crisp, professional grip.

Out on the unloading platform, Company B soldiers worked alongside the Shoshone commune workers, unloading thirty tons of Ceres Oasis winter wheat flour, four hundred pounds of dried citrus slices, and forty 55-gallon drums of winterized Wasatch diesel. 

Elderly commune drillers and young apprentices crowded around the boxcar doors, their eyes bright with wonder as they tasted fresh, sweet oranges and felt the warmth of full grain rations.

"We have two months of food in our silos tonight, Arthur," Naomi Tsosie murmured, watching her people. "You keep your promises. The Shoshone Commune will keep ours."

Beside the track, Corporal Davis ran a flexible star-iron drop cable from the train’s telegraph jumper into Tsosie’s radio room, connecting a brass polar duplex transceiver:

*CLICK-CLACK. CLACK-CLICK.*

The sounder tapped out the confirmation:

```
JACKSON HOLE COMMUNE CONNECTED. 
TENTH HUB ACCESSION RATIFIED. 
ALLIES SECURED. MOVING TO OBSIDIAN RIDGE.
```

Arthur walked with Eleanor down to the commune’s primary thermal outflow canal, where boiling water from the deep hot springs steamed against the snowbanks. 

Arthur drew a chromel-alumel thermocouple pyrometer from his vest, dipping the stainless-steel probe into the surging water:

"Water temperature: two hundred and twelve degrees Fahrenheit at atmospheric surface pressure," Arthur noted, checking the temperature gradient:

$$q = -k \cdot \nabla T \approx -0.6 \text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K} \times \left(\frac{100^\circ\text{C} - (-10^\circ\text{C})}{0.05 \text{ m}}\right) \approx 1,320 \text{ W/m}^2$$

"Thermal flux across the surface rock is rising by twelve percent per hour. Inside the Tartarus steam vaults, ambient air temperatures will exceed five hundred and fifty degrees Fahrenheit."

Eleanor knelt beside the steaming stream, dipping her bare fingers into the boiling mineral water. Around her hands, the golden Starlight Sovereign aura formed a tranquil thermal buffer—the boiling water curled around her skin without scalding, cooled into gentle warmth by her harmonic resonance.

"I can shield us from the direct steam, Artie," Eleanor said softly, looking up at him. "My starlight can bend the heat away. But your machines will need insulation."

"Precisely," Arthur agreed. "Which is why the Mark V requires aerospace silica aerogel."

Arthur turned to Master Kovacs and Sergeant Major Bradley, who were waiting at the machine shop door:

"Joseph, Bradley. Move the mobile machine shop into the commune roundhouse. I am commencing the immediate fabrication of the **Chimera Frame Mark V: Cryo-Thermal Expansion Chassis**."

Arthur clicked his gold-plated pocket watch shut and slipped his number-two pencil behind his ear.

*11:55 PM.*

"We have thirty-six hours before the winter solstice countdown expires. Let us begin."
