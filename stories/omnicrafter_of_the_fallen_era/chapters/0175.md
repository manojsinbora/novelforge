*Day 75 // 5:45:00 PM MST.*

Forged from pure, refined First Dynasty aurum-iridium alloy that did not tarnish or corrode, the circlet was formed of interlocking star-iron gears, wheat stalks, and lightning conduits. "Aurum-iridium alloy," Arthur Vance said, his voice flat, dry, and perfectly audible in the quiet room. "Specific gravity approximately twenty-one point four grams per cubic centimeter. "Arthur..." Silas began, bewildered. "It’s... it’s an imperial crown. It’s not head protection for a maintenance yard."

It is a decorative mass of non-ferrous metal that consumes sixteen hundred grams of high-purity aurum-iridium that could otherwise be used to manufacture forty-two precision galvanometers or twelve hundred high-temperature thermocouple junctions."

Arthur looked outward, scanning the faces of the twelve hundred delegates across the hall.

"Monarchy," Arthur Vance stated, his baritone voice calm and uncompromising, "is an obsolete, highly unstable administrative architecture. It concentrates systemic decision-making into a single biological organism subject to cognitive bias, emotional irrationality, arterial sclerosis, and hereditary incompetence. History proves that hereditary empires possess a mean structural time-to-failure of less than two hundred and forty years, usually collapsing into catastrophic civil partition due to succession friction."

He paused, resting his left hand flat upon the calfskin master ledger.

"I did not spend seventy-five days laying continuous welded rail and balancing telluric power grids in order to replace twenty fragmented scrap-warlords with a single, gilded continental warlord. An 'emperor' issues arbitrary edicts based upon personal whim, vanity, and political expedience. An engineer adheres strictly to observable physical law, material limits, and metric audits."

Arthur’s eyes met those of Silas, Calloway, Cross, and Morales.

"I decline the crown," Arthur Vance said, his voice carrying the finality of a hydraulic press seating a bearing. "Categorically and permanently. A collective intake of breath rippled through the hall.

"My office is defined by the Sovereign Charter and the ledger before me," Arthur concluded. "My title is not 'Emperor.' My title is not 'High Sovereign.' My title remains strictly, exactly, and exclusively: Chief Archivist & Master of Infrastructure. We do not need monarchs to govern us. For three long seconds, twelve hundred people sat frozen in stunned comprehension.

Then, Captain Gregory Cross snapped his boots together, brought his gauntleted hand to his brow in a crisp, sharp military salute, and spoke in a voice that rang like iron against granite:

"Hail the Chief Archivist & Master of Infrastructure!"

The delegates rose once more, but the nature of their ovation had transformed. It was no longer the fawning adulation of subjects bowing before a master; it was the ferocious, unshakeable pride of free men and women cheering for the truth.

"Chief Archivist Vance!" they roared. "Master of Infrastructure!"

Eleanor Vance stood beside the podium, a gentle, radiant smile gracing her lips. She looked at her brother, whose expression remained completely impassive as he opened the ledger and prepared his pencil for the next entry.

"You could have had an empire, Arthur," Eleanor murmured softly beneath the applause.
