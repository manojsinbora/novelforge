# Chapter 80: The Battle of the North Platte Trestle

The vast expanse of the central Nebraska sandhills was cleaved in two by the frozen, braided channels of the North Platte River.

*Day 25 // 2:15 PM.*

At Milepost Three Hundred and Sixty, near the ancient rail junction of North Platte, the transcontinental railroad crossed the river via a monumental structure of pre-Collapse civil engineering:

**The Great North Platte Steel Truss Trestle.**

The bridge was a six-hundred-foot, four-span Warren deck-truss structure elevated sixty feet above the frozen, snow-swept riverbed on colossal reinforced-concrete piers. Each steel span measured one hundred and fifty feet in length, built of heavy riveted silicon-steel box girders and diagonal tension eye-bars designed under the historic Cooper E-80 railroad live loading standards to carry two-hundred-ton freight trains across the river without exceeding the allowable deflection limit of span length divided by eight hundred ($L / 800 = 2.25\text{ inches}$).

Three miles west of the trestle, the armored train *Prometheus* slowed its speed from thirty miles per hour to a cautious crawl of five miles per hour.

In the forward pilot car, Arthur Vance sat on a folding stool beside the wheel axle, wearing a dual-channel acoustic stethoscope connected to an **Inductive Rail Geo-Phone Pickup Probe** clamped directly onto the star-iron railhead.

Beside him, Captain Rachel Cross adjusted the strap of her combat helmet. "What are the rails telling you, Arthur?"

Arthur closed his eyes, listening to the microscopic elastic acoustic waves transmitting through the steel lattice of the track:

"Continuous rail acoustic telemetry," Arthur dictated calmly, opening his leather ledger. "Sound velocity through one-hundred-and-thirty-six-pound steel rail is five thousand one hundred and twenty meters per second. At a distance of fifteen thousand eight hundred feet, I detect rhythmic, high-frequency mechanical impacts at approximately forty-two impacts per second."

Cross’s eyes narrowed. "Pneumatic rock drills?"

"Precisely," Arthur confirmed, clicking his mechanical pencil. "Pneumatic rotary hammer drills boring two-inch blast holes directly into the concrete load-bearing cap of Pier Three and into the rocker bearing assemblies supporting Span Three. In addition, I detect harmonic resonances consistent with six individual combustion generators idling on the bridge deck. Baron Malakor’s combat engineers are preparing a structural demolition."

Eleanor stepped into the compartment, her violet eyes clouded with grave concern. "They intend to drop the bridge into the ice as the train approaches?"

"A standard asymmetric denial tactic," Arthur replied. "If Span Three is dropped into the river, clearing the twisted six-hundred-ton steel wreckage, driving new timber falsework, and fabricating replacement star-iron girders would require seventy days of heavy civil engineering labor, completely halting our eastern advance. We will not allow the bridge to be severed."

Arthur stood upright, checking his gold pocket watch.

*2:22 PM.*

"Captain Cross, deploy the rail-speeder one mile behind the ridge. You and your six best marksmen will advance along the frozen river bottom beneath the southern bluffs to establish sniper overwatch on Pier Three. Eleanor and I will infiltrate the maintenance catwalk beneath the bridge deck to disarm the demolition charges."

"Understood," Cross replied with crisp military focus. "Rules of engagement?"

"Neutralize hostiles attempting to ignite primary fuses," Arthur directed. "Under no circumstances are high-explosive munitions to be discharged against the structural truss diagonals. A stray fifty-caliber projectile striking a tension eye-bar under load could initiate catastrophic brittle fatigue fracture."

At 2:40 PM, a dense snow squall swept down the river valley, masking the frozen riverbed in a blinding whiteout.

Moving through the reeds and frozen sandbars sixty feet beneath the colossal steel trestle, Arthur and Eleanor advanced in absolute silence. 

The wind howled through the open steel trusses fifty feet above, screaming like a tortured banshee through the lacework of cross-braces and diagonal struts. Above them, perched on the catwalk of Span Three, thirty elite sappers of the Iron Barony—known as the **Torch-Beard Sappers**—were frantically working beneath the roar of two portable gasoline air compressors.

Arthur looked up through the snow, using his brass pocket telescope to assess the demolition layout:

"Demolition configuration: sixty twenty-five-pound kegs of military ammonal and nitro-starch," Arthur whispered through his throat microphone. "Thirty kegs strapped to the load-bearing concrete pedestal of Pier Three; thirty kegs lashed in linear cutting charges along the lower tension chords of the truss."

"How are they wired?" Eleanor asked, keeping her starlight tightly suppressed to avoid illuminating their position against the dark river ice.

"Dual-path initiation," Arthur analyzed. "An electric capacitive discharge blasting machine located on the upper rail deck, supplemented by redundant slow-burning safety fuses filled with black powder and asphaltum. If the electrical circuit fails, the thermal fuses burn at forty seconds per foot."

Arthur unslung a pair of star-iron ascending clamps from his tactical harness. 

He gripped the vertical steel lattice of Pier Three and began scaling the frozen concrete pier with swift, rhythmic, silent ascents. Eleanor climbed beside him, her boots gripping the icy steel rivets with effortless agility.

At sixty feet above the frozen river, Arthur swung himself onto the narrow, two-foot-wide steel maintenance catwalk running directly beneath the railroad ties of Span Three.

Directly ahead, kneeling over the master detonation manifold, were six sappers wearing sheepskin vests and grease-blackened welding goggles. Their leader, a scarred veteran with a braided copper beard, was holding an electrical blasting machine in one hand and an ignited sulfur match in the other:

"Timer is set for two minutes!" the sapper chief shouted over the wind. "The train’s headlamp is clearing the western ridge! When she touches the approach spans, we blow the whole river out from under them!"

Arthur Vance stepped out of the swirling snow onto the catwalk, ten feet behind the sappers.

"Sapper Chief," Arthur’s calm, dry voice sliced through the blizzard like a razor. "Your demolition calculation is structurally defective."

The sappers whipped around in shock, fumbling for their sawed-off shotguns:

"Who the hell are you?!" the chief roared, lunging for the blasting machine plunger.

Before the chief’s thumb could depress the brass plunger, a suppressed rifle shot cracked from the riverbed sixty feet below:

*PHUT!*

Captain Cross’s precision armor-piercing round tore through the electrical blasting machine’s capacitor casing, shattering the bakelite box into fragments and severing the internal battery leads.

"Kill him! Light the fuses!" the chief screamed, hurling his burning sulfur match onto the black powder quick-match fuse leading to the sixty ammonal kegs.

*SSSSSSSSSSSSSSS!*

The spark caught. A bright, hissing train of white-hot fire raced along the lower truss chord at fifteen feet per second, heading straight toward seven hundred and fifty pounds of high explosives lashed to the central tension joint.

If those kegs detonated, the lower chord would be severed instantly:

$$\text{Bending Moment: } M = \frac{w \cdot L^2}{8}$$

$$\text{Tension Force: } T = \frac{M}{d} = \frac{w \cdot L^2}{8 d}$$

Without the lower chord to resist the immense tensile force of four hundred and eighty thousand foot-pounds, the entire one-hundred-and-fifty-foot steel span would fold like an accordion, plunging into the abyss.

Arthur did not hesitate for a microsecond.

"Eleanor! Suppress thermal propagation!" Arthur shouted.

Eleanor sprang forward along the narrow catwalk. 

She extended her right hand, unleashing a focused beam of 528 Hz starlight directly along the burning fuse trail. The coherent harmonic field instantly robbed the black powder of its chemical oxidation energy, extinguishing the racing spark four inches from the primary blasting cap.

Simultaneously, the sapper chief lunged at Arthur with a heavy thirty-inch structural steel pry bar, swinging the iron bar with all his strength in a vicious horizontal swipe aimed at Arthur's neck.

Arthur did not step back on the icy catwalk.

He brought up the **Chimera Frame Mark V** on his right arm, locking the hexagonal biogenic tungsten chisel into forward parry position.

*CLANG-SHATTER!*

The thirty-inch steel pry bar struck the diamond-tipped chisel at full speed. 

The vanadium-alloyed diamond chisel, hardened to twelve hundred megapascals, did not even scratch. Instead, the extreme localized kinetic shock shattered the sapper’s brittle carbon-steel pry bar in half, sending the broken end flying over the railing into the frozen river far below.

Arthur stepped into the chief’s guard, his boots planted firmly on the steel grating.

The Mark V’s pneumatic accumulator engaged with a soft, controlled hiss. Arthur drove his open left mechanical palm against the chief’s chest harness, exerting exactly two hundred and forty pounds of pneumatic clamping force, pinning the sapper chief flat against the vertical steel truss upright.

*THUD!*

The remaining five sappers, staring at Eleanor’s glowing golden hands and Arthur’s unstoppable mechanical frame, dropped their tools and raised their trembling hands into the air.

"Do not move," Arthur said calmly, holding the chief pinned with zero effort. "A fall from this catwalk onto river ice results in an impact velocity of sixty-two feet per second, yielding a ninety-eight percent fatality rate. I suggest you hold onto the safety railing."

Arthur took a pair of heavy diagonal wire cutters from his vest and clipped the explosive detonator harnesses from all sixty ammonal kegs, letting the inert wire coils drop harmlessly into his pocket.

He reached into his waistcoat, pulled out his gold pocket watch, clicked the hunter case open, and checked the second hand:

*2:48:15 PM.*

"Catwalk secure. Demolition array neutralized in six minutes and fifteen seconds," Arthur reported into his throat microphone. "Captain Cross, bridge is clear. Bring up Master Kovacs and the repair team."

Within twenty minutes, Master Joseph Kovacs and ten Confederation bridge mechanics arrived on the deck above. 

Lowering heavy pneumatic rivet squeezers and portable electric arc-welding leads connected to the *Prometheus*’s auxiliary generator, Kovacs’ crew went to work. They reinforced the drilled blast holes on Pier Three with high-early-strength hydraulic cement grout, welded two five-eighths-inch star-iron gusset plates across a scarred eye-bar on the lower chord, and torqued sixty high-tensile star-iron clamping bolts across the rocker bearings to ensure zero lateral sway under locomotive loading.

Eleanor Vance knelt beside the captured sappers on the catwalk, offering them steaming mugs of hot sweetened tea from an insulated canteen.

"You don't have to die for Baron Malakor," Eleanor said gently, her starlight warming their frozen fingers. "The Western Confederation builds bridges. We do not destroy them. There is honorable work for all of you in the rail shops."

The sapper chief, his rough face streaked with tears and charcoal, looked down at his calloused hands. "The Baron told us you were demons who came to enslave the plains. He told us if we didn't blow the bridge, he'd throw our wives into the blast furnaces at Gary."

"Baron Malakor will not touch your families," Arthur stated firmly, leaning over the railing. "His reign of scrap extortion ends at the Missouri River."

At 3:35 PM, Captain Cross fired a green illumination flare from the bridge deck.

Two miles to the west, the deep, magnificent three-chime steam whistle of the *Prometheus* sounded across the plains:

*WHOOOOOOOOO-TOOOOOOOOOOO!*

With slow, majestic power, the two-hundred-ton armored train *Prometheus* rolled onto the approach spans of the Great North Platte Trestle. 

The heavy star-iron rails hummed under the weight of the massive locomotive. The sixty-foot concrete piers absorbed the dynamic load with rock-solid stability, the reinforced steel trusses deflecting by less than three millimeters as the pride of the Western Confederation crossed the North Platte River.

Arthur Vance closed his pocket watch with a sharp, metallic click.

He opened his ledger and recorded the entry on page seventy-four:

*Bridge Assessment: North Platte Four-Span Warren Truss.*  
*Status: Demolition thwarted. Structural repairs verified under Cooper E-80 static live load.*  
*Casualties: 0.*  
*Mainline to Omaha: 100% OPEN.*

"The North Platte is crossed," Arthur announced, looking eastward toward the Missouri River. "Next stop: The river city of Omaha."
