# Chapter 17: The Chimera Frame Mark II

The Bridgeport vertical milling machine hummed with high-frequency precision as the four-flute carbide end mill carved a three-inch dovetail slot into a solid billet of 4140 chromoly steel.

Arthur Vance leaned over the machine table, his clear rubber safety goggles reflecting the green-tinted floodlights. A fine stream of water-soluble cutting coolant hissed against the spinning cutter, washing bright silver chips into the drainage trough.

*1:42 PM.*

Outside the granite walls of Workshop Section 3, Bunker Zero-Seven was humming with purposeful, orderly industry.

The thirty scholars rescued from the rail yard had integrated seamlessly into the mountain facility. Under Professor Finch and Old Man Henderson, Sub-Level One’s vacant administrative wing had been converted into the **New Municipal Archives**: forty-two crates of rescued First Dynasty codices and medical folios were being systematically indexed, dried with silica desiccant, and cataloged into steel map drawers. 

In the medical bay, Dr. Ward and Vera Bennett were utilizing the laboratory's precision centrifuge to synthesize twenty liters of Arthur’s zinc-silver stabilizing elixir, establishing an emergency stockpile capable of treating two hundred acute miasma infections.

Arthur shut off the milling machine spindle, wiped a smudge of cutting fluid from the dial indicator, and examined the finished workpiece.

On the heavy maple assembly bench lay the components of his newest undertaking: **Project: The Chimera Frame Mark II**.

The Mark I prototype had performed admirably in the field: it had shattered Vance Rogan’s hydraulic jaws, withstood Marcus Cole’s fifty-caliber rifle ricochet, and survived two explosive thermite engagements. 

However, as Arthur had noted in his pocket leather journal over his noon lunch of salted beef and dried apricots, empirical field testing had revealed three critical engineering limitations:

1. *Asymmetry:* Single-arm operation created a fourteen percent torsional imbalance along the lumbar vertebrae during maximum kinetic discharge.
2. *Pneumatic Capacity:* The eighteen-hundred-PSI single accumulator exhausted its pressure after four continuous full-thrust cycles, requiring manual recharge from an external compressor.
3. *Material Fatigue:* Standard carbon steel mounting pins were exhibiting micro-fissures along their shear planes from exposure to fifty-thousand-volt starlight arc discharges.

Arthur had addressed every single deficiency.

He walked over to the shop's electric heat-treating furnace, pulled on his heavy leather gauntlets, and used tongs to withdraw a glowing, cherry-red titanium-chitin composite bracer.

He quenched the piece in a tank of mineral oil with a violent, roaring hiss:

*FSSSSSSSSSSSH!*

Thick white vapor rolled toward the ceiling ventilation hood. When Arthur lifted the piece from the bath, the metal had taken on an iridescent, oil-slick sheen of dark slate-gray and deep sapphire.

This was no ordinary scrap armor.

For the Mark II, Arthur had incorporated two extraordinary materials:
First: Twelve pounds of pulverized **Star-Iron Ore** collected from the basalt tailings beneath the Section 14 pylon.
Second: Six ultra-hard **Tungsten-Carbide Drill Teeth** salvaged from the shattered drill auger of Marcus Cole’s fallen Caterpillar bulldozer.

By forge-brazing the tungsten teeth into the articulated knuckle joints and infusing the chromoly spine with meteoric star-iron, Arthur had created an alloy with thirty percent greater tensile strength than armor-grade titanium, while maintaining near-zero electrical resistance.

Furthermore, the Mark II was a **Dual-Chassis System**:

**The Right Arm (The Kinetic Striker):**
Upgraded with twin parallel pneumatic cylinders operating at a blistering twenty-four hundred PSI. The strike terminal was no longer a simple wrench jaw; it was an articulated **Kinetic Pile-Driver**. At the touch of a palm toggle, a hardened two-inch tungsten-carbide chisel could cycle three times per second with twelve hundred pounds of linear impact force—capable of punching clean through thirty millimeters of solid vehicular steel or shattering a subterranean titan's chitin carapace like porcelain.

**The Left Arm (The Sovereign Aegis):**
A wide, curved forearm buckler forged from overlapping Vanguard Strider chitin plates and backed by a dense grid of woven silver-copper mesh. It functioned as an active kinetic-energy dampener and high-voltage directional conduit, allowing Arthur to catch brute kinetic blows and redirect the shockwave safely into the ground.

Arthur slid both forearms into the fitted leather sleeves.

*SNICK-CLACK. SNICK-CLACK.*

Both bracers locked around his arms with airtight, pressurized precision. 

The weight was extraordinary: six point eight pounds total, yet so exquisitely balanced across the forearm flexor groups that it felt as light as a pair of winter gloves. 

Arthur closed his right fist.

*PSSSHT-PSSSHT-PSSSHT!*

The pneumatic pile-driver cycled three times in a tenth of a second, the tungsten chisel blurring with concussive, machine-gun rapidity. A sharp gust of compressed nitrogen blew across the bench, scattering metal shavings.

Arthur opened his left hand, and the curved chitin buckler expanded outward by four inches on spring-loaded pantograph hinges, forming a solid, interlocking shield of slate-gray armor.

"Tolerances: within zero point zero zero zero two inches," Arthur observed, checking his brass pocket watch sitting on the milling table. 

*2:48 PM.*

"Total assembly duration: four hours, twelve minutes. Mechanical efficiency: optimal."

The workshop door clattered open, and Eleanor stepped inside, accompanied by Professor Finch.

Eleanor was wearing her dark canvas field coat, carrying two freshly brewed mugs of chicory tea. When she saw Arthur standing in the center of the shop, clad in dual gauntlets of dark, iridescent star-iron and monster chitin, her green eyes lit up with genuine delight.

"Well, look at you," Eleanor said, setting the mugs on the corner of the workbench. "You went from an archivist with a wrench to an ancient siege engine with spectacles."

"The Mark II is an ergonomically optimized material handling apparatus," Arthur replied smoothly, though the faint trace of a grin tugged at the corner of his mouth. "It reduces musculoskeletal strain by sixty-two percent during heavy structural clearance."

Professor Finch walked around Arthur, inspecting the star-iron inlays on the left bracer with scholarly awe. 

"The glyph channels along the forearm, Arthur... they mirror the eighth-century conduits of the First Dynasty sentinel armor. Where did you calculate the coil spacing?"

"Volume four of the *Codex Astronomicus*, page ninety-six," Arthur said. "The harmonic inductance of star-iron follows standard Maxwellian electromagnetic equations if you factor in the tertiary crystalline permittivity."

Finch chuckled softly, shaking his head. "Only you, Arthur Vance, would reduce ancient celestial relics to nineteenth-century physics."

Eleanor stepped closer, reaching out with her bare right hand.

"Try it, Artie," she whispered.

She laid her palm against the central star-iron induction coil on Arthur’s right bracer.

*HUMMMMMMM.*

A gentle, sapphire current surged from Eleanor’s fingers into the metal.

The intricate glyphs along both bracers flared with soft, tranquil blue light. A deep, harmonious warmth flooded through the frame, settling into the internal nickel-iron storage cells. The needle on Arthur’s forearm voltage meter swept smoothly from zero all the way to **Thirty-Five Thousand Volts**, humming with pure, silent starlight potential.

There was no wild arcing. No burning ozone. 

The medicine had stabilized Eleanor’s frequency, and Arthur’s metallurgical crafting had provided the perfect mechanical vessel. Together, they were a complete, closed circuit.

Before Arthur could log the electrical parameters in his journal, the workshop intercom horn on the wall squawked with sharp, urgent static:

*SKRRRRRRRRT.*

Davis’s voice boomed through the speaker, breathless and high-pitched:

"Arthur! Professor Finch! Get to the Command Center immediately! The shortwave antenna just pulled a Priority One distress call on the northern emergency band!"

***

Two minutes later, Arthur and Eleanor hurried into the Command & Operations Center.

Dr. Ward, Miller, and Davis were crowded around the large military vacuum-tube receiver console. The green tuning eye tube was pulsing violently, locked onto the fifty-megahertz emergency civil defense frequency.

Davis slapped the speaker toggle, and a static-filled, desperate voice echoed through the vaulted room:

"—MAYDAY! MAYDAY! THIS IS EMERGENCY LOGISTICS COMMUNE HIGH ROCK! ALL STATIONS ON FREQUENCY, WE ARE UNDER COORDINATED SIEGE—"

The signal popped with concussive interference. In the background, the unmistakable sound of automatic rifle fire and deafening, high-pitched screeching echoed through the microphone:

"—THE RIVER CANYON BROKE OPEN! HUNDREDS OF GRAVE-CHITIN BURROWERS HAVE UNDERMINED OUR SOUTHERN GRAIN SILOS! AND ACID-SPITTER DRAKES ARE TEARING THROUGH THE GREENHOUSE GLASS! OUR FORTY-MAN MILITIA CANNOT HOLD THE PERIMETER! WE HAVE TWO HUNDRED CIVILIANS TRAPPED IN THE CENTRAL BARN! WE HAVE LESS THAN FOUR HOURS BEFORE THE ROOF COLLAPSES! IF ANYONE CAN HEAR THIS... PLEASE... GOD HELP US—"

A violent metallic screech ripped through the speaker, followed by the sound of shattering glass, a man’s strangled scream, and the sudden hiss of empty carrier static.

*Kzzzzzzzzzt.*

The Command Center fell into stunned, heavy silence.

Davis looked up at Arthur, his face pale. "High Rock Commune. Fifteen miles north along the Old Timber Line. Arthur... they’re the agricultural heart of the entire county. If those silos fall, there won't be a grain of wheat or seed potato left in the northern territory."

Arthur stood perfectly still, his eyes fixed on the illuminated relief map table.

His encyclopedic mind instantly assembled the operational variables:
*Target distance:* fifteen point four miles.  
*Transit corridor:* The Old Northern Timber Line. Standard gauge rail, thirty-foot elevated river ballast.  
*Motive power:* Depot Shunter Number 7 (GE 44-Ton Switcher Locomotive).  
*Adversary:* Tier 2 Intermediate subterranean burrowers and aerial acid-spitters.  
*Civilian lives:* two hundred men, women, and children.  
*Strategic prize:* sustainable agricultural food independence for the entire Blackwood Ridge sanctuary.

Arthur Vance reached into his waistcoat pocket, pulled out his brass pocket watch, and clicked the stem with a crisp, sharp snap.

*3:04 PM.*

"Davis. Miller," Arthur’s voice rang across the concrete room with absolute, unyielding authority.

"Fire up Switcher Number Seven. Couple the armored boxcar and the flatbed technical. Pack thirty gallons of concentrated calcium hydroxide neutralizer and all remaining thermite canisters."

Arthur turned to Eleanor, whose green eyes were blazing with determined sapphire light.

"Eleanor," Arthur said softly. "Pack your field kit. We are taking the iron train north."
