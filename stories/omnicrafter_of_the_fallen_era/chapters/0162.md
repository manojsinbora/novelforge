This was the Great Plains—the vast, temperate grassland biome that had once sustained the caloric foundation of an entire continent. For centuries, its deep glacial loess and prairie mollisols had produced more wheat, corn, and grain sorghum than any other geographical basin on the planet.

Yet during the twenty-five years following the Calamity, this continental breadbasket had become a desolate wasteland. When the Subterranean Primordial Rift unsealed along the lithospheric fault lines of the interior, mutagenic void miasma had vented upwards through deep crustal fractures, infiltrating the Ogallala Aquifer—the mammoth high-plains groundwater reservoir underlying eight states. Desiccated by relentless dry westerlies, millions of square miles of topsoil had blown away in dark, suffocating dust blizzards, leaving behind cracked calcic hardpan and abandoned, rusted irrigation pivots.

Now, that ecological nightmare was dissolving.

With the First Dynasty Anchor Node Eleven successfully re-synchronized and the abyssal litho-core rift permanently sealed beneath Mammoth Cave, the subterranean pressure gradient had reversed. The telluric standing waves propagating through the deep continental crust had purged the aquifer conduits. Mutagenic ionization had collapsed to absolute zero, unleashing an explosive, spontaneous biological renaissance across the heartland.
