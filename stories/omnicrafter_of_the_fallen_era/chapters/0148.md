# Chapter 148: The Savannah River Silos

The heavy star-iron flanged wheels of the armored command locomotive *Prometheus* rumbled with low, harmonic resonance across the reinforced steel-truss span of the Savannah River rail bridge. Below the roadbed, the amber, silt-laden waters of the Savannah River swept southward toward the Atlantic shelf at three point eight knots, swollen with spring meltwater from the Blue Ridge watershed. Along the eastern and western riverbanks, dense stands of loblolly pine, bald cypress draped with pale gray resurrection moss, and water tupelo formed a brooding, primeval canopy. The perpetual frost that had locked the Carolina and Georgia Piedmont in brittle stasis for decades had receded; the morning air registered fifteen point two degrees Celsius under an overcast sky of stratocumulus clouds drifting east at twelve kilometers per hour.

*Day 61 // 7:00:00 AM.*

Arthur Vance stood upon the reinforced steel observation platform situated immediately aft of the *Prometheus*'s main drive-cab. His charcoal wool archivist's overcoat was buttoned to the high throat collar, its cuffs turned back by exactly two centimeters to reveal the starched linen of his shirt and the matte-black mechanical pencil clipped into his breast pocket. In his left palm rested his gold hunter-case pocket watch. 

The blued-steel sweep-second hand completed its vertical traverse across the enamel dial, aligning precisely with the twelve.

*Click.*

Arthur depressed the crown catch with a controlled thumb stroke. The gold lid snapped shut with an audible metallic register. He returned the watch to his waistcoat pocket, extracted the mechanical pencil, and clicked the knurled end-cap twice. Exactly zero point five millimeters of high-density graphite protruded from the hardened steel sleeve.

Opening the heavy, brass-cornered calfskin master ledger balanced across his forearm, Arthur set down the opening audit log:

Behind him, Master Silas Thorne stepped out onto the open platform grating. The veteran forgemaster carried a polished brass vernier caliper in his chest pocket and a two-meter folding surveyor’s rule slotted into his oiled leather tool harness. He rubbed his stubbled chin, squinting through heavy bifocal lenses at the concrete security pylons rising out of the pine forest three miles ahead.

"Three hundred square miles of federal reservation, Arthur," Silas said, leaning against the cold star-iron railing. "Back before the Collapse, this was where the Old Federation made every gram of tritium and plutonium for their strategic arsenal. My grandfather's old machining logs had three pages redacted in heavy black ink just for mentioning the heavy water separation towers. If the primary containment structures held through the tectonic ruptures, there is enough exotic metal down in those silos to reinforce fifty capital ships."

"Sentimental speculation is unquantifiable, Master Thorne," Arthur replied without raising his head from his ledger. "The Savannah River Site was constructed between 1950 and 1955, utilizing reinforced monolithic cast-in-place Portland concrete with compressive strengths exceeding forty-five megapascals. The subterranean vaults were engineered to survive direct seismic ground accelerations of zero point three five gravity and hydrodynamic pressures from localized dam failures. Our concern is not historical lore. Our concern is material yield and isotopic decay."

Arthur turned to the starboard catwalk where Captain Gregory Cross stood with four technical operators of the Sovereign Pioneer Battalions. Cross’s Mark VI exoskeleton hummed softly on idle pneumatic cycle, the star-iron joints self-lubricating with a faint hiss of pressurized synthetic oil.

"Captain Cross," Arthur instructed. "Engage the slow-speed shunt drive. Route the *Prometheus* along the spur leading to the C-Reactor and Defense Waste Processing Facility nexus. Deploy forward radiological scout arrays on fifty-meter umbilical cables. I will not tolerate undetected contamination on our rolling stock."

"Understood, Chief Archivist," Cross replied, snapping a sharp hand salute before barking orders into his field intercom. "Switching track levers to the Savannah Industrial Loop. Scout array deployed."

The heavy train slowed from thirty-five miles per hour to a measured crawl of eight miles per hour, its star-iron wheels screeching lightly as the flanges engaged the rusted, heavily ballasted siding that cut deep into the restricted reserve.

*Day 61 // 8:45:30 AM.*

The industrial complex loomed out of the pine barrens like a civilization carved from petrified gray stone. Massive monolithic structures—windowless, brutalist, and vast—stretched across hundreds of cleared acres. Giant parabolic cooling towers stood silent against the low clouds, their concrete shells streaked with centuries of dry lichen and iron oxide wash. Abandoned switchyards, elevated steam pipe gantries insulated with weathered aluminum cladding, and rusted rail spurs crisscrossed the facility floor.

The *Prometheus* came to a smooth halt along the dedicated heavy-materials transfer dock fronting Building 400-D—the historic heavy water production and isotopic separation facility.

Arthur Vance descended the steel carriage stairs, his polished calfskin boots stepping firmly onto the cracked asphalt of the apron. Eleanor Vance stepped down behind him. Her violet eyes scanned the massive steel distillation columns that towered sixty meters into the damp sky, linked by complex webs of stainless steel piping and massive cryogenic heat exchanger manifolds.

"The air here is still, Arthur," Eleanor murmured, her fingers brushing the cold star-iron guardrail of the train's boarding gate. "The earth around this place is not angry. It feels asleep—heavy, dense, and encased in lead."

"That is because the civil engineers of the mid-twentieth century understood mass shielding," Arthur said, reaching into his instrument satchel. He withdrew a calibrated Ludlum Model 3 survey meter paired with an alpha-beta-gamma pancake Geiger-Mueller probe, along with an array of pocket micro-dosimeters. 

He flipped the toggle switch. The analog needle ticked upward, resting steadily at zero point zero eight micro-Sieverts per hour. 

"Normal background," Arthur recorded, clicking his pencil. "Gamma flux is eighty nano-Sieverts per hour. No radon concentration anomalies. The external shell of Facility 400-D is completely uncompromised."

Master Silas Thorne walked toward the main distribution manifold of the cryogenic distillation yard, running a gloved hand along a series of two-foot-diameter stainless steel transfer conduits. He pointed his brass-tipped flashlight into an open inspection port.

"Look at these pipe runs, Arthur," Silas exclaimed, his eyes widening behind his spectacles. "AISI 316L low-carbon austenitic stainless steel, tungsten inert gas welded with zero porosity. Not a spot of pitting corrosion. The seals on these isolation valves are double-seated fluorocarbon elastomer with pure silver back-up rings."

"This was the heavy water extraction facility utilizing the Girdler sulfide catalytic exchange process, followed by fractional water distillation," Arthur explained in his flat, pedagogical cadence. "To moderate heavy-water nuclear reactors, isotopic deuterium purity must exceed ninety-nine point seven five percent by weight. Light water absorbs thermal neutrons at a cross-section of zero point six six barns, whereas deuterium possesses a neutron capture cross-section of only zero point zero zero zero five two barns—a three-order-of-magnitude reduction in neutron parasitic loss."

Arthur walked to the facility's master control bulkhead, inserting a heavy star-iron bypass key into the pre-Collapse pneumatic latch mechanism. With a sharp twist and a hiss of vented inert nitrogen, the vault seal gave way.

Inside the subterranean climate-controlled basement vault, illuminated by the high-output LED searchlights mounted to Captain Cross’s exoskeleton, stood forty-eight cylindrical storage vessels fabricated from five-centimeter-thick 304L stainless steel. Each cylinder was stamped with official federal metallurgical stamps and archival barcodes.

Arthur approached the nearest vessel, checking the pressure dial and liquid-level sight glass.

"Vessel D2O-Alpha-01 through 48," Arthur read aloud. "Operating pressure: one hundred and four kilopascals under positive dry nitrogen head. Temperature: twelve degrees Celsius. Mass manifest per vessel: four thousand five hundred kilograms. Total inventory across forty-eight vessels: two hundred and sixteen metric tons of reactor-grade heavy water ($D_2O$), certified purity ninety-nine point eight five atomic percent deuterium."

Silas Thorne let out a low breath. "Two hundred and sixteen metric tons. Arthur, do you realize what this means for the zero-point induction steam cycles we designed back in Richmond? If we use deuterium oxide as the primary moderator-coolant in the auxiliary closed-loop thermal exchangers, the thermal heat capacity jumps, and we eliminate radiolytic hydrogen formation entirely."

"Precisely," Arthur said, making three concise ledger notations. "The specific heat capacity of heavy water is four thousand two hundred and twenty-eight Joules per kilogram-Kelvin at room temperature. More importantly, deuterium atoms provide the ideal non-absorbing kinetic transfer medium for our zero-point flux converters. Transfer crews will begin suctioning forty metric tons into the shielded tank carriages SC-01 and SC-02 immediately."

*Day 61 // 10:15:15 AM.*

Leaving the technical salvage crew to oversee the automated pneumatic transfer of the heavy water reserves, Arthur, Eleanor, Silas, and Captain Cross advanced to the Defense Waste Processing Facility (DWPF) complex and the adjacent Subterranean Heavy Metallurgy Vaults.

Here, the architecture descended deep into the subterranean bedrock beneath the Savannah River terrace gravels. A massive star-iron freight elevator, counterweighted with twenty metric tons of lead pigs, carried the party down sixty-five meters into Vault Level Three.

The elevator gates opened with a heavy clang onto an immaculate, dry subterranean gallery constructed from high-density basalt aggregate concrete. Overhead, banks of sealed sodium vapor lamps flickered to life as the *Prometheus*’s umbilical power line fed forty-eight hundred volts of three-phase alternating current into the substation busbars.

Before them lay the entrance to the Vitrified Borosilicate Glass Storage Vaults.

"Take micro-dosimeter readings at five-meter intervals," Arthur commanded. He held his own Ludlum survey meter forward, sweeping the pancake probe across the seams of the massive twelve-ton star-iron blast door. 

The meter clicks were steady and sparse: twenty-two counts per minute. 

"Dosimeter reading: zero point zero nine micro-Sieverts per hour," Arthur stated. "Absolute barrier hermeticity verified."

"What did they put in here, Arthur?" Captain Cross asked, his hand resting on the Star-Iron Mark IV battle-rifle slung over his exoskeleton chest plate. "Radioactive waste from the cold war?"

"Waste is a misnomer invented by primitive supply chains," Arthur said coldly. "Vitrification was the process of blending high-level liquid fission products with molten borosilicate glass frit at eleven hundred and fifty degrees Celsius, then pouring the molten mixture into welded stainless steel canisters. Each canister is three meters in height, sixty-one centimeters in diameter, with a wall thickness of nine point five millimeters of 304L stainless steel."

Arthur actuated the electronic hydraulic dogs of the blast door using an archival diagnostic bypass console. The twelve-ton door groaned inward on precision needle-roller hinges.

Beyond the door lay the cavernous glass storage vault. Rows of vertical storage sleeves descended into the concrete floor, cooled by a passive natural convection air-cooling ventilation shaft that drew cool subterranean air from the deep aquifer and vented it upward through HEPA filtration banks.

Arthur walked to the edge of the mezzanine, leaning his upper body over the brass-railed observation platform.

"The radioisotopes encased in this borosilicate matrix—specifically Cesium-137 and Strontium-90—have decayed through more than eight half-lives over the past two hundred and fifty years," Arthur calculated mentally, his pencil scratching the numbers onto the ledger grid with blinding speed. "Cesium-137 half-life: thirty point zero five years. Decay factor: $(0.5)^{8.3} \approx 0.0031$. The original high-intensity thermal heat output of several kilowatts per canister has dropped to less than nine watts. The vitrified matrix itself—consisting of sixty percent silicon dioxide, fifteen percent boron trioxide, twelve percent sodium oxide, and trace lithium and aluminum oxides—is now a chemically inert, ultra-dense vitreous ceramic."

Eleanor walked alongside the sleeve caps, her hand hovering inches above the steel plugs. A faint, tranquil violet luminescence shimmered across her skin, reflecting the crystalline purity of her Sovereign resonance.

"The glass inside these tubes has crystallized, Arthur," Eleanor observed softly. "The radiation didn't shatter the glass. Over two and a half centuries, the slow decay heat annealed it. The boron atoms in the glass lattice have aligned into perfect micro-crystalline clusters. It feels... incredibly dense. Almost impenetrable."

"Boron-10 has a thermal neutron capture cross-section of three thousand eight hundred and thirty-seven barns," Arthur noted, his eyes scanning Eleanor’s readings. "When fused into a high-silica glass network and annealed over two centuries, this material represents the single most effective neutron-absorbing biological shield ever manufactured on this continent. We will salvage two hundred canisters. They will serve as the inner core containment liner for our transcontinental subterranean locomotives and stationary zero-point reactors."

*Day 61 // 11:30:45 AM.*

Passing through the decontamination airlock of Vault Level Three, Arthur led the party into the Special Nuclear Materials Archive—a subterranean repository cut into dry kaolin clay beds beneath the water table.

Here were housed the strategic reserves of non-radioactive refractory metals, neutron moderators, and ultra-high-purity alloys that had been stockpiled during the late twenty-first century to support advanced fusion-fission hybrid reactor development.

Silas Thorne stopped dead in his tracks before Bay Seven. He pulled off his leather work gloves, dropped to his knees, and let out a breathless laugh.

"Arthur... Great Heavens above. Look at these crates."

Stacked in precision rows on treated star-iron pallets were hundreds of wooden crates banded with corrosion-resistant Monel strapping. Silas cut the strapping of the nearest crate with his side-cutters and pried the lid open.

Inside, nestled in vacuum-sealed nitrogen foil pouches and desiccated silica bags, were cylindrical ingots of a silvery-gray, exceptionally lightweight metal.

Arthur stepped forward, took a pair of stainless steel calipers from his breast pocket, and measured the top billet.

"Diameter: one hundred and fifty point zero millimeters. Length: three hundred point zero millimeters," Arthur stated, before pulling a digital electronic balance from his utility pack. He placed the billet on the load cell. "Mass: nine point eight zero kilograms. Calculated density: one point eight five grams per cubic centimeter."

"Nuclear-grade beryllium," Silas Thorne whispered, his fingers trembling as he touched the mirror-polished face of the billet. "Brush Wellman S-65 grade vacuum hot-pressed beryllium. Purity ninety-nine point four percent. Low beryllium-oxide inclusion. Arthur, do you know how hard it was to find even twenty pounds of pure beryllium in the Pacific Northwest? We had to cannibalize aerospace gyroscopes! And here... there must be fifty tons of it!"

"Precisely forty-two point six metric tons across three hundred and twenty pallets," Arthur corrected precisely, checking the archival inventory plaque mounted to the concrete pillar. "Beryllium is an element of unique atomic mechanics. Atomic number four, atomic weight nine point zero one two. It possesses an extremely low thermal neutron absorption cross-section of zero point zero zero nine barns, paired with a massive neutron scattering cross-section of seven barns. Furthermore, under high-energy photon bombardment exceeding one point six six mega-electron-volts, beryllium undergoes the $(n, 2n)$ reaction: a single incident neutron knocks loose two secondary neutrons, functioning as an energetic neutron multiplier."

Arthur looked at Silas with unblinking, analytical intensity.

"When machined into parabolic reflectors and placed around our zero-point flux induction coils, this beryllium will reflect stray telluric harmonics back into the resonance cavity with an efficiency of ninety-nine point eight two percent. It eliminates harmonic leakage, allowing us to increase the power density of the *Prometheus*'s main drive by four hundred percent without risking thermal runaway."

"I'll have the overhead crane hooked up immediately," Silas said, scrambling to his feet. "We're taking every last ounce."

"Every gram is already allocated in the master ledger," Arthur replied. "Move to Bay Nine."

Bay Nine contained an even more vital structural treasure: three thousand seamless extruded tubes arranged in heavy star-iron transport racks. Each tube was twelve meters long, polished to a dull silver luster, with an outer diameter of nine point five millimeters and a wall thickness of zero point five seven millimeters.

Arthur inspected the metallurgical stamp etched into the collar of the lead rack:

`ALLOY: ZIRCALOY-4 (UNS R60804) — NUCLEAR FUEL CLADDING GRADE.`
`COMPOSITION: Zr (BAL), Sn 1.45%, Fe 0.21%, Cr 0.10%, O 0.12%, C < 0.015%, H < 0.0025%.`

"Zircaloy-4," Arthur murmured, his mechanical pencil flying across the page. "Tin-iron-chromium stabilized zirconium alloy. Specifically formulated to eliminate nickel, thereby drastically reducing hydrogen absorption and post-irradiation hydriding embrittlement. Thermal neutron capture cross-section: zero point one eight barns. Tensile strength at four hundred degrees Celsius: three hundred and eighty megapascals. Yield strength: two hundred and forty megapascals."

"This tubing was engineered to withstand seven hundred degrees Fahrenheit in pressurized water at fifteen megapascals for six years without a single pinhole leak," Silas added, his voice reverent. "We can use this to build the primary heat exchangers for the entire Deep South power grid."

"We will use it for both heat exchangers and zero-point flux guide tubes," Arthur said. "Zircaloy's atomic lattice possesses exceptional structural transparency to the five hundred and twenty-eight Hertz telluric pulse. Star-iron provides structural rigidity, but Zircaloy-4 provides quantum flux stability. We will salvage all three thousand tubes."

*Day 61 // 12:45:00 PM.*

The heavy star-iron gantry crane on flatcar FC-10 swung smoothly across the loading dock, guided by the precision hand signals of Master Silas Thorne. High-tensile braided steel cables lowered the final pallet of vacuum-sealed beryllium billets into the cushioned, star-iron-reinforced hold of shielded carriage SC-04. Pneumatic hold-down clamps engaged with twin metallic thuds, locking the cargo against lateral acceleration.

Arthur Vance stood at the edge of the dock, checking the balance of each carriage using an electronic optical level mounted on a tripod. The track deflection under the massive two-hundred-and-seventy-seven-ton load was exactly one point four millimeters—well within the permissible elastic deformation limit of forty-five-kilogram-per-meter rail.

Eleanor walked up beside him, wiping a stray drop of spring rain from her forehead. She looked back across the silent, brutalist expanse of the Savannah River nuclear silos.

"We took what we needed, Arthur," she said. "And the earth here remains undisturbed. No leaks, no poison, no fire."

"The Old Federation left an immense legacy of material science," Arthur replied, closing his calfskin ledger and sliding his mechanical pencil into its holster. "Their flaw was not their metallurgy or their chemical engineering. Their flaw was their inability to manage systemic entropy. They accumulated infinite energy without establishing coherent feedback loops. We do not repeat their structural oversights."

He drew his pocket watch from his waistcoat, pressed the crown, and observed the sweep-second hand tick into place.

*1:00:00 PM.*

*Click.*

The gold casing snapped shut.

"Captain Cross, Silas," Arthur ordered. "Secure the perimeter doors of the Savannah silos. Re-engage the passive dry nitrogen seals on the remaining underground vaults. We depart immediately for the Georgia interior. The Black Belt agricultural basin awaits our audit."

The whistle of the *Prometheus* sounded—a deep, resonant two-tone chime that echoed across the pine forests and the winding waters of the Savannah River, announcing the sovereign advance southward.
