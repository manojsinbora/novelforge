# Chapter 77: The Platte River Outpost

The North Platte River cut through the windswept grasslands of eastern Wyoming like a frozen ribbon of jade.

*Day 22 // 8:30 AM.*

Where the river curved around the sandstone bluffs of an ancient pre-Collapse junction town once known as Casper, the armored train *Prometheus* hissed to a halt upon a newly restored siding. The ambient temperature was eight degrees Fahrenheit, with gusting northwest winds driving dry snow devils across the plains.

The strategic imperative of this geographical threshold was immediately apparent to Arthur Vance.

Standing on the wooden cross-ties of the main line, his brass-bound ledger open and resting securely against his forearm, Arthur surveyed the terrain. To the west, the snow-covered peaks of the Laramie Mountains formed a defensive barrier protecting the mountain passes to the Continental Divide. To the east, the Platte River valley opened into an unbroken highway of flat, gentle grades leading straight toward the Missouri River and the heartland of the continent.

"Location survey: North Platte Milepost Two Hundred and Fourteen," Arthur dictated, his mechanical pencil scratching across the ledger page. "Elevation: five thousand one hundred and twenty feet above sea level. Strategic designation: **Fort Platte Forward Logistics Redoubt**."

Captain Rachel Cross stepped down from the pilot car, her heavy wool trench coat buttoned tight and her binoculars slung across her chest.

"Water and fuel are our primary bottlenecks, Arthur," Cross said, gesturing toward the frozen river two hundred yards away. "The Platte is frozen under forty inches of river ice, and the local homesteaders tell us the surface water is heavily contaminated with alkali salts and acid drainage from abandoned open-pit strip mines north of the bluffs. The *Prometheus* consumes six thousand gallons of treated boiler feedwater for every fifty miles of heavy haulage. If we feed her acidic river slush, boiler scale and foaming will ruin the fire-tubes before we reach Nebraska."

"Surface water is thermodynamically and chemically unsuitable for high-pressure star-iron boilers," Arthur agreed calmly. "Which is why we will tap the subterranean Cloverly sandstone aquifer at a vertical depth of eight hundred and twenty feet."

Arthur turned to Daniel Teton and Master Joseph Kovacs, who had just stepped off the maintenance flatcar.

"Uncouple the **Portable Sonic Resonance Drill Rig**," Arthur instructed. "We will complete artesian wellhead installation before noon."

The drill rig was an extraordinary piece of precision engineering salvaged from Bunker 07 and adapted for rail-bound deployment. Mounted on a heavy forty-foot flatcar, the rig utilized dual eccentric counter-rotating weights driven by a fifty-horsepower electric motor, vibrating a six-inch alloy drill string at a resonant frequency of one hundred and twenty Hertz.

Arthur stepped onto the flatcar to supervise the drill rig's mechanical positioning.

"Sonic drilling mechanics," Arthur explained to the young Shoshone roughnecks securing the hydraulic stabilizing outriggers:

"Unlike conventional rotary drilling that grinds rock through abrasive friction, sonic resonance fluidizes the surrounding sedimentary strata. At one hundred and twenty Hertz, shear wave propagation reduces the shear strength of unconsolidated sandstone and shale to near zero, permitting penetration velocities exceeding two feet per minute with minimal drill string torque."

Arthur engaged the master pneumatic clutch.

*BUZZZZZZZZZZ-THUMMMMMM!*

The six-inch drill string began oscillating at high frequency, a deep, bone-rattling hum that vibrated through the steel flatcar and into the frozen ground. 

With smooth, effortless power, the drill bit sank into the earth. It sheared through four feet of surface frost in ninety seconds, passed through ninety feet of alluvial gravel, and plunged into the underlying Cretaceous shale formations. 

Every thirty feet, Confederation roughnecks clamped a new section of flush-jointed casing onto the string, tightening the joints with hydraulic tongs.

At 10:45 AM, at a depth of exactly eight hundred and fourteen feet, the vibration frequency shifted with a sharp acoustic ring.

*GUSH-HISS-ROOOOAR!*

A subterranean reservoir of pressurized artesian water struck the drill bit. 

A magnificent geyser of crystal-clear, fifty-five-degree water erupted from the casing head, surging thirty feet into the cold winter air before splashing across the frozen gravel. The water was completely free of alkali salts, sulfur runoff, or heavy metal contamination—filtered through eight hundred feet of pristine prehistoric quartz sandstone.

Arthur knelt at the casing head, catching a sample of the water in a borosilicate test tube. He added three drops of phenolphthalein indicator and tested the conductivity with a digital bridge:

"Total dissolved solids: forty-two parts per million," Arthur recorded in his ledger with a small nod of satisfaction. "pH level: seven point two. Total hardness: zero point eight grains per gallon. Absolute boiler-feed grade. Hydrodynamic flow rate calculation:"

$$\text{Darcy's Law: } Q = \frac{-k \cdot A}{\mu} \cdot \frac{dP}{dx}$$

$$Q \approx 365.4 \text{ Gallons Per Minute}$$

"Three hundred and sixty-five gallons per minute of self-discharging artesian flow," Arthur announced. "Sufficient to supply the *Prometheus*, thirty local homesteads, and a permanent steam-cleaning shed simultaneously."

Naomi Tsosie’s drilling crew immediately clamped a forged star-iron wellhead Christmas tree over the casing, routing the pristine artesian water through an insulated overhead crane arm directly into the locomotive’s tender cistern and a newly erected forty-thousand-gallon wooden water tank.

To guarantee zero bacterial contamination and prevent seasonal stagnation, Arthur oversaw the installation of an inline electrolytic chlorination cell powered by the train's auxiliary turbine generator. Operating at twelve volts direct current, the cell generated trace amounts of sodium hypochlorite from mineral brine, maintaining an active chlorine residual of zero point five milligrams per liter.

Within two hours, the desolate siding had been transformed into a bustling hive of civilization.

Under the rhythmic direction of Sergeant Major Hank Bradley, fifty soldiers of Company B and thirty paroled raiders from Station Forty-Two cleared two miles of overgrown sidings, erected a heated timber telegraph shed, and established fortified sandbag revetments armed with water-cooled machine guns.

By 11:30 AM, Arthur ascended the newly erected thirty-foot star-iron telegraph mast behind the depot.

Working with Sergeant Bradley, Arthur spliced the heavy shielded telegraph cable directly into the long-range transmitter. He clicked the telegraph key three times:

*DIT-DAH-DIT... DIT-DAH-DIT...*

Across four hundred miles of mountain ranges, the wireless repeating station atop Mount Sentinel acknowledged the link in crisp, rapid Morse code:

**[MT SENTINEL // OBSERVATORY TO FORT PLATTE]**  
**[SIGNAL 5/5. TELEGRAPH CARRIER WAVE SYNCHRONIZED AT 528 HZ. FORT PLATTE LOGISTICS LINK ESTABLISHED.]**

By midday, news of the miraculous artesian geyser, the telegraph link, and the peaceful, orderly arrival of the great armored train had spread like wildfire across the Platte River basin.

Dozens of local settlers—fur trappers, cattle ranchers in sheepskin coats, and wandering trade caravans—began arriving at Fort Platte on horse-drawn sleighs and tracked utility wagons. They brought bundles of dried prairie venison, tanned wolf pelts, and sacks of pinto beans, eager to trade for salt, kerosene, and medical supplies.

Eleanor Vance stood on the open wooden veranda of the newly built trading post, distributing warm wool blankets and sealed tins of antibiotics to women and children whose lips were blue with winter cold. Her Mid-Tier Sovereign starlight warmed the entire clearing, turning the sub-zero wind into a mild, soothing breeze.

An elderly, leather-skinned frontier scout named Jedediah Hawke, wearing a beaver-fur cap and carrying an antique lever-action Winchester rifle, walked up to Arthur Vance, staring in awe at the smoking *Prometheus* and the gleaming artesian fountain.

"I haven't seen a high-pressure steam train run this valley since I was a boy of ten, mister," Hawke rasped, squinting at Arthur’s clean charcoal waistcoat and pocket watch. "You folks really came all the way from the mountains?"

"We originated from Bunker Seven and the Great Basin," Arthur replied evenly, wiping a speck of limestone dust from his mechanical pencil. "We have established standard-gauge star-iron rail connectivity across the Continental Divide. Fort Platte is now officially designated as an accredited logistics depot of the Western Confederation."

Hawke took a sip of hot chicory coffee from a tin cup, his gaze turning grim as he pointed eastward toward the open plains.

"Then you folks better keep your powder dry and your engines stoked, sir," Hawke said, lowering his voice. "Because two hundred miles down the Platte, near the forks of the river... you're heading straight into the domain of **Baron Malakor**."

Arthur clicked his pencil and opened a clean page in his ledger. "Provide a factual breakdown of Baron Malakor and his operational capabilities."

"Malakor calls his outfit the **Iron Barony of the Rust Belt**," Hawke explained, leaning against the timber railing. "He's a warlord who took over the ruined steel mills and rail yards between Omaha and Chicago twenty years ago. He runs three thousand heavily armed scavengers, fifty armored steam-crawlers, and a fleet of ironclad riverboats that patrol the Missouri River."

"What is his economic and logistical doctrine?" Arthur inquired deadpan.

"Extortion and scrap-metal plunder," Hawke spat. "He enforces what he calls the *Iron Tithe*. Every farming commune, every cattle ranch within three hundred miles has to pay half their grain and half their scrap iron every autumn, or Malakor sends his 'Reapers' to burn their cabins to the ground and chain the young men to his rolling mills."

Hawke paused, looking Arthur dead in the eye.

"And there's worse, Mr. Vance. Word came down from the river scouts three days ago. Malakor has been stripping every pre-Collapse bridge and factory in Nebraska to build something he calls the **Colossus of Gary**. A two-hundred-ton walking siege dreadnought armed with naval mortars and flail cutters. He intends to march it west along the Platte tracks to seize the mountain coal mines and take your locomotives."

Arthur Vance listened to the report without a single change in facial expression. He noted the details down in precise, tidy columns:

*Target: Baron Malakor // Iron Barony of the Rust Belt.*  
*Manpower: ~3,000 hostile combatants.*  
*Prime Movers: Armored steam crawlers, improvised ironclad gunboats.*  
*Primary Kinetic Asset: 'Colossus of Gary' (200-ton walking chassis; 152mm mortars).*  
*Tactical Objective: Extortion of Western Confederation fuel, star-iron, and locomotives.*

Arthur closed the ledger and clicked the pencil shut.

He pulled his gold pocket watch, clicked the lid open, and checked the time.

*1:15 PM.*

"A two-hundred-ton walking chassis," Arthur said quietly.

Captain Cross stepped up beside him, her brow furrowed with tactical concern. "Arthur, a two-hundred-ton machine armed with siege mortars could derail the *Prometheus* from two miles away. How do we counter an asset of that scale?"

"A walking chassis of two hundred tons is a mechanical monstrosity of catastrophic inefficiency," Arthur replied, his voice utterly devoid of fear. "The ground contact pressure exerted by a two-hundred-ton bipedal or quadrupedal frame in alluvial river mud exceeds eighty pounds per square inch. Its joint gimbals will suffer enormous bending moments, and its boiler will require fifty tons of water per day merely to maintain hydraulic pressure. In short: it is an engineering absurdity that violates every principle of structural mechanics."

Arthur turned to Hawke and the gathered settlers.

"We offer the Platte Valley settlements an official mutual treaty: **The Platte Valley Agricultural Compact**," Arthur announced. "The Confederation guarantees you free artesian water, five hundred gallons of refined kerosene per month from Wasatch, medical supplies, and collective defense against Baron Malakor. In return, your communes will provide right-of-way track maintenance, wood ties, and advance scouting along the river."

Hawke and the elders stared at each other, stunned by the terms.

"You're not demanding a tithe?" Hawke asked in shock. "No grain taxes?"

"Taxes levied through coercive extortion are economically parasitic," Arthur replied. "Trade based on mutual thermodynamic and logistical surplus creates lasting stability. We build, Mr. Hawke. We do not plunder."

Hawke took off his fur hat and extended a weathered, calloused hand. "By God, Mr. Vance... you've got yourself a deal. The Platte valley is with the Confederation."

Arthur shook the scout's hand, then turned toward the maintenance flatcar, where Master Kovacs was unloading crates of fresh star-iron plate.

"We do not halt our advance," Arthur declared calmly. "We fortify Fort Platte, complete the water cranes, and push our tracklaying crews eastward toward the Black Hills junction. When Baron Malakor’s machine arrives, we will dismantle it according to the laws of physics."
