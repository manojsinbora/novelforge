# Chapter 33: The Gates of Vulcan Junction

The smell of coking coal, sulfur dioxide, and superheated refractory brick hung over the eastern horizon like a permanent volcanic shroud.

*Day 5 // 12:48 PM.*

Depot Shunter Number 7 crested the final gravel cut at Milepost 44, its iron wheels decelerating smoothly as Arthur Vance brought the consist onto the western classification ladder of **Vulcan Junction**.

Ahead of them, sprawling across three square miles of the river flats where the Blackwood River converged with the Great Northern Freight Corridor, stood a monumental cathedral of American heavy industry: **The Cascade Steel Works**.

Constructed in 1948 and modernized through three successive decades of industrial expansion, the facility was a staggering labyrinth of heavy metallurgy. 

Soaring one hundred and twenty feet into the hazy sky stood four massive cylindrical **Blast Furnaces**, each wrapped in a dense web of skip hoists, downcomer pipes, and towering refractory-brick Cowper hot-blast stoves. Beyond the furnaces lay the half-mile-long open-hearth pouring sheds, the blooming mill, the continuous rail-rolling trains, and a coking battery of sixty-four ovens that stretched into the distance like a fortress wall.

Yet the great mill was not operating in peace.

From the roof of the central coking tower, black coal smoke billowed in violent, erratic bursts. Across the twenty parallel classification tracks of the freight yard, over fifty railroad hopper cars and boxcars had been overturned and dragged into a fortified perimeter.

And circling that perimeter, like iron hyenas around a wounded mammoth, were the mechanized war engines of Warlord Raymond Kane’s **Cinder Vanguard**.

Arthur stopped the locomotive beneath the steel umbrella of an abandoned railcar repair shed, three hundred yards west of the main yard throat.

He climbed to the roof of Car 42, unslung his brass-barrel terrestrial telescope, and focused the optical glass on Blast Furnace Number Four.

Beside him, Captain Rachel Cross and Sergeant Marcus Cole lowered themselves against the wooden catwalk, their binoculars sweeping the industrial compound.

"Count the enemy vehicles, Sergeant," Cross ordered quietly.

Cole adjusted the diopter on his military optics, his jaw tightening.

"Three heavy steam-dredgers," Cole reported with grim precision. "Thirty-ton Marion rail-shovels, converted into armored assault tortoises with half-inch boiler plate welded over the boilers and tracks. The center dredger has a dual forty-millimeter Bofors autocannon mounted on the cab roof. The two flanking units have heavy steam-powered ramming rams and high-pressure scalding lances."

"Infantry strength?" Cross asked.

"At least sixty armed combatants," Cole replied. "Equipped with hunting rifles, trench shotguns, and cutting torches. They’ve established interlocking sandbag bunkers around the primary coal tipple and the cast house access ramps."

Arthur shifted his telescope toward the circular base of **Blast Furnace Number Four**.

The sight that met his eyes was both heroic and horrifying.

The cast house—a massive structural steel building with open corrugated iron sides where molten pig iron was tapped from the furnace hearth—was fortified with stacked rail ties, heavy ingot molds, and steel scrap plates.

Inside, silhouetted against the blinding, orange-white glow of the furnace tuyeres, were seventy surviving members of the **Cascade Steelworkers Guild**.

They were wearing scorched split-cowhide foundry aprons, green-tinted furnace goggles, and asbestos leggings. At their head stood a barrel-chested, white-bearded man in his late fifties holding an eight-foot steel tapping rod like a war spear: **Master Foundryman Joseph Kovacs**.

The workers were desperately feeding oak timbers, scrap furniture, and bags of bituminous coal by hand into the furnace blowpipes to maintain combustion.

Arthur lowered his telescope, pulled out his pocket thermometer and his field ledger, and quickly calculated the thermal decay rate:

```
BLAST FURNACE NO. 4 // THERMAL DECAY AUDIT:
- Hearth Capacity: 400 metric tons of molten pig iron.
- Current Hearth Temperature: 1,020°C (1,868°F).
- Critical Solidification Threshold: 800°C (1,472°F).
- Thermal Loss Rate: 1.8°C per minute due to severed cooling and blast gas.
- Time Remaining Before Irreversible Hearth Freezing: 2 hours, 12 minutes.
```

"If that furnace hearth drops below eight hundred degrees," Arthur explained calmly to Captain Cross, "four hundred tons of molten iron and slag will cool into a solid, monolithic block of solid pig iron sixty feet across."

"What does that mean for the mill?" Cross asked.

"It means the permanent, catastrophic death of the facility," Arthur answered. "You cannot re-melt four hundred tons of solid iron inside an enclosed refractory brick hearth. To remove it would require four thousand pounds of dynamite, which would shatter the furnace lining, the water cooling jackets, and the structural bosh columns. The Cascade Steel Works would be rendered dead scrap forever."

Eleanor knelt on the roof of the boxcar, her bare fingers resting against the galvanized steel flashing. Her eyes glowed with brilliant celestial gold, tracking the subterranean currents pulsing beneath the classification yard.

"Arthur," Eleanor whispered, her voice vibrating with urgency. "The telephone lines... look at the wooden pole beside the repair shed."

Arthur looked down.

Running from the shed to the cast house of Blast Furnace 4 was an intact, two-wire **Sound-Powered Industrial Intercom Line**—a vintage emergency communication system installed in the 1950s that required zero external electrical power, operating entirely on acoustic diaphragm induction.

Arthur descended the ladder with measured, agile steps.

Inside the repair shed, an antique cast-aluminum Western Electric wall telephone hung beneath an emergency lighting battery. Arthur lifted the heavy phenolic handset, pressed the generator crank, and turned the brass handle three times:

*RING-RING... RING.*

For eight seconds, there was only the hum of acoustic wire. Then the receiver clicked open, and a harsh, gasping voice filled with smoke and exhaustion boomed through the earpiece:

*"WHO IS CALLING THE CAST HOUSE?! IF THIS IS KANE, GO TO HELL! WE WILL DUMP THE SLAG ON YOUR HEADS BEFORE WE SURRENDER THE FURNACE!"*

Arthur pressed the push-to-talk bar, his cadence level, polite, and unhurried:

"Master Foundryman Joseph Kovacs. This is Arthur Vance, Chief Archivist and Mechanical Engineer of the Blackwood Confederation, speaking from the western rail classification shed."

A stunned, coughing silence fell over the wire.

*"...Blackwood? The library archivist from Oakhaven? You're on the other side of the collapsed bridge!"*

"The bridge at Broken Creek has been repaired with continuous welded rail," Arthur informed him accurately. "We have arrived with Depot Shunter Number Seven, Company B of the 116th Engineer Battalion, and twenty metric tons of hard red winter wheat flour to replenish your guild’s caloric deficit."

Kovacs let out a ragged, disbelieving sob over the line. 

*"Twenty tons of flour... God Almighty. Vance, we’ve been eating roasted industrial soy meal for four days. But you can't reach the cast house! Raymond Kane has three thirty-ton armored steam-dredgers and sixty cutthroats zeroed in on our ramps! His forty-millimeter autocannon will shred your locomotive into kindling!"*

"A forty-millimeter Bofors autocannon possesses high kinetic velocity, but relies on line-of-sight targeting and hydraulic traversing gears," Arthur replied with clinical calm. "Kovacs, answer three technical questions:
First: Is the underground central steam distribution main from the boiler house charged?
Second: Are the pneumatic track-switching air reservoirs in the interlocking tower pressurized?
Third: What is the current hydrostatic pressure in your furnace cooling jacket?"

Kovacs’s voice sharpened instantly, the veteran foundry engineer recognizing a fellow master of the machine:

*"One: The central steam main is hot at two hundred and fifty PSI saturated steam. We’ve been using it to blow the tuyeres.
Two: The interlocking tower reservoirs hold ninety PSI, but Kane shot the tower operator yesterday. The main switches are locked in reverse.
Three: Our cooling jacket water is at twelve PSI and falling. The pumps are starving!"*

"That is all the data required," Arthur said. "Kovacs. Instruct your seventy workers to don their full asbestos fire hoods, keep the hearth combustion wood-fed, and prepare the iron ladles. We will eliminate Kane’s vanguard and restore your cooling water in precisely forty-two minutes."

*"Vance, wait—how the hell are you going to take on three armored steam dredgers with a single locomotive?!"*

Arthur Vance pulled his brass pocket watch from his waistcoat, checked the time, and clicked it shut with a crisp, authoritative snap.

*1:18 PM.*

"With fluid dynamics, high-pressure saturated steam, and twenty-four hundred pounds of pneumatic impact force," Arthur Vance replied smoothly into the heavy telephone receiver.

He hung up the phenolic handset, turned to Captain Cross, and unrolled the sprawling, grease-stained yard schematics of Vulcan Junction across the technical truck’s hood.

"Captain Cross. Sergeant Cole. Eleanor. Gather round," Arthur commanded, adjusting his spectacles.

The four gathered close over the blueprint, their breath pluming in the cool mountain draft beneath the corrugated iron roof of the repair shed.

"Observe the yard geometry," Arthur began, tracing the layout with the brass ferrule of his mechanical pencil. 

"Vulcan Junction is organized as a classic gravity classification yard. The primary arrival ladder consists of twenty parallel tracks fanning out from the main throat at Interlocking Tower B. Kane has positioned his three thirty-ton steam-dredgers in a linear blocking echelon across Tracks Four, Five, and Six, directly covering the cast house approach ramps."

"A frontal assault with Depot Shunter Number Seven would be tactical suicide," Captain Cross observed grimly, studying the fire zones. "That center dredger’s forty-millimeter Bofors autocannon fires two-pound high-explosive armor-piercing rounds at two hundred and forty rounds per minute. It would punch through our locomotive’s quarter-inch cab steel like paper."

"A direct kinetic duel between a shunter locomotive and an autocannon is unnecessary," Arthur countered. "We will defeat Kane through mechanical infrastructure control."

He pointed to Interlocking Tower B—a three-story brick-and-concrete switch tower positioned fifty yards east of the repair shed, overlooking the entire yard throat.

"Interlocking Tower B controls the electro-pneumatic switch machines for all twenty tracks. Kovacs confirmed that the tower’s primary air receivers remain charged at ninety PSI. If we can secure that tower, we can manually manipulate the track points beneath Kane’s vehicles."

"Divide and conquer," Sergeant Cole grinned, a grim predator's smile touching his weathered face. "Split the three dredgers onto separate spurs."

"Precisely," Arthur affirmed. "Furthermore, running three feet beneath Tracks Four, Five, and Six is the mill’s central **twelve-inch saturated steam distribution main**. Operating at two hundred and fifty PSI, the steam inside that cast-iron pipe is four hundred and six degrees Fahrenheit. There are three above-ground isolation blowoff valves located in the maintenance pits directly beneath Kane’s dredgers."

Arthur looked at Eleanor.

"Eleanor. Your role will be critical," Arthur said softly. "The switch machines in Tower B are operated by electrical solenoids whose control wiring was partially severed by gunfire. You will use your Starlight Sovereign resonance to bridge the broken relays, allowing us to throw the electro-pneumatic switch valves from the tower console."

Eleanor nodded firmly, her green eyes blazing with clear resolve. "I understand, Artie. I can feel the copper wiring running under the tracks. I'll hold the relays open."

"Phase One of the operation," Arthur briefed, his tone snapping with military precision:

"1. Diversion: Captain Cross and Sergeant Cole will maneuver the armored Humvee along the northern perimeter levee, engaging Kane’s infantry bunker at the coal tipple with controlled bursts of M2 fifty-caliber fire. This will draw the attention of the center dredger and force its forty-millimeter turret to traverse ninety degrees away from the yard throat.
2. Infiltration: Under cover of that engagement, Eleanor and I will advance through the subterranean drainage trench beneath Track Two, breach Interlocking Tower B, and secure the pneumatic switch console.
3. Decoupling: Once the switches are under our control, Davis will advance Depot Shunter Number Seven at fifteen miles per hour along Track Eight, pushing our armored flatbed into flanking position while I initiate high-pressure steam depressurization beneath Kane’s tracks."

Arthur glanced at the three soldiers, then at Eleanor.

"We have one hour and forty-four minutes before the molten pig iron in Blast Furnace Number Four cools past the point of recovery," Arthur Vance concluded, checking his brass watch one final time.

*1:26 PM.*

"Assemble your equipment. Double-check your ear protection and safety goggles. It is time to dismantle an iron warlord."
