# Chapter 118: The Newark Salt Meadows

The dawn over the Newark Basin did not break in gold or crimson, but in a dull, leaden shroud of sulfuric vapor and freezing coastal mist. At twenty-eight degrees Fahrenheit, the moisture blowing inland from Newark Bay condensed into brittle rime frost upon the steel handrails and armor plating of the locomotive *Prometheus*. 

*Day 45 // 6:30:00 AM.*

He closed the watchcase and returned it to the fourth pocket of his charcoal wool waistcoat. Reaching into the inner breast pocket of his tailored greatcoat, he withdrew a six-inch German-silver drafting scale and a mechanical pencil loaded with 0.5-millimeter 4H graphite.

"Just pulled the sample from Milepost 84.7, three hundred yards past the old Passaic River junction. It’s bad, Arthur. Worse than the Ohio mudflats, worse than the swamp at Gary."

Arthur took the core-barrel, uncapped the threaded brass collar, and extracted the sediment cylinder onto a zinc inspection tray. He brought the edge of his mechanical pencil against the sample, slicing cleanly into the strata.

"Post-glacial Lake Hackensack varved clay," Arthur stated, his voice flat, detached, and devoid of theatrical dread. Structural mild steel would corrode at a rate of one point eight millimeters per year. "What’s the bedrock depth?" Kovacs asked, leaning over the handrail and spitting a dark flake of tobacco into the track bed.

Arthur turned toward the open salon door behind them. "Eleanor."

In the dull, gloomy dawn of the salt marsh, her violet eyes held an inner, coherent luminescence—a soft, pearl-colored clarity that hummed at the exact pitch of an untuned tuning fork.

"The polychlorinated biphenyls and refractory polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons possess carbon-chlorine bond energies ranging from three hundred and twenty-seven to four hundred kilojoules per mole. Eleanor nodded, a calm, unwavering focus settling across her delicate features. "The resonance is ready, Arthur. Whenever you place the first conduit."
