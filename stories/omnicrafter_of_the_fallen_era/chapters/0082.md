# Chapter 82: The Dead City of Omaha

The ruined skyline of pre-Collapse Omaha rose above the frozen western bluffs of the Missouri River like a graveyard of petrified giants.

*Day 27 // 7:20 AM.*

Centuries of relentless prairie blizzards, thermal cycling, and unmaintained drainage systems had reduced the ancient metropolis to a labyrinth of crumbling concrete canyons. Towering corporate skyscrapers stood as hollow, windowless skeletons encrusted with rime frost; collapsed elevated expressways lay in fractured heaps across snow-choked boulevards; and frozen weeping icicles fifty feet in length hung from shattered glass curtain walls like the fangs of ancient predators.

Yet beneath this silent urban necropolis lay one of the most critical logistical objectives of the eastern expedition:

**The Omaha Telecommunications Central Office & Midwestern Agricultural Seed Vault.**

Arthur Vance stepped down from the tracked personnel carrier onto the frozen asphalt of Dodge Street, his heavy charcoal wool greatcoat buttoned tight against the sub-zero chill. Beside him walked Eleanor Vance, her Mid-Tier Sovereign starlight casting a warm golden perimeter that insulated them from the minus fifteen-degree morning air, while Captain Rachel Cross and eight veteran scouts of Company B provided tight tactical coverage, their carbines scanning the dark hollows of shattered office lobbies.

"Location coordinates confirmed," Arthur announced, checking his brass navigational sextant against the morning sun and referencing his leather ledger. "Forty-one degrees, fifteen minutes, thirty seconds north; ninety-five degrees, fifty-six minutes west. The subterranean facility was engineered four decades before the Great Collapse as a hardened Category-Five continuity-of-government nexus, buried eighty feet beneath the foundations of the Central Telephone Exchange."

Captain Cross looked up at the looming fifty-story granite monolith before them, its entrance sealed behind a collapsed ten-ton reinforced concrete lintel.

"The main lobby is completely crushed, Arthur," Cross said, shining her tactical lantern into the rubble. "How do we breach eighty feet of collapsed structural debris without causing a secondary progressive collapse?"

"We do not enter through the collapsed civilian lobby," Arthur replied calmly. "Pre-Collapse telecommunications infrastructure required dedicated pressurized nitrogen cable utility vaults to prevent moisture ingress into high-density fiber-optic conduits. The auxiliary subterranean maintenance shaft is located beneath the transformer substation two hundred yards to the north."

Arthur led the team across the snow-drifted plaza to an unassuming brick utility enclosure encrusted with rusted ivy. 

Within the enclosure stood a circular, four-foot-diameter cast star-iron manhole cover, marked with faded pre-Collapse heraldry:

**DEPARTMENT OF CIVIL CONTINUITY // FIBER-OPTIC TRUNK // RESTRICTED ACCESS**

Arthur knelt beside the star-iron cover, pulling an ultrasonic thickness gauge and a dental inspection mirror from his vest.

"Cover thickness: two point five inches of high-strength nodular star-iron," Arthur recorded in his ledger, clicking his mechanical pencil. "Locking mechanism: three internal radial dog-bolts engaged by an electromagnetic solenoid. The solenoid lost power when the city grid collapsed, causing the spring-loaded fail-safe to default to the locked position."

"Can we cut the dogs with an oxy-acetylene torch?" Cross asked.

"Cutting nodular star-iron with oxy-acetylene requires temperatures exceeding three thousand degrees, which would vaporize the delicate elastomeric hermetic seals and melt the fiber-optic terminal cables directly below," Arthur replied. "Instead, we will exploit thermal contraction and hydraulic shearing."

Arthur reached into his tool harness and withdrew a pressurized canister of liquid nitrogen freezing spray, fitted with a narrow stainless-steel needle nozzle:

"Liquid nitrogen boils at minus three hundred and twenty degrees Fahrenheit," Arthur explained. "By applying concentrated cryogenic refrigerant directly to the perimeter dog-bolt recesses, the cast star-iron undergoes rapid thermal contraction: $\Delta L = \alpha \cdot L \cdot \Delta T$. The resulting radial shrinkage will pull the locking dogs back from their retaining collars by approximately zero point zero eight inches."

Arthur sprayed the freezing liquid along the circular seam.

*FSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSH!*

Dense white clouds of freezing vapor billowed into the freezing air as frost instantly rimed the iron lid. A rapid series of crisp metallic pings sounded from within the casting:

*PING! PING! TING!*

Arthur engaged the **Chimera Frame Mark V** on his right arm. He locked the vanadium-tipped pry adapter beneath the recessed lifting lip and engaged the Closed-Loop Accumulator at two thousand PSI:

*CLACK-HISSSSS!*

With effortless hydraulic smoothness, the two-hundred-pound star-iron manhole cover lifted from its frame, revealing a vertical access shaft plunging eighty feet into the subterranean gloom, equipped with an intact stainless-steel cage ladder.

"Shaft unsealed," Arthur announced, checking his pocket watch.

*7:44 AM.*

"Atmospheric sensors indicate oxygen concentration at twenty point eight percent, carbon monoxide zero, methane zero. The pressurized nitrogen preservation system has purged moisture and preserved the vault atmosphere. Descend in tactical order."

Down through the vertical steel shaft they climbed, descending past layers of fractured city pavement, buried subway utility corridors, and deep subterranean bedrock.

At eighty feet, the ladder terminated on a reinforced concrete landing before a colossal six-inch-thick vault door of solid brushed nickel-chromium alloy, guarded by an active, humming electronic console.

A bank of red phosphor diodes glowed in the darkness, illuminated by an intact First Dynasty geothermal thermopile system that had drawn power from deep crustal heat for three centuries.

Across the vault door, an amber CRT monitor flickered to life, displaying a warning message in glowing green phosphor text:

**[FACILITY 18-B // CONTINENTAL COMMUNICATIONS & AGRONOMIC HERITAGE]**  
**[SECURITY STATUS: LOCKDOWN LEVEL FOUR]**  
**[THERMITE PURGE CHARGES ARMED // UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY WILL TRIGGER VOLUMETRIC THERMAL INCINERATION]**

"Thermite purge charges," Captain Cross whispered, her breath catching. "The ancients rigged the entire vault to incinerate if anyone forced the door."

"A standard First Dynasty archival denial protocol," Arthur said calmly, stepping directly in front of the optical sensor aperture. "The system relies on an acoustic and optical dual-channel biometric challenge. The optical sensor is a monochromatic charge-coupled camera; the acoustic sensor is a piezoelectric microphone tuned to detect the fundamental resonant frequency of an authorized Sovereign Vessel."

Arthur looked at Eleanor.

"Eleanor," Arthur said softly. "The system is listening for the First Dynasty harmonic standard. Five hundred and twenty-eight Hertz."

Eleanor nodded with serene confidence.

She stepped up to the audio receiver grille, closed her eyes, and let out a gentle, melodic breath. From her lips and her radiant core resonated a single, pure, perfectly stabilized tone of 528.00 Hz starlight.

The acoustic vibration washed over the piezoelectric sensor like a warm summer breeze.

Simultaneously, the optical sensor caught the golden luminescence radiating from her violet eyes.

Inside the vault door, the heavy relays clicked in a rapid, synchronized sequence of mechanical harmony:

*CLACK-CLACK-CLACK-WHIRRRRRR!*

The green phosphor monitor cleared its warning and flashed a new status line:

**[BIOMETRIC HARMONIC VERIFIED // SOVEREIGN ARCHIVIST DETECTED]**  
**[THERMITE PURGE DISARMED // DISENGAGING HYDRAULIC LOCKING BOLTS]**

With a deep, echoing sigh of pressurized nitrogen gas, the massive six-inch vault doors swung open on balanced hydraulic pivots, revealing the cavernous treasure vault within.

Arthur, Eleanor, and Cross stepped across the threshold, their flashlights sweeping a vast, dust-free subterranean chamber that stretched two hundred feet in length.

The discoveries within were breathtaking:

Along the northern wall stood row upon row of hermetically sealed cryogenic storage cabinets, maintained at minus twenty degrees Fahrenheit by liquid nitrogen cooling loops. Inside each cabinet rested hundreds of sealed titanium canisters, labeled with laser-etched barcodes and precise agronomic classifications:

"The Midwestern Agricultural Seed Vault," Arthur announced, his voice carrying a rare, profound reverie as he examined the canisters. "Forty thousand canisters containing pure, non-hybridized, disease-resistant heritage crop strains: heirloom Dent corn, hard red winter wheat, high-protein soybeans, sweet sorghum, and nitrogen-fixing crimson clover. The genetic baseline of the entire American agricultural breadbasket, completely preserved from contamination."

Eleanor touched a frost-covered titanium cylinder, tears of quiet joy glistening in her eyes. She released a soft pulse of Sovereign starlight, checking the biological vitality of the frozen seeds:

"Their cellular membranes are completely intact," Eleanor whispered softly. "Zero crystallization damage. With these seeds, Arthur... we can re-plant the entire Missouri and Mississippi valleys. The farming communes will never have to face winter starvation again."

"A permanent solution to regional caloric deficits," Arthur agreed, logging the inventory in his ledger. "Forty thousand units of elite seed stock represents a potential caloric yield exceeding twelve trillion kilocalories over three planting seasons."

Arthur moved to the central optical catalog desk, opening a sealed aluminum drawer containing dozens of five-inch optical glass storage discs.

He inserted the master disc into a portable laser optical reader connected to his field terminal:

"Data extraction: Midwestern Geological and Infrastructure Survey," Arthur dictated, watching high-density vector schematics populate the screen. "Civil blueprints: full right-of-way alignment for the Chicago and North Western mainline from Omaha to Chicago. Municipal utilities: deep artesian pumping stations in Des Moines, Cedar Rapids, and the Quad Cities. And most significantly..."

Arthur paused, his mechanical pencil hovering over the page.

On the screen, a high-priority First Dynasty subterranean energy schematic flashed with pulsating amber vectors:

**[REGIONAL ANCHOR NODE 06 // IOWA CENTRAL HORTICULTURAL CORE]**  
**[LOCATION: 18 MILES WEST OF DES MOINES // SUBSURFACE FACILITY 09]**  
**[FUNCTION: BIO-RESONANT SOIL STABILIZATION & MESOCLIMATE HARMONIZATION]**  
**[CURRENT STATUS: DORMANT // ZERO-POINT HARMONIC DRIFT DETECTED]**

"Anchor Node Six," Arthur announced, looking up at Eleanor with intense, calculating focus. "Located directly along our rail advance, eighteen miles west of Des Moines. A specialized bio-resonant planetary pylon engineered specifically to harmonize the soil microbial biome, accelerate photosynthesis, and regulate seasonal precipitation across the entire Midwestern corn belt."

"It's dormant," Eleanor observed, her fingers tracing the holographic map. "If we reach Des Moines and recalibrate Node Six to five hundred and twenty-eight Hertz, we won't just feed the Confederation—we will awaken the soil of the entire Midwest."

Arthur turned toward the southern gallery of the vault, where the primary telecommunications processing cores stood.

Here sat the greatest strategic communication prize of all:

A massive, armored junction terminal connecting directly to the **First Dynasty Armored Transcontinental Coaxial Cable**.

Arthur knelt before the terminal, stripping the protective lead sheath from the star-iron busbars with his insulated wire strippers. He connected a portable multi-meter and an inductive pulse tester to the core conductor:

"Coaxial cable electrical continuity test," Arthur said, checking the galvanometer dial.

The brass needle swung sharply to the right and locked in place:

"Continuity confirmed!" Arthur announced with quiet satisfaction. "The cable is intact, buried twelve feet beneath the continental railway roadbed. It runs unbroken from this vault across Iowa, Illinois, and Indiana, linking Omaha directly to Chicago and the Eastern Seaboard!"

Arthur immediately pulled the portable telegraph repeater set from Cross’s tactical pack, splicing the high-altitude radio-telegraph link from the *Prometheus* directly into the transcontinental cable:

*CLICK-CLACK! CLICK-CLACK!*

Within sixty seconds, the telegraph sounder on the desk began chattering with blinding, crystalline speed:

**[OAKHAVEN GRAND LIBRARY // MAYOR SILAS VANCE TO OMAHA ANNEX]**  
**[SIGNAL RECEIVED THROUGH TRANSCONTINENTAL COAXIAL TRUNK! SIGNAL INTEGRITY: 100%! OAKHAVEN ARCHIVE NOW LINKED DIRECTLY TO THE HEART OF THE MIDWEST!]**

Arthur Vance tore off the paper telegraph slip, folded it with surgical precision, and tucked it into his waistcoat pocket.

Just then, Sergeant Major Bradley's voice crackled urgently over the tactical radio channel:

*‘Captain Cross! Arthur! We have visual contact from the river bluffs! Baron Malakor’s vanguard has crossed the Nishnabotna River... The Colossus of Gary is on the march! It’s fifteen miles east of Council Bluffs and closing fast!’*

Arthur Vance’s face remained an unreadable mask of ice and steel.

He pulled his gold pocket watch, clicked the hunter case open, and checked the time:

*10:15 AM.*

"Total salvage operation duration: two hours and thirty-one minutes. The archives of Omaha are secured. The transcontinental telegraph is alive. The seed stock is ready for loading."

Arthur snapped the pocket watch shut and turned to Captain Cross.

"Captain Cross, have Company B haul the first ten thousand seed canisters to the *Prometheus* refrigerated cars immediately. Master Kovacs, ready the rail batteries. We cross back to Council Bluffs to meet Baron Malakor's war machine."
